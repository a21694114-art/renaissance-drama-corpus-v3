NOw ſtand ye forth my merry men all
and hark what I ſhall ſay,
Of an aduenture I ſhall you tell
the which befell this other day.
As I went by the high way
with a ſtout Frier I met.
And a quarter ſtaffe in his hand,
lightly to me he leapt
And ſtill he bad me ſtand.
there were ſtripes two or three,
But I cannot tell who had the worſe
but well I wot the whorſon
Leapt within me
and fro me tooke my purſſe.
Is there any of my merry men all
that to the Frier will goe,
And bring him to me foorth withall
whether he will or no.
Yes Maiſter I make God auow
to that Frier will I goe,
And bring him to you
whither he will or no,
Deus hic, Deus hic, God be heere
is not this a holy word of a Frier,
God ſaue all this company,
But am not I a iolly Frier
For I can ſhoote both farre and neere.
and handly the ſword and buckler
and this quarter ſtaffe alſo
If I meet with a gentle man or yeman,
I am not afraid to looke him vpon
nor boldly with him to carp,
If he ſpeake any words to me,
He ſhall haue ſtripes two or three
that ſhall make his body ſmart.
But maiſters to ſhew you the matter
wherefore and why I am come hither
in faith I will not ſpare,
I am come to ſeeke a good yeoman
in Bernisdale men ſay is his habitation
His name is Robin hood
And if he be better man then I
his ſeruant will I be and ſerue him truly
But if that I be a better man then he
by my truth my knaue ſhall he be
and lead theſe dogges all three.
Yeeld thee Frier in thy long cote my throte,
I beſhrew thy hart Knaue thou hurteſt
I trowe Frier thou beginneſt to dote
who made thee ſo malapert and ſo bold,
To come into this Forreſt heere,
among my fallow deere.
Goe louſe thee ragged knaue
if thou make many words I wil giue thee on ye eare
though I be but a poore Frier
to ſeeke Robin hood I am come heere
and to him my hart to break.
thou louſie Frier what wouldſt thou with him
he neuer loued Frier nor none of Friers kin.
Auaunt ragged knaue
or ye ſhall haue on the skin.
Of all the men in a morning thou art ye worſt
to meet with thee I haue no luſt,
For he yt meeteth a frier or a fox in the morning
to ſpeede ill that day he ſtandeth in ieopardie
therefore I had rather meet with the deuill
Frier I tell thee as I think,
then meete with a frier or a fox
in a morning or I drinke.
Auaunt thou ragged knaue this is but a mock
if yu make many words yu ſhalt haue a knock,
Hark frier what I ſay heere,
ouer this water thou ſhalt me beare
the bridge is borne away.
To ſay thee nay I will not
to let thee of thine oth it were great pitty & ſin
but vpon a Friers back and haue euen in.
Nay haue ouer.
now am I frier within & thou Robin without,
to lay thee heere I haue no great doubt:
Now art thou Robin without & I frier within
Lye there knaue. chooſe either ſinke or ſwim.
Why thou louſie frier what haſt thou doon
Marry ſet a knaue ouer the ſhoon,
Therefor thou ſhalt abye
Why wilt thou fight a pluck
And God ſend me good luck,
Then haue a ſtroke for Frier tuck.
Holde thy hand Frier and heare me ſpeak
Say on ragged knaue
me ſeemeth ye begin to ſweat.
In this Forreſt I haue a hound
I will not giue him for an hundreth pound:
Giue me leaue my horne to blowe,
that my hound may know.
Blowe on ragged knaue without any doubt,
vntill both thine eyes ſtart out:
Heere is a ſorte of ragged knaues come in
Clothed all in kendall greene
and to thee they take their way now.
Peraduenture they doe ſo
I gaue thee leaue to blow at thy will,
now giue me leaue to whiſtle my ſill.
Whiſtle Frier euill might thou fare,
vntill both thine eyes ſtare.
Now cut and Bauſe,
Bring foorth the clubbes and ſtaues
And downe with thoſe ragged knaues.
How ſaiſt thou Frier wilt thou be my man
to doo me the beſt ſeruice thou can,
Thou ſhalt haue both gold and fee,
And alſo heer is a lady free I will giue hir vnto thee
And her chaplain I thee make
to ſerue her for my ſake.
Heere is a huckle duckle an inch aboue the buckle
She is a trul of truſt to ſerue a frier at his luſt
A pricker, a prauncer, a tearer of ſheetes,
a wagger of buttocks when other men ſleeps,
Goe home ye knaues and lay crabs in the fier
for my lady & I will dance in the mire for very pure ioy.
Liſten my merry men all
and harken what I ſhall ſay,
Of an aduenture I ſhall you tell
that befell this other day:
With a proud potter I met
and a roſe garland on his head,
the flowers of it ſhone meruailous freſh
this ſeuen yeere & more he hath vſed this way
Yet was he neuer ſo curteous a potter
as one peny paſſage to pay.
Is there any of my merry men all
that dare be ſo bolde,
to make the Potter pay paſſage
either ſtluer or golde.
Not I maiſter for twenty pound ready tolde
For there is not amongſt vs all one that
dare meddle with that potter man for man
I felt his hands not long agon
but I had leuer be heere by thee,
therefore I know what he is
Meete him when ye will
or meete him when you ſhall,
He is as proper a man as euer you medled withall
I will lay with thee little Iohn xx. pound ſo red
if I with the potter meet
I will make him pay paſſage mauger his hed.
I conſent thereto ſo I eat bread,
If he pay paſſage mauger his head
twenty pound ſhall ye haue for your meed.
Out alas that euer I ſaw this day.
For I am cleane out of my way:
From Nottingham towne,
If I me hie not the faſter
Or I come there the market will be doon.
Let me ſee are thy pots whole and ſound.
Yea maiſter but they will not breake ye ground ſake
I will them break-for the cuckold thy maſters
And if they will break the ground
thou ſhalt haue three pence for a pound.
Out alas what haue you doon
if my maiſter come he will breake your crowne
Why thou whorſon art thou heere yet
thou ſhouldeſt haue been at market.
I met with Robin hood a good yeman
He hath broken my pottes
and cald you Cuckold by your name.
Thou maiſt be a gentleman ſo God me ſaue,
but thou ſeemeſt a naughty knaue:
Thou calleſt me a cuckold by my name.
And I ſwere by God and ſaint Iohn.
Wife had I neuer none
this cannot I deny
But if thou be a good fellow
I will ſell my horſe harnes, pots & paniers too
thou ſhalt haue the one halfe and I the other
if thou be not ſo content thou ſhalt haue
ſtripes if thou were my brother.
Hark potter what I ſhall ſay.
this 7. yeer and more thou haſt vſed this way
Yet were thou neuer ſo curteous to me
as one peny paſſage to pay.
Why ſhould I pay paſſage to thee,
For I am Robin hood cheefe gouernour
vnder the greene-wood tree.
This ſeuen yere I vſed this way vp and down
yet payed I paſſage to no man,
Nor now I will not begin do the worſt yu can.
Paſſage ſhalt thou pay heere vnder the greenwoodtree
or els thou ſhalt leaue a wed with me:
Yf thou be a good fellow as men doo thee call,
laye away thy bowe
And take thy ſword and buckler in thy hand
and ſee what ſhall befall.
Little Iohn where art thou,
Here maiſter I make auow
I toulde you maiſter ſo God me ſaue,
that you ſhould finde the potter a knaue:
Holde your buckler faſt in your hand,
and I will ſtiffely by you ſtand,
Ready for to fight,
Be the knaue neuer ſo ſtout
I ſhall rap him on the ſnout,
and put him to flight.
