Methods

Corpus

The English performance-language view of corpus v3 (EEBO-TCP texts, DEEP metadata, 580 edition documents): the language presented as spoken, sung or recited in performance — speeches, choruses, songs, and reviewed prologues, epilogues and inductions attributed to their edition, whether encoded in the body, front or back matter. Selection combines structural XML rules with recorded contextual decisions (the corpus repository's decision tables). Speaker labels, encoded stage directions, notes, headings and running titles are removed at extraction; book-level dedications and plot summaries are excluded. Spelling: a second view applies EarlyPrint's reg token by token where a node could be aligned to EarlyPrint (99.5 % of nodes paired; accepted reg values at 19 % of token positions); unaligned nodes keep the extracted text with only long s and word-initial VV normalized. EarlyPrint can supply corrected readings where its transcription has them, but the pairing does not establish that every <gap> was restored. The unregularized text is shown on each chunk page for comparison.

Name masking

Character names were replaced by the placeholder someone before embedding, play by play, using the cast lists of Kim's character-clustering project matched to each edition by its TCP id (484 editions matched exactly, 73 through the collection volume's base id, 1 by an explicit override; 22 editions without a cast list — pageants and entertainments — were left unmasked). Only persons are masked: places, nations and demonyms, deities, role words (king, duke, nurse) and personifications stay. A name that is also a common word is masked only when capitalised; spelling variants of a cast name within the edition (I/J, u/v, similarity ≥ 0.85) are caught. 86,736 of 8,588,995 tokens were replaced (1.0 %). Keywords are computed on the unmasked text, so names can appear in a topic's keyword list although the embedding never saw them. Nicknames absent from the cast lists (Harry, Jack) survive the masking and may still link passages.

Clustering

UMAP to 5 dimensions (n_neighbors 15, min_dist 0.05, cosine) → HDBSCAN (min_cluster_size 30, min_samples 10, Euclidean, EOM). Three seeds were run; this site shows seed 42 and reports each chunk's topic in the other seeds on its page.

Fitted on one representative edition per work: 15,370 chunks of 516 editions. The other 64 editions (2,052 chunks) were placed afterwards by umap.transform and hdbscan.approximate_predict and are excluded from the topic statistics and the genre comparison (each chunk page shows whether it was in the fit). Edition policy: the earliest dated edition, except the New Oxford Shakespeare control texts for eight Shakespeare works (Folio for seven, Q1 for Lear) — the policy of Kim's project, recorded in edition_selection.csv. Among the representative editions 59.6 % of chunks are unassigned.

Choice of clustering scheme

The same embeddings were clustered under several schemes, three seeds each; statistics on the representative editions. ARI = adjusted Rand index between seed pairs (a similarity of partitions, not a share of clusters reproduced); flip = share of chunks that are an outlier in one seed and assigned in another; name share = share of the top-10 keywords that are character names. HDBSCAN with min_samples 10 was taken as the main analysis (stable cores, few single-work clusters, coverage reported as a share); K-means 50 assigns every chunk but its partitions vary more between seeds and its cluster edges were found diffuse on reading, so it is kept as a limited comparison for a few core themes, not as a second analysis.

Keywords and labels

Class-TF-IDF (BERTopic's ClassTfidfTransformer, default settings) on one document per topic with a uniform vectorizer (min_df 1, max_df 1.0, alphabetic tokens, a stop-list including early-modern function-word spellings); KeyBERT-style words rank the same candidates by cosine to the topic centroid with the embedding model; MMR diversifies them (λ 0.5). Words capitalised in ≥80 % of their occurrences are marked as probable names. Labels are written by the researcher from the keywords and the chunks; AI drafts are marked as such until confirmed.

Review of the clusters

Drafts. For every cluster an AI draft was written from the uniform keywords, the work concentration (share of the largest work, of the three largest, number of works with three or more chunks) and a sample of chunks — the chunks read are listed per cluster (chunks_read, 63 clusters). Each draft proposes a content-first label without genre words, a pattern basis (thematic discourse / shared narrative / recurring role or name / mixed), a use category and a short basis (content note, concentration, suitability for cross-work comparison). Drafts never propose included; concentration in one work is recorded as a flag, not used as an exclusion rule.

Workbook. The drafts, statistics, three keyword lists and three representative chunks per cluster go into a review workbook (topic_sheet.xlsx). The reviewer edits three columns: the use category — included (enters the genre comparison), pending (read but undecided), contextual only (one work or one story, kept for context) — the label, and notes. A round-trip script validates ids, cluster sizes and values before writing the decisions back, keeps the drafts as they were before the review, and derives a review status from what changed.

Decisions. All 63 clusters were read and classified: 53 cross-work theme (confirmed, in the genre comparison), 6 pending review, 4 single work / story. Review status: 57 confirmed by the researcher, 6 left pending by the researcher. 31 clusters carry reviewer notes (which cite the excerpts read, mostly three passages from different works plus full-chunk follow-ups on boundary cases); 26 labels were revised from the draft. Two policies were fixed during the review: a shared story (Troy, Hercules, Thebes, Antony and Cleopatra, the Fairy Queen) counts as a subject for comparison in the same way as a subject area (Rome, English chronicle matter, the gods) does — the pattern basis keeps the distinction visible; and a cluster dominated by one work is not excluded for that reason but re-checked without that work (see Genre comparison). Each topic page shows the classification, its basis, the review status and the notes; the drafts before and after the review are kept in the repository (ab_spelling/drafts_masked_hdb10/).

Typicality

Cosine similarity between a chunk's embedding and the mean embedding of its topic. Unassigned chunks show their nearest topic instead.

Genre comparison

Chunk → edition → work → genre, word-weighted, editions of a work averaged, works equal-weighted; only topics classified as cross-work themes enter the comparison, and the share of each genre's words that falls outside them is reported. Kruskal–Wallis across genres with ≥10 works, Benjamini–Hochberg over the selected topics; descriptive. Dominant-work check: a topic in which one work holds ≥ 33 % of the topic's words is aggregated again without that work, and the Genre page reports how the highest genre and the means move (the clustering itself stays fixed; the threshold is an operational rule and does not cover several works of one author or story).

Genre of a work: the British Drama label (Wiggins & Richardson, via DEEP) when it is a single main genre; when it is compound or missing, the Annals of English Drama label (Harbage, Schoenbaum & Wagonheim, via DEEP) when that is a single main genre; otherwise the work is described but not placed in a genre. Both labels and the source used are recorded for every work (see the Genre page and each play page).
