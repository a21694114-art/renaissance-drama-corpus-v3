Nay, nay, I am Noble, fellow, very Noble, a very rock of friendship, but, but, I had a house and barn burned down to the ground, since you were here. How? How burned ask Sim? By fire, sir, by fire. To build up which, for I am a poor man, a poor man, I was forced by course of Law to enter upon your Land, and so for less money than you had of me, I was fain to sell it to another, that by four days' stay, a man should lose his blood, our livings, our blood, o my heart, o my head. Pray take it not so heinous, we'll go to him. I'll buy it again of him, he wonot be too cruel. A dog, a very dog, there is more mercy in a pair of unbribed Bailiffs, to shun all such solicitings, he's rid to York, a very cut-throat Rogue, but I'll send to him. A honest old man, how it moves him, this was my negligence, good Sim convey him into some warmer room, and I pray, however Fortune, she that gives ever with the dexterity she takes, shall please to fashion out my sufferings, yet for his sake, my deceased Father, the long friend of your heart, in your health, keep me happy. Oh right honest young man Sim. Sir, Blood. Have I done't well? The devil himself could not have done't better. I tell thee an old saw sirrah, he that dissembles in wealth shall not want, they say doom's day is coming, but think you not an't, this will make the pot seethe Sim, good Sir talk no more, my mouth runs over, Sleep, wake, worthy beggar, worthy indeed to be one, and am one worthily— how fine it is to wanton without affliction, I must look out for Fortunes, over again, no, I have money here, and 'tis the curse of merits, not to work, when she has money: there was a handsome Widow, whose wild mad jealous husband died at Sea, let me see, I am near Blackfriars, I'll have one start at her, or else— By my troth 'tis he Captain Young's son, I have loved him even with languishings, ever since I was a girl but should I know it, I should run mad sure, what handsome Gentlemen travail and manners makes, my Father begun to ye Sir, in a cup of final Beer. How does he pray? Pretty well now Sir. Mas 'tis small indeed— you'll pledge me? Yes sir. Pray will ye tell me one thing? What is't. Which is smaller, this Beer, or your maidenhead? the Beer a great deal Sir. I in quality. But not in quantity. No? Why? Let me try, and I'll tell you. Will you tell me one thing before you try? Yes. Which is smaller, this Beer or your wit? O the Beer, the Beer. In quality. Yes, in quantity.
