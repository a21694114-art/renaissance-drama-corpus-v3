Duke Rodorick, and my noble cousin Flanders: Are your Battalions ready for the charge? Ten thousand men of Orleans I command, And those are bravely marshalled on the plain, Ready to be commanded by your Highness. As many of the warlike brood of Mars Do call me General: those, my gracious Lord, Together with myself I recommend, To be commanded by your Majesty. Thanks, Earl of Flanders, Duke of Orleans, thanks, What lets us that we charge not on the Foe? My Lord of Pembroke, are your Englishmen squadroned with ours, and ready for the charge? The French and English make one warlike body, Whereof your Highness is the moving head: Or peace or war, as pleaseth you direct. Then war, and give the signal through the Host. Navarre, Navarre, submission were more meet, Then to add blood to wrong. What wrong, King Lewes? The Kingdom of Navarre we will acknowledge To hold of none but of the King of Kings. Three hundred years prescriptions on our sides, So long thy Ancestors by fealty Have held thy Kingdom of the Crown of France. Talk not of years, years limit not a Crown, There's no prescription to enthrall a King: He finds it written in the Rolls of time, Nauar's a Kingdom solely absolute, And by collusion of the Kings of France, Because it lies so fitly upon France, The people speaking all one mother tongue, It hath been wrested for a Royalty, Untruly due unto the Crown of France. That Pembroke speaks the truth, behold my sword, Which shall approve my words substantial. Pembroke, you are too plain in your discourse. I tell thee, Roderick, Pembroke soldier-like Hath truly opened, what ten thousand lives Will hardly do, if war be made the judge. If war be judge? why, shallow-witted Bourbon, Who shall decide this difference, but war? Hath not the judge put on his Scarlet rob? Is not the field prepared? our men in armour? The Trumpets ready for the sound of death? And nothing hinders us but our own words? Leave idle parley, my dread sovereign Lord, And soon resolve the Duke in fire and smoke, That he maintains a title false and forged, And that Navarre is a usurping Lord. On that i'll hazard all these valiant lives. Sound Drums and Trumpets, make King Lewes know, He makes his best friend prove his greatest Foe. Why pause our drums? our trumpets, speak as loud, Till the bright air be made a purple cloud. Pause, gracious father. Noble father, pause. Let Ferdinand thy son so far prevail, That peace, not war may end this difference. For Bellamira's sake abstain from war. Philip thy son humbly desires a peace. Let not my father sheath his warlike sword Within the bowels of his Countrymen. Thy daughter Katharina prays the like. From whence proceeds this sudden sound of peace? Comes it from me? what, from my Ferdinand? From Bellamira my sweet second self?
