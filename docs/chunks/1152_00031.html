How came ye hurt sir? I fell out with my friend the noble colonel, My cause was nought, for 'twas about your honour: And he that wrongs the Innocent ne'er prospers; And he has left me thus for charity, Lend me a bed to ease my tortured body, That ere I perish I may show my penitence, I fear I am slain. Help gentlemen to carry him, There shall be nothing in this house my Lord, But as your own. I thank ye noble sir. To bed with him, and wife give your attendance. Doctors and surgeons. True, it is so sir, And take you heed, this last blow do not spoil ye, He is not hurt, only we made a scuffle, As though we purposed anger, that same scratch On's hand he took, to colour all and draw compassion, That he might get into your house more cunningly, I must not stay, stand now, and you're a brave fellow. I thank ye noble colonel, and I honour ye, Never be quiet. He's most desperate ill sir, I do not think these ten months will recover him. Does he hire my house to play the fool in, Or does it stand on Fairy ground we are haunted, Are all men and their wives troubled with dreams thus. What ail you sir?
