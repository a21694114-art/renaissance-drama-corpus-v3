Admit it true, that she were far beyond me in all respects, does that give you a licence to forswear yourself? Forswear myself, how? Perhaps you have forgot the innumerable oaths you have uttered in disclaiming all for wives but me: I'll not remember you: God give you joy. Nay but conceive me, the intent of oaths is ever understood. Admit I should protest to such a friend, to see him at his lodging tomorrow: Divines would never hold me perjured, if I were struck blind, or he hid him where my diligent search could not find him: so there were no cross act of mine own in't. Can it be imagined I meant to force you to marriage, and to have you whether you will or no? Alas you need not. I make already tender of myself, and than you are forsworn. Some sin I see indeed must necessarily fall upon me, as whosoever deals with women shall never utterly avoid it: yet I would choose the least ill; which is to forsake you, that have done me all the abuses of a malignant woman, contemned my service, and would have held me prating about marriage, till I had been past getting of children: then her that hath forsook her family, and put her tender body in my hand, upon my word. Which of us swore you first to? Why to you. Which oath is to be kept then. I prithee do not urge my sins unto me, Without I could amend 'em. Why you may by wedding me. How will that satisfy my word to her? 'tis not to be kept, and needs no satisfaction, 'tis an error fit for repentance only. Shall I live to wrong that tender hearted virgin so? It may not be. Why may it not be? I swear I had rather marry thee then her: but yet mine honesty. What honesty? 'tis more preserved this way: Come, by this light servant thou shalt, I'll kiss thee on't. This kiss indeed is sweet, pray God no sin lie under it. There is no sin at all, try but another. O my heart. Help sister, this lady swoons. How do you? Why very well, if you be so. Sure a quiet mind lives not in any woman: I shall do a most ungodly thing. Hear me one word more, which by all my hopes I will not alter. I did make an oath when you delayed me so; that this very night I would be married. Now if you will go without delay, suddenly, as late as it is, with your own Minister to your own Chapel, I'll wed you, and to bed. A match dear servant. For if you should forsake me now, I care not, she would not though for all her injuries, such is her spirit, If I be not ashamed to kiss her now I part, may I not live.
