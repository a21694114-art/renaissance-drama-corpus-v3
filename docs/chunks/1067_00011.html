Yes honey suckle, and do as much for them one day, if things stand right as they should stand, Bill, Pigeon do; thou'st be my catamountain, and I thy sweet briar, Honey, we'll lead you to kind examples (pretty ones) believe it, and you shall find us, one in one, whiles hearts do last. Ever mine own, and ever. Well said old Touch hole. All happiness, all joy. A plenteous issue, A fruitful womb— Thou hast a blessing Secco. Indeed a' has Sir, if ye know all, as I conceive you know enough, if not the whole: for you have (I may say) tried me to the quick, through and through, and most of my carriage, from time to time. 'Twould wind-break a moil, or a ringed mare, to vie burdens with her. What's that you mumble, Gelding, shey. Nothing forsooth, but that y'are bouncing couple well met, and 'twere pity to part ye, though you hung together in a smoky chimney. 'Twere e'en pity indeed, Spadone, nay thou'st a foolish loving nature of thine own, and withheld well to plain dealings o' my conscience. Thank your Brideship— your Bawdship. Our sister is not merry. Sadness cannot Become a Bridal harmony. At a wedding, free spirits are required. You should dispense With serious thoughts, now Lady. Well said Gentlefolks. Fie Castamela sie, A dance, a dance. By any means, the day is not complete else. Indeed He be excused, By no means, Lady. We are all suitors. With your pardons, spare me For this time, grant me licence to look on. Command your pleasures, Lady,— every one hand Your Partner— nay, Spadone, must make one. These merriments are free. With all my heart, I'm sure I am not the heaviest In the company. Strike up for the honour of the Bride and Bridegroom. So, so, here's art in motion: on all parts, Ye have bestirred ye nimbly, I could dance now, e'en till I dropped again; but want of practice Denies the scope of breath or so, yet sirrah, My catamountain, do not I trip quickly, And with a grace too, sirrah. Light as a feather. Sure you are not without a stick of Licorice in your pocket forsooth; you have I believe stout lungs of your own, you swim about so roundly without rubs; 'tis a tickling sight to be young still. Madam Morosa? Child. To you in secret. That earwig scatters the troop now, I'll go near to fit'em. My Lord upon my life. Then we must sever. Ladies and gentlemen, your ears. Oh 'twas ever a wanton monkey— a' will wriggle into a starting hole so cleanly— and it had been on my wedding day,— I know what I know. sayst so Spadone? Nothing, nothing, I prate sometimes beside the purpose, whoreson lecherous weasel? Look, look, look how officious the little knave is— but— Why? there's the business, butts on one's forehead, are but scurvy butts. Spadone, discharge the fiddlers instantly.
