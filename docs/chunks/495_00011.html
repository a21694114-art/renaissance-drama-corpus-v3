With all my heart, we'll leave him with you sir, and I am sorry far for your heaviness. So this goes well, once I'll be blunt again. For now the times draws on of his awake. I never slept more sound in my life, But stay, how comes my hands so bloody, So is my face, me thinks, stay here's my man. Why how now blunt why stars thou so upon me, are you alive sir, Didst thou set any body to kill me. Not I sir but wicked Florance did, And haddest been slain, had it not been for me, Sentloe behold thou Harbert and thy friend, That thus hath waited on thee like thy man, To save thee from a thousand miseries, I am a amazed, and know not what to say O my dear Harbart: O my loving friend. Leave of imbracments till some other time, The king is come to Bristol, newly landed, Come as we go along I'll tell thee all, Things wonderful that yet thou dreamest not off. All hail thou blessed bosom of my peace, Richard finds instance of his home return, Bristol, thou happy road where first Island, Doth welcome me now from the holy land. Send word to London of our safe arrival, While we awhile in Bristol here repose us. Fame with her brazen trump hath born this tidings hither. And Bristol with their Citizens express, Their gladness by their triumph, at your safety. And we with you will put these triumphs on, But for this unlucky accident, Which makes old Eustice and Vmphrevil sad Which grieves me for the noble gentlemen. By blessed Saint Paul my liedg I cannot brook it to see my child, my air, my Anabell, this here: what a wretch was this? Now by saint Charity if I were judge, a halter were the least should hamper him. though nature wrassels with my stayed reason, and wills me plead for mercy for my son, yet justice with impartial wings directs, My thoughts from pity, and my words for right, My liedg to make an entrance to your fame, Regard Vmphreviles wrong, punish my son, I cannot lose him better than by law, Nor is he lost that doth example give, Of sweet amends to such as lewdly live. Virtue commends thy course, and patience his, In both I pity, what you both desire, If justice could be glazed with pities wings, Call for the prisoner, let us crown the time, With justice, for these honourable men. Have mercy Richard, mercy in a king, Is like the peerless Diamond set in gold, he out of envy and of fury speaks, I out of love and passion plead for him. What pleasing advocate hath pity raised, To plead the prisoners cause, himself not there. One that doth bear the greatest grief of all, The hapless wife of woeful Vallenger. Why thou art wronged, therefore shouldest claim, Revenge for thee, and justice for thy husband.
