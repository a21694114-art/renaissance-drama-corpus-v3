How say you boys, will you not? Yes noble father, if our words will serve And if our words will not, our swords shall. Call hither to the stake, my two rough bears. Call Buckingham, and bid him arm himself. Call Buckingham, and all the friends thou hast, Both thou and they shall curse this fatal hour. Are these thy bears? we'll bait them soon, Despite of thee and all the friends thou hast. You had best go dream again, To keep you from the tempest of the field. I am resolved to bear a greater storm, Then any thou canst conjure up to day, And that i'll write upon thy Burgonet, Might I but know thee by thy household badge. Now by my father's age old Neuells crest, The Rampant bear chained to the ragged staff, This day i'll wear aloft my burgonet, As on a mountain top the Cedar shows, That keeps his leaves in spite of any storm, Even to affright thee with the view thereof. And from thy burgonet will I rend the bear, And tread him underfoot with all contempt, Despite the Bearward that protects him so. And so renowned sovereign to arms, To quell these traitors, and their complises. Fie, charity for shame, speak it not in spite, For you shall sup with Iesus Christ to night. Fowl Stigmatic, thou canst not tell. No, for if not in heaven, you le surely sup in hell. So, lie thou there and breath thy last. What's here, the sign of the Castle? Then the prophesy is come to pass, For Somerset was forewarned of Castles, The which he always did observe, And now behold, under a paltry alehouse sign, The Castle in S. Albon, Somerset hath made the Wissard famous by his death. Clifford of Comberland, 'tis warwicke calls, And if thou dost not hide thee from the Bear, Now whilst the angry Trumpets sound Alarms, And dead men's cries do fill the empty auret Clifford I say, come forth and fight with me, Proud Northern Lord, Clifford of Comberland, Warwick is hoarse with calling thee to arms. Warwickestand still, and view the way that Clifford haws with his murdering curtle-axe, through the fainting troops to find thee out. Warwick stand still, and start not till I come. How now my Lord, what a foot? Who killed your horses The deadly hand of Clifford, noble lord, Five horse this day, slain under me, And yet brave Warwick I remain alive, But I did kill his horse he lou'de so well, The boviest grey that ere was bred in North. Hold War wick, and seek thee out some other chase Myself will hunt this Dear to death. Brave lord, 'tis for a crown thou fightest, Clifford farewell, as I intend to prosper well to day. It grieves my soul to leave thee vnassaild Now Clifford, since we are singled here alone, Be this the day of Doom to one of us, For now my heart hath sworn immortal hate To thee, and all the house of Eancaster.
