She has fitted you With a pair of fool's Coats, as handsomely As any tailor, that had taken measure, Give me thy hand. Nay lay your heads together How to revenge it, and so gentlemen I take my leave. She has abused us. Let us take his Council, we can be but what we are. A pair of credulous fools. This other fellow Fairefeild has prevailed. Which if he have— What shall we do? I think we were best let him alone. D'ee hear? we'll to her again, you'll Be ruled by me, and tell her what we think on her. She may come to herself, and be ashamed on't. If she would affect one of us, for my part I am indifferent. So say I too, but to give us both the canvas let's walk, and think how to behave ourselves. What d'ee mean to do with him? Thou art too much a Tyrant, the seven years are past, That did oblige me to expect my Husband Engaged to Sea, and though within those limits Frequent intelligence hath reported him Lost, both to me, and his own life, I have been careful of my vow; and were there hope Yet to embrace him, I would think another Seven years no penance, but I should thus Be held a cruel woman, in his certain Loss, to despise the love of all mankind. And therefore I resolve, upon so large A trial of his Constancy, at last To give him the reward of his respects. To me and— Marry him. You have apprehended! No marvel if men rail upon you then, And doubt whether a Widow may be saved, We Maids are thought the worse on, for your easiness, How are poor women overseen? We must Cast a way ourselves upon a whining lover In charity, I hope my cousins Ghost Will meet, as you go to Church, or if You scape it then, upon the Wedding night— Fie, Fie. When you are both a-bed and candles out. Nay put not out the candles. May they burn blue then, at his second kiss And fright him from— well I could say something But take your course— he's come already. Put him off, but another twelvemonth, so, so, Oh love into what foolish labyrinths Dost thou lead us! I would all women were But of my mind, we would have a new world Quickly, I will go study Poetry, A purpose, to write verses in the praise Of th' Amazonian Ladies, in whom only Appear true valour (for the instruction Of all posterity) to beat their husbands. How you endear your servant. I will not Be guilty of more stay. Sweet Lady. Y'are come in time Sir, to redeem me. Why Lady. You willbe as comfortable as strong waters, There's a Gentleman. So uncivil to affront you? I had no patience to hear him longer; Take his offence before you question him.
