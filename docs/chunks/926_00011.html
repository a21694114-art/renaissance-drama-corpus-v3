Dull Caitiff, leave these abortive Provects, And talk in the newest fashion. I'll have My very Dug bark i'th' Courtly garb. Step aside. They are as mad as thy Cousen. The excrements and mere defects of nature, shall be reduced to Ornaments in me. I'll feed upon the tongues of Nightingales, For so each fart I let, will be a Song— For the Peripatetics being Butchers Here in Sienna:— A Pallas hewn in an entire Carbuncle. Encircled with a More that flows with Lhasis— Deriu'd their Augury from the warm Entrails Of a Calf. Sir. These are some of those, that laughed at ye In the presence. At me? thou liest. They laughed at thee. Why then the Devil, will ne'er give a Man Leisure, to believe a truth. signior Lothario, the great Minion To our Duke: I greet your health, with all joy. And I with all humility. And I with all celerity. Hark! thou dull Sinner. Is this real? hah? Sir, let him, that hath a heart of his own Think what he list. Do they adore, or flout me now? All is witchcraft. know when the Moon winks There's something in't, besides an eclipse. Miscreant: What suspicious follies Dost thou create within that Wooden-skull? And with what Heathen-phrase uttered? Know Dog, If I employ my wrath— Alas sir i've more faults than misbelief. Therefore give me your blessing, and let me Go home in peace. 'tis true, when the sky falls We shall have Larks. But let weaker stomachs, Expect such curious meat. I can eat Oats, and Garlic, under my own Roof. How? will Borachio leave the Court? What accident of dire portent is fallen? Gentlemen applaud my patience: Because, He cannot furnish me with wholesome Suits, He doubts my power to get 'em granted. Why we, will furnish him with Suits. But wont ye flout, and play the knave with one? How (Knave!) was that the word? Interpret the word, as yourself shall please. I scorn to be your Dictionary. Marry come up: Are your ears so tender? I hope I'm a Man, although a sinner. Use no choler Amorous child. But if Thou wantest suits, thy Lord being near the Duke, May furnish thee with— Or methinks thou wouldst become a knighthood Get him to beg it for thee. No, no, Hot words make but warm air, A fig For a Knight-errant; that hath a style, and ne'er a hedge. Then get a Patent to survey Brine-pits. Or else for casting Ordinance in loam. Or else search Saint Peter's patrimony, Lay prebendries are good, and Simony Is an old Paradox. Hold, hold Enough sufficeth all women but whores. He that expects the Morning lengthens the Night Therefore straightway let my Lord get the Duke To sign these Patents: which done I'll return to the wife of my bowels, And die for joy.
