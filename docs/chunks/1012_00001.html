I shall applaud. The Novelties. And I admire. They tumble, My skull's too narrow. Now his Fancies caper. Confidence, wait you upon Opinion, Here Admiration, there Novelty, This is a place for jollity and Laughter Fancy will dance himself too. How like you this device? 'tis handsome— but Opinion will like nothing. It seems new. 'twas bold. 'twas jocund. Did not I do the fool well? Most Admirably. Nay, and the Ladies do but take My part, and Laugh at me, I am made, ha, ha. I could wish something sir, of other nature To satisfy the present expectation. I imagine, nay, I'm not ignorant of proprieties And persons, 'tis a time of peace, i'll fit you. And instantly make you a representation Of the effect. Of peace? I like that well. And since in nothing, they are more expressed Then in good fellowship, i'll present you with a Tavern. A spick and span new Tavern. Wonderful, here was none within two minutes. No such wonder Lady, Taverns are quickly up, it is but hanging out a Bush at a Nobleman's door, or an Alderman's gate, and 'tis made instantly. will't please you Ladies to except the wine? Well said Confidence. It will be new for Ladies To go to th' Tavern, but it may be a fashion, Follow me Admiration. And the fool, I may supply the absence of your Fiddlers. If we can, let's leave Opinion behind us, Fancy will make him drunk, I am glad they are off, are these effects of peace? Corruption rather. Oh the Beggars show The benefit of peace. Their very breath Hath stifled all the Candles, poisoned the Perfumes, Beggars a fit presentment? how They cleave still to my nostril, I must tell you, I do not like such base, and sordid persons, And they become not here. I apprehend, If these distaste you, I can fit you with Persons more cleanly, What think you of Projectors? How Projectors! Here's one already. This is a jockey, He is to advance a rare, and cunning bridle Made hollow in the Iron part, wherein A vapour subtly conveyed, shall so Cool and refresh a horse, he shall ne'er tire And now he falls to his pace. This other? His habit speaks him A Country fellow, that has sold his acres To purchase him a flail, which by the motion Of a quaint wheel, shall without help of hands, Thresh Corn all day, and now he lays about him. This with a face Philosophical and beard, Hath with the study of twenty years, found out A lamp, which placed beneath a furnace, shall Boil Beef so thoroughly, that the very steam Of the first Vessel, shall alone be able To make another Pot above seethe over. A most Scholastic project; his feet follow The motions of his brain. But what thing's this? A chimaera out of Rabelais? A new project, A Case to walk you all day under water. So vast for the necessity of air, Which, with an artificial bellows cooled Under each arm, is kept still from corruption, with those glass eyes, he sees, and can fetch up Gold, or whatever jewels ha' been lost, In any River of the World.
