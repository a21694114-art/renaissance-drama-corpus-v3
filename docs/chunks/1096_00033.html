Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

We must be careful to prevent their mutiny. It will become our wisdoms to secure the court And prisoner. Captain of the guard. 2. What can you say for yourself Lord Chancellor. Again, I confess all, and humbly fly to The royal mercy of the King. And this submission is the way to purchase it. Hear me great judges, if you have not lost For my sake all your charities, I beseech you, Let the King know my heart is full of penitence, Calm his high-going sea, or in that tempest I ruin to eternity, oh my Lords, Consider your own places, and the helms You sit at, while with all your providence You steer, look forth and see devouring quicksands, My ambition now is punished, and my pride Of state and greatness falling into nothing, I that had never time through vast employments To think of heaven, feel his revengeful wrath, Boiling my blood, and scorching up my entrills, There doomsday is my conscience black and horrid, For my abuse of justice, but no stings Pricked with that terror as the wounds I made Upon the pious Admiral, some good man Bear my repentance thither, he is merciful, And may incline the King to stay his lightning Which threatens my confusion, that my free Resign of title, office, and what else My pride looked at, would buy my poor lives safety, For ever banish me the court, and let Me waste my life far off in some Village. How? Did your Lordship's note his request to you, he would direct your sentence to punish him with confining him to live in the country, like the Mouse in the Fable, that having offended to deserve death, begged he might be banished into a Parmesan. I hope your Lordships will be more just to the nature of his offences. I could have wished him fall on softer ground For his good parts. My Lord, this is your sentence for you high misdemeanours against his Majesty's judges, for your unjust sentence of the most equal Lord Admiral, for many and foul corruptions and abuse of your office, and that infinite stain of the King's person, and honour, we in his Majesty's name, deprive you of your estate of Chancellor, & declare you uncapable of any judicial office, & beside condemn you in the sum of two hundred thousand crowns; whereof one hundred thousand to the King, and one hundred thousand to the Lord Admiral, and what remaineth of your estate to go to the restitution of those you have injured, and to suffer perpetual imprisonment in the Castle, so take him to your custody. Your Lordships have been merciful in his sentence. They have spared my life then, that some cure may bring, I spend it in my prayers for the King. Allegre I am glad he hath so much strength, I prithee let me see him. It will but Enlarge a passion— my Lord he'll come Another time and tender you his service.
