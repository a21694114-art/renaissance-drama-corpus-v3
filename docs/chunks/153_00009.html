According to my heart? Yet at least wise, prolong it for a day or two, while I get me away somewhither, that I may not behold it. Nay but listen to me now Carinus, I hold it in no wise the part of an honest man, to pike a thank where none is due unto him: God wot I am more desirous to be rid of this marriage, than you are to obtain it. You have revived my spirits. Now if either yourself, or Byrria here, can do aught in the matter, be doing, fain, find out and procure the means that you may have her: I for my part will so handle the matter, as she may not be married to me. I have my desire. Oh, in very good time do I see Davus, whose advise I use altogether. But in good sooth thou wilt tell me nothing, except such things as are not worth the knowing: dost thou not get thee hence. Yes truly, and that with a very good will. O Good God, what good news do I bring with me, But where shall I now find Pamphilus, that I may release him from that fear he is in, and fill his heart with joy. He is very merry, what the matter is I know not. It is nothing to any purpose, he hath not yet understood of this mischief. Whom I verily believe, if he have yet hard of the marriage prepared for him. do you not hear what he saith? That he is beside himself, seeking for me all the Town over. But where shall I seek for him, or whither shall I now first go? What, do you linger to speak to him? Well, I go my ways. Davus, come hither, stay. Who calls me? O my Master Pamphilus, you are the man I look for. Well met Carinus: I find you both in good time, even you two I would speaks with all. Davus, I am a forlorn man. But yet listen to me a little. I am cast away. I wot what you fear. Truly and my lief is in hazard in very deed. I wot also what you fear. I must be married. And that I know too. Yea but too day. You dull me with too many words, and yet I know the matter already. You fear lest you must marry Philumena: And you (Carinus) take care how you may marry her. Thou hast hit the nail on the head. That same is it. And in that, is there no danger at all: I warrant you. I pray thee for God's sake, rid me presently out of this fear, poor wretch that I am. Lo, I put you out of fear, Chremes will not at this time give you his daughter to wife. How knowest thou somuch?
