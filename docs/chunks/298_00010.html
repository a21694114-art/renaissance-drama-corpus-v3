Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Make Chastity Pandora's waiting maid, For modest thoughts beseems a woman best. Away with chastity and modest thoughts, Quo mihi fortuna si non conceditur uti? Is she not young? then let her to the world, All those are strumpets that are over chaste, Defying such as keep their company, 'tis not the touching of a woman's hand, Kissing her lips, hanging about her neck. A speaking look, no nor a yielding word, That men expect, believe me Sol 'tis more, And were Mars here he would protest as much. But what is more than this is worse than nought, I dare not stay lest she infect me too. what is he gone, then light foot Ioculus, Set me Pandora in a dancing vain, Fair mother I will make Pandora blithe, And like a Satire hop upon these plains. Go Cupid give her all the golden shafts. And she will take thee for a forester. I will and you shall see her straight in love. Here Venus sit, and with thy influence, Govern Pandora, Nature's miracle. Prithee be quiet wherefore should I dance? Thus dance the Satyrs on the even lawns. Thus pretty satire will Pandora dance. And thus will Cupid make her melody. Were I a man I could love thee. I am a maiden wilt thou have me? But Stesias saith you are not. What then? I care not. Nor I. Nor I, Then merely farewell my maiden head. These be all the tears I'll shed, Turn about and trip it. Venus'. Cupid and Ioculus, come leave her now. The boys are gone and I will follow them. I will not follow them they are to young. What honey thoughts are in Pandora's brain? Hospitis est tepedo nocte recepta sui. Ah I envy her, why was not I so? And so will I be, where is Iphicles, Melos, Learchus? any of the three? I cure the sick? I study Poetry? I think of honour and of chastity? No: love is fitter than Pandora's thoughts, Yet not the love of Stesias alone, Learchus is as fair as Stesias, And Melos lovelier than Learchus far. But might I chose, I would have Iphicles, And of them all Stesias deserves the least. Must I be tied to him? no I'll be loose, As loose as Helen, for I am as fair. Mistress, here be the herbs for my masters wound? Pretty Gunophilus, give me the herbs: Where didst thou gather them my lovely boy? Upon Learchus plain. I fear me Cupid danced upon the plain, I see his arrow head upon the leaves. And I his golden quiver and his bow. Thou dost dissemble, but I mean good sooth, These herbs have wrought some wondrous effect, Had they this virtue from thy Lily hands? Let's see thy hands my fair Gunophilus. It may be they had, for I have not washed them this many a day.

A speaking looke, no nor a yeelding worde, That men expect, beleeue me Sol tis more, And were Mars here he would protest as much. But what is more then this is worse then nought, I dare not stay least she infect me too. what is he gone, then light foote Ioculus, Set me Pandora in a dauncing vayne, Fayre mother I will make Pandora blyth, And like a Satyre hop vpon these playnes. Go Cupid giue her all the golden shafts. And she will take thee for a forrester. I will and you shall see her streight in loue. Here Uenus sit, and with thy influence, Gouerne Pandora, Natures miracle. Prethee be quiet wherefore should I daunce? Thus daunce the Satyrs on the euen lawnes. Thus prety Satyr will Pandora daunce. And thus will Cupid make her melody. VVere I a man I could loue thee. I am a mayden wilt thou haue me? But Stesias saith you are not. VVhat then? I care not. Nor I. Nor I, Then merely farewell my mayden head. These be all the teares Ile shed, Turne about and tryppe it. Uenus. Cupid and Ioculus, come leaue her now. The boyes are gone and I will follow them. I will not follow them they are to young. VVhat hony thoughts are in Pandoraes brayne? Hospitis est tepedo nocte recepta sui. Ah I enuie her, why was not I so? Thou doost dissemble, but I meane good sooth, These hearbes haue wrought some wondrous effect, Had they this vertue from thy Lilly hands? Lets see thy hands my fayre Gunophilus.
