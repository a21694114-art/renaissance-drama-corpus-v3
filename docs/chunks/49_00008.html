And shall his sprights have no reward their angers to appease? Yes very great, for all the world shall celebrate his praise, And lands unknown that never saw, the man so praised by fame, Shall heat and keep for many years the glory of his name. If bloodshed veil his ashes ought strike of an ox's head, And let no blood that may be cause of mother's tears, be shed. What furious frenzy may this be that doth your will so lead, This earnest careful suit to make in travail for the dead? Let not such envy toward your father in your heart remain, That for his sacrifice ye would procure another's pain, Proud tyrant, while prosperity thy stomach doth advance, And cowardly wretch that shrinks for fear in case of fearful chance. Is yet again thy breast inflame, with brand of Venus' might? Wilt thou alone so oft deprive Achilles of his right? This hand shall give the sacrifice, the which if thou withstand. A greater slaughter shall I make, and worthy Pyrrhus' hand. And now to long from PRINCE's slaughter doth my hand abide, And meet it were that Polyxene were laid by Priam's side. I not deny, but Pyrrhus chief renown, in war is this, That Priam slain with cruel sword, to your father humbled is. My father's foes we have them known, submit themselves humbly, And Priam presently ye wot, was glad to crave mercy. But thou for fear not stout to rule, liest close from foes up shit: while thou to Ajax, and Ulysses, dost thy will commit. But needs I must, and will confess, your father did not fear: When burned our fleet with Hector's brands, & Greeks they slaughtered wear. While loitering then a loof he lay, unmindful of the fight. In steed of arms with scratch of quill, his sounding harp to smite. Great Hector then despising thee, Achilles songs did fear: And Thessal ships in greatest dread, in quiet peace yet wear. For why aloof the Thessal fleet, they lay from Trojans hands, And well your father might have rest, he felt not Hector's brands. Well seems a noble king to give another king relief. Why hast thou then a worthy king bereaved of his life? A point of mercy sometime is, what lives in care to kill. But now your mercy moveth you a virgin's death to will. Account ye cruel now her death whose sacrifice I crave. Your own dear daughter once ye know, yourself to th'altars gave. nought else could save the Greeks from seas, but th'only blood of her: A king before his children ought, his country to prefer. The law doth spare no captives blood nor will'th their death to stay That which the law doth not forbid, yet shame doth oft say nay. The conqueror what thing he list, may lawfully fulfil.
