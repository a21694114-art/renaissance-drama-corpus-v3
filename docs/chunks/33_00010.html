If ye be set in mer●alynge Then shall ye here a marvelous thing And though in deed all be not true Yet sure the most part shall be new I did a cure no longer a go But Anno domini millelimo On a woman young and so fair That never have I seen a gayre God save all women from that likeness This want on had the fallen sickness Which by dissent came lineally For her mother had it naturally Wherefore this woman to recure It was more hard ye may be sure But though I boast my craft is such That in such things I can do much How oft she fell were much to report But her head so gydy and her heels so short That with the twyngling of an eye Down would she fall even by and by But or she would arise again I showed much practise much to my pain For the tallest man within this town Should not with ease have broken her sown All though for life I did not doubt her Yet did I take more pain about her Than I would take with my own sister Sir at the last I gave her a glister I thrust a thampyon in her tewel And bad her keep it for a jewel But I knew it so heavy to carry That I was sure it would not tarry For where gunpowder is once fired The tampyon will no longer be hyerd Which was well seen in time of this chance For when I had charged this ordinance suddenly as it had thonderd Even at a clap loosed her bumberd Now mark for here beginneth the revel This tampion flew ten long mile level To a fair castle of lime and stone For strength I know not such a one Which stood upon an hill full high At foot whereof a river ran buy So deep till chance had it forbidden Well might the regent there have riden But when this tampyon on this castle light It put the castles so far to flight That down they came each upon other No stone left standing by god's mother But rolled down so fast the hill In such a number and so did fill From bottom to bryme from shore to shore This foresaid river so deep before That who list now to walk thereto May wade it over and wet no shoe So was this castle laid wide open That every man might see the token But in a good hour may these words be spoken After the tampyon on the walls was wroken And piece by piece in pieces broken And she delivered with such violens Of all her inconueniens I left her in good health and lust And so she doth continue I trust. Sir in your cure I can nothing tell But to our purpose ye have said well.
