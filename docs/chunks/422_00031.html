Here daughter Venus, I drink to thee. Thank you, good Father Iupiter. Why, Mother Iuno! Gods and Fiends! what, wilt thou suffer this ocular Temptation? Mars is enraged; he looks big, and begins to stut, for anger. Well played, Captain Mars. Well said, Minstrel Momus: I must put you in? must I? When will you be in good fooling of yourself, fiddler? never? O, 'tis our fashion, to be silent, when there is a better Fool in place, ever. Thank you, Rascal. Fill to our daughter Venus, Ganymede; who fills her father with affection. Will't thou be ranging, Iupiter, before my face? Why not, Iuno? why should Iupiter, stand in awe of thy Face, Iuno? Because it is thy wives Face, Iupiter. What, shall a Husband be afraid of his wives Face? will she paint it so horribly? We are a King, Cotqueane; and we will reign in our pleasures; & we will cudgel thee to death, if thou find fault with us. I will find fault with thee, King Cuckold-maker: what, shall the King of Gods turn the King of Good fellows, and have no Fellow in wickedness? This makes our Poets, that know our Profaneness, live as profane, as we: By my Godhead, Iupiter; I will join with all the other Gods, here; bind thee hand and foot; throw thee down into earth; and make a poor Poet of thee, if thou abuse me thus. A good smart-tongued Goddess; a right Iuno. Iuno, we will cudgel thee, Iuno: we told thee so yesterday, when thou wert jealous of us, for Thetis. Nay, to day she had me in Inquisition too. Well said, my fine Phrygian Fry, inform, inform. Give me some wine, King of Heralds; I may drink to my Cockatrice. No more, Ganymede; we will cudgel thee, Iwo: By Styx, we will. i's well; Gods may grow impudent in Iniquity, and they must not be told of it. Yea, we will knock our Chin against our Breast; and shake thee out of Olympus, into an Oyster-boate, for thy scolding. Your Nose is not long enough to do it, Iupiter; if all thy Strumpets, thou hast among the Stars took thy part. And there is never a Star in thy Forehead, but shall be a Horn, if thou persist to abuse me, A good jest, I faith. We tell thee, thou anger'st us, Cotqueane; and we will thunder thee in pieces, for thy Cotqueanity: we will lay this City desolate, and flat as this hand, for thy offences. These two fingers are the Walls of it; these within, the People; which People, shall be all thrown down thus, and nothing left standing in this City, but these walls. Another good jest. O, my hammers, and my Cyclops! this Boy fills not wine enough, to make us kind enough, to one another; Nor thou hast not collied thy face enough, Stinkard. I'll ply the table with Nectar, and make them friends. Heaven is like to have but a lame Skinker, then.
