The wine, come coz, I'll talk with you anon. Do, lose no time, good quartermaster. There are the horse, come, Fly. Charge, in Boys, in; Lieutenant o'the ordinance. Tobacco, & pipes. Who's that? Old Iordan, good! A comely vessel, and a necessary. New-scoured he is: Here's to thee, Martial Fly. In milk, my young anon says. Cream o'the grape! That dropped from Iuno's breasts, and sprung the Lily! I can recite your fables, Here is, too, The blood of Venus, mother o'the Rose! The dinner is gone up. I hear the whistle. ay, and the fiddlers. We must all go wait. Pox of'is waiting, Quarter Master, Fly. When Chambermaids are sovereigns, wait their Ladies. Fly scorns to breath. or blow upon them, he. Old Parcel Peck! Art thou there? how now? lame Yes faith: it is ill halting afore cripples, I ha' got a dash of a jade, here, will stick by me. O you have had some fantasy, fellow Peck, Some revelation— What? To steal the hay, Out o'the racks again: I told him so, When the guests backs were turned. Or bring pe The bottom upwards, heaped with oats; and cry. Here's the best measure upon all the road! when You know the guest, put in his hand, to feel, And smell to the oats, that grated all his fingers upo'the wood— Mum! And found out your cheat. I ha'been i'the cellar, Pierce. You were then, there, upo' your knees; I do remember it: To ha'the fact concealed. I could tell more, soaping of saddles, cutting of horse tails, And cropping— pranks of ale, and hostelry— Which he cannot forget, he says, young Knight: No more than you can other deeds of darkness, Done i'the cellar. Well said, bold professor. We shall ha'some truth explained. We are all mortal, And have our visions. Truly it seems to me That every horse has his whole peck, and tumbles p to the eares in littour, When, indeed There's no such matter; not a smell of provender. Not so much straw as would tie up a horse-tail! Nor any thing i'the rack, but two old cob webs! And so much rotten hay, as had been a hen's riest! And yet he's ever apt to sweep the mangers! But puts in nothing. These are fits, and fancies, Which you must leave, good Peck. And you must pray t may be reueal'd to you, at some-times, Whose horse you ought to cozen; with what conscience; The how; and when; a parson's horse may suffer— Who's master's double beneficed; put in that. A little greasing i'the teeth; 'tis wholesome: And keeps him in a sober shuffle. His saddle too May want a stirrup. And, it may be sworn, His learning lay o' one side, and so broke it. They have ever oats i'their cloak-bags, to affront us. And therefore 'tis an office meritorious, To tith such soundly.
