Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Here's a benefit Placed most deservingly! I do not like it. I do not apprehend him. A fair gown Indeed, and soap, and starch enough, to dazzle The eyes of some young country heir, that has Never been drilled through Drury lane, or Bloomsbury. But 'pray thee (friend) whose daughter hast thou married? What may she have to name? What shall I answer? I am i'th' briers. Tell him 'tis Constantina Our justice's Niece. Most excellent dissembler! As though you know not Constantina sir. But is this Constantina? True. 'Tis truer; Somewhat than you do believe it is. Is this Jack Loveall's sister? 'Tis. But is this she Whom Cleopes once loved, and has forsaken? O me! why do I live and hear that name? Did you not mark that sigh? how smartly't came? No, no, I have not fitted you, I have not. 'Tis a young Roscius I tell you. No sooner Was Cleopes named, but the arch-villain sighed, As if it had been truly Constantina. I do not like this business yet. Is this That cried up wonder? that Fidelia? A sudden change. Yet at the last? 'tis well, I'll give the word Unto Pandora: but with special care That the boy knows not of his Master's presence. What business is't that this same Midwives face Does fetch and carry thus about I wonder? high, she appears again. All health old man. Old? and how old? but what's the news that you Are rigged with now? and whither bound I pray you? Next to that loving pair of friends, whose sorrows I have lamented oft, and amongst which I judge it not the least, that while ye two Discourse in sighs, and tears, that wanton maid That is the cause of all your heaviness, Lasciviously does sport herself, and melts In the embraces of another. How? Regardless of your woes, or her own honour. Now all the Gods! where is he? Woman speak, What is he for a man? I know him not, So far as to his name; but this mine eyes Dare witness, 'tis a composition Of blood and spirits not to be despised. A feature able enough to tempt; beside— Neander, whilst we strive about the shadow we have the substance ravished from us. Ha? It cannot be, 'tas no affinity With truth; It must not be believed good Lucius. Can ye retire yourselves under this tree A little, and expect? but ere I go, Ye shall both promise as ye're Gentlemen To endure the sight with patience. we will. It is enough. But does this woman gull us? Or is it real think'st thou? Hark. No more. Can ye believe it yet? are your eyes yet Instructed? 'tis my boy Endymion, Now hell and tortures! Were all odours lost, And beggared Nature had not sweets enough T' embalm the dying Phoenix left, from hence From this same lip, She might restore herself.
