Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

To morrows a new sweet. Yes to melt it, But let's agree tonight, how it shall be handled I'll have a new gown. shalt have any thing. And such a riding suit as Mistress Newlove's, What though I be no gentlewoman borne, I hope I may achieve it by my carriage. Thou sayst right. You promised me a horse too, and a lackey. Thou shalt have horses six, and a postilion. That will be stately sweet heart, a postilion. Nay we'll be in fashion, he shall ride before us In winter, with as much dirt would damp a musket, The inside of our coach shall be of scarlet. That will be dear. There is a die projecting Will make it cheap wench, come thou shalt have any thing. Where is this chest, I long sweet to behold Our Jndies. Mistress let's melt it first, and then 'tis fit You should dispose it, then 'tis safe from danger. I'll be a loving Mistress to my boy too. Now fetch it in and let's rejoice upon't. Hold your light Mistress, we may see to enter. Ha what's here? call you this a chest? We ha' missed sir. Our haste and want of light made us mistake? A very Coffin. How, a Coffin? Boy, 'tis very like one. The devil owed us a shame, and now he has paid us. Is this your Treasure? Bury me alive in't. It may be there is no room. Nay, I will search it, I'll see what wealth's within,—— a woman's face, And a fair woman's. I cannot tell sir, Belike this was the sadness that possessed 'em; The plate stood next, I'm sure. I shake, I shake Boy, what a cold sweat— This may work, what will become on's sir? She is cold, dead cold: d'ye find 'your conscience, d'ye bring your Gillians hither— nay, she's punished, Your concealed love's cased up? 'tis Maria, the very same, the Bride, new horror! These are fine tricks, you hope she's in a sound, But I'll take order she shall ne'er recover To bore my nose, come, take her up and bury her Quickly, or I'll cry out; take her up instantly. Be not so hasty fool, that may undo us; we may be in for murder so; be patient, Thou seest she's dead, and cannot injure thee. I am sure she shall not. Be not sir dejected, Too much a strange mistake! this had not been else, It makes me almost weep to think upon't. What an unlucky thief am I? I'll no considering, either bestir yourself, or— Hold. Let it not stay to smell then, I will not Endure the stink of a Rival. Would 'twere there again. We must bury her. But where o'th' sudden, or with what providence, That no eyes watch us. Take a Spade and follow me. The next fair ground we meet, make the Church yard; As I live, I'll see her lodged.
