I prithee do Kate, they say thou art a shrew. And I like thee the better, for I would have thee so. Let go my hand, for fear it reach your ear. No Kate, this hand is mine, and I thy love. Y faith sir no, the woodcoke wants his tail. But yet his bill will serve, if the other fail, How now Feranda, what, my daughter? she's willing sir, and loves me as her life. 'tis for your skin then, but not to be your wife. Come hither Kate, and let me give thy hand To him that I have chosen for thy love, And thou to morrow shalt be wed to him. Why father, what do you mean to do with me, To give me thus unto this brainsick man, That in his mood cares not to murder me? But yet I will consent and marry him, (For I me thinks have lived too long a maid,) And match him too, or else his manhood a good. Give me thy hand, Ferando loves thee well, And will with wealth and ease maintain thy state. Here Ferando, take her for thy wise. And sunday next shall be our wedding day. Why so, did I not tell thee I should be the man? Father, I leave my lovely Kate with you, Provide yourselves against our marriage day, For I must high me to my country house In haste, to see provision may be made, To entertain my Kate when she doth come. Do so, come Kate, why dost thou look So sad? be merry wench, thy wedding daie's at hand, Son, far you well, and see you keep your promise So, all thus far goes well. Ho Saunder. Saunder, I faith you are a beast, I cry God heartily mercy, my hearts ready to run out of my belly with laughing, I stood behind the door all this while, And heard what you said to her. Why, dost thou think that I did not speak well to her? You spoke like an ass to her, i'll tell you what, And I had been there to have wooed her, and had this Cloak that you have, I'd have had her before she Had gone a foot further, and you talk of Woodcoks for all this. With her, and I cannot tell you what. Well sirrah, and yet thou seest I have got her I mary, 'twas more by hap then any good cunning, I hope she'll make you one of the head men of the parish shortly. Well sirrah, leave your jesting and go to Polidor's house, The young gentleman that was here with me, And tell him the circumstance of all thou know'st, Tell him on sunday next we must be married, And if he ask thee whither I am gone, Tell him into the country to my house, And upon Sunday i'll be here again.
