ay marry sir, I'd buy one then myself. But this talk is by the way, where shall's sup tonight, Five pound received, let's talk of that, I have a trick worth all, you two shall bear him toth' Tavern, whilst I go close with his hostess, and work out of her, I know she would be glad of the sum to finger money; because she knows 'tis but a desperate debt, and full of hazard, what will you say if I bring it to pass that the hostess shall be contented with one half for all; and we to share other; fifth-shillings bullies. Why I would call thee King of Sergeants, and thou shouldst be Chronicled in the Counter book for ever. Well put it to me, we'll make a Night on't 'faith. 'sfoot I think he receives more money he stays so long. He tarries long indeed, may be I can tell you upon the good liking on't the Gentleman may prove more bountiful. That would be rare, we'll search him. Nay be sure of it we'll search him! and make him light enough. Oh here comes the Gentleman; by your leave sir. God you god den sirs,— would you speak with me; No, not with your worship sir, only we are bold to stay for a friend of ours that went in with your worship. Who? not the scholar? Yes e'en he and it please your worship? Did he make you stay for him? he did you wrong then why, I can assure you he's gone above an hour ago. How? sir? I paid him his money, and my man told me he went out at back door. back door? Why, what's the matter. He was our prisoner sir, we did arrest him. What he was not? you the sheriffs Officers— you were to blame then, Why did you not make known to me as much: I could have kept him for you, I protest He received all of me in Britain Gold Of the last coining; Vengeance dog him with't. 'sfoot has he gulled us so. Where shall we sup now sergeants? Sup Simon now, eat Porridge for a month; Well, we cannot impute it to any lack of goodwill in your Worship,— you did but as another would have done, 'twas our hard fortunes to miss the purchase, but if ere we clutch him again, the Counter shall charm him. The hole shall rot him. Amen. So, Vex out your Lungs without doors, I am proud, It was my hap to help him, it fell fit, He went not empty neither for his wit: Alas poor wretch, I could not blame his brain, To labour his delivery, to be free, From their unpitying fangs— I'm glad it stood, Within my power to do a Scholar good. How now, whose's that? what are you? The same that I should be Captain. George Pye-boord, honest George? why camest thou in half faced muffled so?
