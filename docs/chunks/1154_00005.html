Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Where do you lead me? How blessed are we, that Fortune hath So much befriended us in this happy? Convenient opportunity? 'Tis an unaccustomed favour, And we aught in duty to acknowledge A thankfulness for it. So sits the pride of Nature to outvie Each glorious Beauty of the checkered field, And Flora's richest Wardrobe. I perceive Y'ave practised long in th'flattering Rules of love. What will reward you? A kiss is more than I can merit. Will you sing. And you'll give me another for my pains. Put it to the trial, You shall not lose your labour. Sat while I do gather flowers, And depopulate the Bowers. Here's a kiss will come to thee; Give me one, I'll give thee three. Thus in harmless sport we may, Pass all idle hours away. Hark, hark how fine The Birds do chime, And pretty Philomela Her moan doth tell. Than pity, pity love, and all is well. Here's the Violet, Pink, and Rose, The sweetest breathe for the nose. Yet thy breath to me doth yield Moore fragrant scents than all the field. Love cares not for flowers or toys, Play games for your apish Boys. Nor superstition For his condition Is for to know if you love or no? Than answer, love I or no. But yet me thinks that face should be The Model of true constancy: Therefore no reason have I, To suspect thy loyalty, Here's another kiss for thee. Give me one, I'll give thee three. Thus in harmless sport we may, Pass all idle hours away. Hark, hark how fine The Birds do chime, And pretty Philomela, Her moan doth tell▪ Than pity, pity Love, and all is well. Y'ave sung me most a sleep, my eyes are dull and heavy, Faith I must make thy lap my pillow. Repose thy gentle head on't. Alas! I'm heavy too, and must obey my destiny. Sleep on, sleep on, For we have so decreed, That thou must bleed: Sleep on, sleep on, And mayst thou never rise, For blood the shepherd cries. Sleep on, sleep on. Rise, rise Lysander to prevent What the Destinies decreed. Thou art constant, permanent, And must not bleed▪ Thy constant seed Shall be the shepherd's joy, Not annoy Shall attend Such a friend As the Lasses need. Rise, rise, awake! And sleep of shake; The Heavens are pleased thy part to take, For thy Love's sake. Lysander, where art? Lord how my fancy's troubled! How fares my Gloriana? I have had Strange thoughts that would have dispossessed Me of my rest, had I not took it here On your sacred lap. That is the man: upon him instantly While I attach his Mistress. Ah me Lysander, what means these sad spectacles▪ We must have life and death. Both life and death: how can that be? Thy life, and thy Lysander's death. I've herded that voice ever now Afford more milder language. Stop her mouth jest she does charm me Unto pity.

'Tis an unaccustom'd favour, And we ought in duty to acknowledge A thankfulnesse for it. A kisse is more than I can merit. Will you sing. And you'l give me another for my paines. Put it to the tryall, You shall not loose your labour. Sit while I doe gather flowers, And depopulate the Bowers. Here's a kisse will come to thee; Give me one, I'le give thee three. Thus in harmlesse sport wee may, Passe all idle houres away.
