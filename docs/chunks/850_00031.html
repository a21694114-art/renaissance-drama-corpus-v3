Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Ah my good Lord, cannot now deny what I have said, o'erheard me, as my bleeding heart Was making confession of my crime. I told him an t shall please your Lordship's The king's officers had eyes to hear such rascals. You have been careful in your office Constable, You may now leave your Prisoner, I'll leave the fell on with your Lordship. Farewell good Murder I see will out. Why didst thou poison him. I was poor, And want made me be hired. Hired, by whom? By Count Virro, There he stands. I do beseech your Lordships not to credit What this base fellow speaks, I am innocent. I do believe you are, sirrah speak truth, You have not long to live. Please it your Lordship I may relate the manner. do. Eugenio was alive when first the news Was spread in Syracuse he was dead, Which false report Count Virro crediting, Became an earnest suitor to his Sister Thinking her Heir, but finding afterwards Her brother lived, and coming home Not a day's journey hence, he sent me to him, And with a promise of five hundred crowns Hired me to poison him, that this is true here's his own hand to witness it against him, Please it your Lordships to peruse the writing. This is his hand. Sure as I live, I have seen Warrants from him with just these Characters. Besides methinks this fellows Tale is likely. 'tis too true, This fellows sudden going from my house Put me into a fear. Count Virro, stand to the bar, What can you say to clear you of this murder? Nothing my Lords, I must confess the fact. Why then against you both do I pronounce. Sentence of death. The Law is just. Wretch that I am, is my dissembled grief Turned to true sorrow, were my acted tears But prophecies of my ensuing woe, And is he truly dead. oh pardon me Deie Ghost of my Eugenio, twas my fault That called this hasty vengeance from the Gods, And shortened thus thy life, for whilst with tricks I sought to fasten wealth upon our house, I brought a cannibal to be the grave Of me and mine, base bloody murderous Count. Vile cozener, cheating Lord, dissembler. Peace, stop the mouth of malediction there, This is no place to rail in. Ye just powers, at to the quality of mans offence, pe your correcting rods, and punish there Where he has sinned, did not my bleeding heart Bear such a heavy share in this day's woe, I could with a free soul applaud your justice. Lord Euphues and Philocles forgive me To make amends, I know's impossible For what my malice wrought, but I would fain Do somewhat that might testify my grief And true repentance. This is that I looked for. You're kind too late my Lord, had you been thus When neede required, you'd saved yourself and me, Our hapless sons, but if your grief be true can forgive you heartily.
