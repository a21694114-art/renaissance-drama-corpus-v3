Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Let's be gone? Have patience worthy Lords, and calm your spirits. Peace prating Scholar: bid the Sea be still, When powerful winds doc toss the raging waves, Or stay the winged lightning in his course; When thou dost this, thy words shall charm me too. Till then preserve thy breath. Mavortius, dar'st thou maintain thy words? How? dare Philarchus? yes, I dare do more; In blood or fire; or where thou darest not come; In the numb fingers of cold death I dare. Swallow those words, or thou shalt eat my sword. He is no Ostrich sir he loves no iron. And yet methinks he should be by his plume. What are you playing with my feather too? O stay your rages, Let not Ambition captivate your blood, Make not your hate's objects for vulgar eyes. A pox upon this linguist, take him hence; Philarchus, I defy thee, and in scorn, Spit on thy bosom: vowing here by heaven, If either sword, or fire, or strength of men, Or any other steeled violence, Can bring to swift confusion what is thine, Upon this grateful soil; it shall be done. And when 'tis done, I will restore my wrongs Out of thy Forts, thy Castles and thy lands. My lands? ay, factious Lord, till then adieu, we'll shine like Comets in next interview. My soul is big in travail with revenge, And I could rip her womb up with a stab, To free th' imprisoned issue of my thought. O, how this vulture, (vile Ambition,) tears on the heart of greatness, and devours, Their bleeding honours, whilst their empty names, Lie chained unto the hill of infamy: Now is the time wherein a melting eye May spend itself in tears, and with salt drops, Write woe, and desolation in the dust, Upon the frighted bosom of our land, Pity and Piety are both exiled, Religion buried with our Father's bones, In the cold earth; and nothing but her face, Left to adorn these gross and impious times. Liberty, liberty, liberty. Nay but stay, stay, my Masters: we have not insulted yet who shall be our Captain. Mass that's true: faith let's all be Captains. Content, so we shall be sure to have no equality amongst us. O, it's best, for, (for mine own part) I scorn to have an equal. Well then: what exploit shall we do first? Marry I'll tell you: Let's pluck down the Church, and set up an Alehouse. O excellent, excellent, excellent, a rare exploit, a rare exploit. Good: this is for exploit: but then there's a thing called Action. O, that's going to Sea; that, we have nothing to do withal, No, we are all for the land, we. Land, I: we'll pluck down all the noble houses in the land, ere we have done. It were a most noble service, and most worthy of the Chronicle.
