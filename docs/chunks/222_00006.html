You must either fight sirrah, or else be hanged: Go take him hence again to prison. Give me my glove, why minion can you not see I cry you mercy Madam, I did mistake, I did not think it had been you. Did you not, proud French-woman, Could I come near your dainty visage with my nails, I'd set my ten commandments in your face. Be patient gentle Aunt, It was against her will. Against her will good King she'll dandle thee, If thou wilt always thus be ruled by her: But let it rest, as sure as I do live, She shall not strike dame Elinor vnreuengde. Believe me my love, thou wert much to blame, I would not for a thousand pounds of gold, My noble uncle had been here in place. But see where he comes, I am glad he met her non Uncle Gloster, what answer makes your grace Concerning our Regent for the Realm of France? Whom thinks your grace is meetest for to send? My gracious Lord, than this is my resolve, For that these words the Armourer doth speak, Doth breed suspicion on the part of York, Let Somerset be Regent over the French, Till trial's made, and York may clear himself. Then be it so my Lord of Somerset, We make your grace Regent over the French, And to defend our rights 'gainst foreign foes, And so do good unto the Realm of France, Make haste my Lord, 'tis time that you were gone, The time of truce I think is full expired. I humbly thank your royal majesty, And take my leave to post with speed to France. Come uncle Gloster, now let's have our horse, For we will to Saint Alban's presently, Madam, your hawk, they say is swift of flight, And we will try, how she will fly to day. Here sir Iohn take this scroll of paper here, Wherein is writ the questions you shall ask, And I will stand upon this Tower here, And hear the spirit what it says to you, And to my questions, write the answers down. Now sirs begin and cast your spells about, And charm the fiends for to obey your wills, And tell Dame Elinor of the thing she asks. Then Roger Bullenbrooke, about thy task, And frame a circle here upon the earth, Whilst I thereon all prostrate on my face, Do talk and whisper with the devils below, And conjure them for to obey my will. Dark night, dread night, the silence of the night, Wherein the Furies mask in hellish troops, Send up I charge you from Sosetus lake, The spirit Askalon to come to me, To pierce the bowels of this centric earth, And hither come in twinkling of an eye, Askalon, Ascenda, Ascenda. Now Bullenbrooke, what wouldst thou have me do? First, of the King, what shall become of him? The Duke yet lives that Henry shall depose, Yet him out live, and die a violent death.
