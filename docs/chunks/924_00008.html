What do you mean to do with your knife, tossed? that will scarce cut Beer and 'twere buttered. Come not near me, Nutmeg, lest I grate you, and slice you: Nutmeg, do you mark? Let's in, and make 'em friends. How now tossed? 'tis all one for that: Oh, are you there? pray tell me which of 'em is't? Is what? Why they are friends: what did you mean Sugar, to make tossed burn thus? No such matter. You will not tell me then. Hark you Beer, march-beer, this way a little. What dost thou mean to do with thy knife? I must stir you a little Beer: what colour had you to quarrel with my Master? Ale. We are sworn brothers. We were at difference, and Wine too. but— Wine too But, but me no butts, I care not a straw for his butts; d'ee hear sir, do you long to be graves's Wine? We are all friends. ay, ay, all friends on my word, tossed. Fire and water are not to be trusted, away new River, away, I wash my hands on thee. Come hither again, tossed. Over head and ears in Ale. How comes this about, Sugar? The truth is, sir, I told him of some difference between you, for he and I had been fallen out, and I had no other security to put in for myself, than to put him upon somebody else. Nutmeg durst scarce speak to him, he was ready to put me in his pocket. I am cool again: I may believe you are friends; then I am content to put up. Sugar and Nutmeg, come, we be three. Let's be all one rather: and from henceforth since they are so well accorded, let's make no difference of our Masters, but belong to 'em in common: for my part, though I wait upon Wine, it shall not exempt my attendance on Beer, or Ale, if they please to command Sugar. A match. I am for any thing but Water. And I. But my service shall be ready for him to, Water and Sugar I hope, may be drunk together now and then, and not be brought within compass of the Statute, to be put i'th' stocks for't, Godamercy Sugar with all my heart, I shall love thy company, far I am solitary, and thou wilt make me pleasant. Stay. Hark Music? Oh some friends of mine, I know 'em, they often come upon the water: let's entertain the air a little, never a voice among you? I Iouiall Wine exhilarate the heart. March Beere is drinke for a King. But Ale, bonny Ale, with Spice and Tost, In the Morning's a daintie thing. Then let vs be merry, wash sorrow away, Wine, Beere, and Ale, shall be drunke to day. I generous Wine, am for the Court. The Citie calls for Beere. But Ale, bonny Ale, like a Lord of the Soyle, In the Countrey shall domineere. Then let vs be merry, wash sorrow away, Wine, Beere and Ale shall be drunke to day. Why, now could I dance for joy.
