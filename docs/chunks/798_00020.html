Yes marry are ye, Sir, for my part, if you'll be quiet, i'll have a sign made of ye, and it shall be called the four Kings of Turnbole. Will you fetch us wine? And a whore (sirrah) Why what d''ee think of me, am I an Infidel, a Turk, a Pagan, a Sarazen, I have been at Bess Turnip's, and she swears all the Gentlewomen went to see a Play at the Fortune, and are not come in yet, and she believes they sup with the Players. damn, we must kill all those rogues, we shall never keep a whore honest for them. Go your ways, sirrah, we'll have but a gallon a piece, and an ounce of Tobacco. I beseech you, let it be but pottles. S'hart you rogue? Master Well-tried, welcome as my soul. Noble Lad, how dost thou? As welcome, as the Tobacco and the Wine Boy. Damn thou art. Bless me (save you Gent) They have not one face among 'em. I could wish myself well from them, I would I had put out something upon my return, I had as leave be at the Barmuthoes. Pray welcome this Gentleman. Is he valiant? Faith he's a little faulty that way: somewhat of a bashful and backward nature, yet I have brought him amongst you, because he hath a great desire to be fleshed. Yes faith Sir, I have a great desire to be fleshed: now Mr. Well-tried said, he would bring me to the only flesh-mongers in the town. Sir, he cannot endure the sight of steel. Not steel? zounds. Now I am going. Here's to you sir, i'll fetch you again with a cup of sack. I pledge you sir, and begin to you in a cup of Claret: Hark you my Lo: what will you say, if I make you beat all these out of the room? What will I say? why I say it is impossible, t's not is mortal man. Well drink apace, if any brave you out brave him, I'll second you, they are a Company of cowards believe me. By this light I would they were else, if I thought so, I would be upon the jack of one of 'em instantly, that same little Dame me, but Mr. Well-tried if they be not very valiant or dare not fight! how come they by such Cuts and gashes, and such broken faces? Why their whores strike 'em with Cans, and glasses, and quart pots, if they have nothing by 'em, they strike 'em with the Pox, and you know that will lay one's nose as flat as a basket hilt Dagger. Well let me alone. This bully dares not drink. Dare I not Sir. Well said, speak to him man. You had best try me Sir. we four will drink four healths to four of the seven deadly sins, Pride, Drunkenness, wrath & Lechery.
