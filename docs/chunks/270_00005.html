I pray thee please the cynic, fit his vain. Fellow behold, I will effect thy will. What profit hast thou by that breath of wind? Why, it warms my hands. But now the heat is laid. It is. Such is the gold, and so it doth abide, A breath of pleasure wavering but a space, Maintained by mighty care, but quickly lost. Now Fortunio let us see what beauty is, Seest thou not this sprig, be't not fresh and green, Now look again, a little violence makes it deformed: Why such is beauty sir, a bait wherewith the world Doth angle arts, entangle towardness, Enforceth reason, traverseth advice: I pray thee let me serve thee Fortunio. To what intent? Because I hate thy course, and will instruct thee, If thou be wise to mark, and prove, and know an honest man. Well, I entertain thee, thou shalt tend on me, But first tell me, whence art thou? Where wast thou borne? I first was borne to be gentle, Nature enforced the seed of good and bad in me, Till death threatened to whip me for my sins, Mercy stepped in, Repent shed tears and kissed me, Devotion healed me, and new christened me, In my own blood that dropped from this wound, And called me Penitent experience. Eta serva vostra fettisima seruidore siniore. Speaks in parables. Let him attend, 'tis time to knock up Lelios' household train. Who knocks there? The prince's guard. Guard, we have no need of guards, go to the tailors, Keep out I say. Sir, if I catch you. I and you can. What a fool is this? What an ass is that? What noise is this? What mean this troop of armed men about my door? Madame, the Senate by a late decree, Hath sent us to make search for Lelio, And if we find him not, to seize his goods. All what is his, my Lord, you may command, The scourge which God afflicteth on our heads, Is for our sins, we take our harms in gree, Go when you will, search where you please, And leave the rest for this poor maid and me. Did ever eyes behold so fair a face? Look not Fortunio, eyes are arrows keen, That wounds all unawares, and are not seen. Why weeps this tender maid, why grieves the mother 'tis I should weep, and I will weep for both, Fie on Sempronio that was so unkind. Fond man, why dost thou torment thyself? I beat Sempronio for abusing thee, Thou lose unbridled man, the cause of harms, Pardon Annetta, pardon Lucida. What ails this aged man he stormeth so? Some lunacy surpriseth me I fear, Art thou Sempronio? This is Annetta, that Lucida, thou Fortunio, But I am not Sempronio, but penitent experience Fair Lucida, as bright as is the morning star. Dry up thy tears, let not thy father's fall Depress thy courage, but revive thy spirits, And think thy beauty sufficient to wed thee presently
