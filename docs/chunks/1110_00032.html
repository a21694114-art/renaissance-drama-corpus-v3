He says true sir, Let all your anger then conclude in this, And bid the Devil take him. A good riddance. I know not how you could dispose him better And empty all your wishes. Well, I am pleased, Employ your best discretions. But where's Nan? Sick in her Chamber, where she keeps her Bed, And dares not thence remove. The worse for her, Yet for our purpose better, as it haps, Because she shall not see him, nor he her; Hark, there's one knocks, 'tis Slightall on my life; Disperse I do entreat, I'll answer him. Where's this Three-headed Dog that keeps Hell gates? He knocks that fain would enter. 'Tis the same, I know him by his roaring. If this, as many men give out, be Hell, Show me the Master? he that keeps the house: Pluto that great grim sir. I am the Lord Of this poor Manor; now, I cannot tell By what hard Fate distressed. Oh, you keep lodgings, And as I understand, the Devil here hath late took up his Inn. My greater grief sir. I would for one night be his Chamber-fellow, Shall I have entertainment, good mine Host? I would not wish you sir. Wish me no wishes, I am the Knight adventurer that would do't: One thing resolve me; hath the Devil your Guest A Horse in the Stable? None sir, I assure you. Then make him pay for's Bed; it is the custom Of every Inn through England. Sir, I know you, And ever wished you to your hearts desire, How well you may remember; if forgot, In you 'tis error, and no fault in me. Which love I thus have studied to requite, To conjure hence your Devil; how appears it? In Feminine shape? Yes, like a woman's sure. But not like Madam Proserpine your wife? No shape else can affright me. I must confess A Devil of her tongue, but no way else sir. Show me my Chamber. This. My lodging. There Command me two fair Tapers, that may last And burn out this night's hell. Instantly. Those, my good sword, this Book, and my bold heart Are Guards sufficient 'gainst a thousand shadows Of no Corporeal essence capable. Here be your Lights. Some wine too, I entreat, 'Tis the best armour to a fainting soul, And then no further trouble. It shall be done. Whom am I to encounter? singly too? Without a second? spirits, or Phantasms? Ghosts being, or imaginary dreams? Not in the comfortable day, and view Of judging eyes, but in the solitude Of melancholy darkness? I'll not think of't, before I find the truth, or mockery. I have brought your Livery. Indeed I must thank you for't? indeed I am your man; now, if you please, Leave me to my adventure. Rest may your Body, And peace possess your thoughts.
