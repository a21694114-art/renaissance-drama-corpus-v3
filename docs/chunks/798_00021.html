I'll pledge 'em, and I thank you, I know 'em all, here's one. Which of the sins? By my troth e'en to Pride. Why well said, and in this do not you only pledge your Mistress health, but all the women's in the world. So now, this little Cup to Wrath, because he and I are strangers. Brave boy, Dame me he shall be a roarer. Damn me, I will be a roarer, or't shall cost me a fall. The next place that falls, pray let him have it. Well, I have two of my healths to drink yet, Lechery and Drunkenness which e'en shall go together. Why how now my Lord, a Moralist? Damn me, art thou a Lo: what virtues hast thou? virtues? enough to keep ere a Dame me Company in England, methinks you should think it virtue enough to be a Lord. Will not you pledge these healths? we'll have no observers. Why, Monsieur Whore-bang? I am no play maker, and for pledging your healths, I love none of the four, you drank to so well. zounds you shall pledge me this. Shall I? Puff, will your Lordship take any Tobacco? you Lord with the white face? Heart he cannot put it through his nose. Faith you have near a nose to put it through, d'ee heart blow your face sirrah. I am a mad man, look is not that one of their heads? Fie no my Lord. Damn me but 'tis, I would not wish you to cross me a purpose, if you have any thing to say to me, so, I am ready. Oh brave Lord, many a roarer thus is made by wine: come it is one of their heads my Lord. Why so then, I will have my humour, if you love me, let's go break windows somewhere. Drawer, take your plate, for the reckoning there's some of their cloaks: I will be no shot-log to such. God's blessing o'your heart, for thus ridding the house of them.
