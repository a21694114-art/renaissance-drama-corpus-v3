Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Ha ha, for christians, thinkest thou he is one? For men: hast thou opinion he is a man? He that changes himself to sundry shapes, Is he a christian? can he be a man? O, Irreligious thoughts, Why worthy Prince I saw him christened, dept into the font Then nine times like the northern laplanders, He backward circled the sacred Font, And nine times backward said his Orisons, As often cursed the glorious host of heaven, As many time's invocke the fiends of hell, And so turned witch, for Gloster is a witch. Have patiented Gentle Prince, he shall appear, Before your Kingly father speedily. Shall he indeed? sweet comfort kiss thy cheek, Peace circle in thy aged honoured head, When he is taken: Hermit I protest I'll build thee up a chapel and a shrine: I'll have thee worshipped, as a man divine, Assure he shall come, and Skinke shall come. I that same Skinke, I prithee send that Skinke, Send both, and both as prisoners crimminate Shall forfeit their last lives to England's state, Which way will Faukenbridge? Over the water, and so with all speed I may to Stepney I must to Stepney too, and revile, and be blithe, Old wink at my mirth, 'tmay make amends, So thou, and I, and our friends, may be friends, Withal my heart, withal my heart Prince, Old Faukenbridge will wait upon your grace, Be good to Gloster for my Marrians sake, And me and mine you shall your servants make, Of that anon my pleasure being served, Gloster shall have what Gloster hath deserved. Why, that's well said, adieu good honest Hermit, Hermit farewell, if I had my desire, I'll make the world thy wondrous deeds admire, Still good, still passing good, Gloster is still Henry's true hate, foe to Iohns froward will. No more of that for them in better time, If this same Hermit be an honest man, He will protect me by this simple life, If not I care not, I'll be ever Gloster, Make him my foot stole if he be a slave, For Baseness over worth can have no power. Robin be think thee, thou art come from Kings. Then scorn to be slave to underlings, Look well about thee Lad and thou shalt see, Them burst in envy that would injure thee. Hermit I'll meet you in your Hermit's gown, Honest, I'll love you: worse, I'll knock you down. Kind friends, we have troubled Lady Faukenbridge, And either she's not willing to be seen, Or else not well: or with our boldness grieved, To ease these I have brought you to this window, Knowing your are in music excellent, I have penned a ditty here: and I desire You would sing it for her love and my content, With all my heart my Lord. Your excellence forgets your Princely worth, If I may humbly crave it at your hands, Let me desire this music be dismissed.
