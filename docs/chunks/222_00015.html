Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Doth he not twit our Sovereign lady here, As if that she with ignominious wrong, Had suborned or hired some to swear against his life? Yea but I can give the loser leave to speak. Far truer spoke than meant, I lose indeed, Beshrew the winners hearts, they play me false. he'll wrest the sense and keep us here all day, My Lord of Winchester see him sent away. Who's within there? take in Duke Humphrey, And see him guarded sure within my house. O! thus king Henry casts away his crouch, Before his legs can bear his body up, And puts his watchful shepherd from his side, Whilst wolves stand snarring who shall bite him first. Farewell my sovereign, long mayst thou enjoy, Thy father's happy days free from annoy. My lords, what to your wisdoms shall seem best, Do and undo, as if ourself were here. What will your highness leave the Parliament? Yea Margaret, my heart is killed with grief, Where I may sit and sigh in endless moan, For who's a traitor? Gloster he is none. Then sit we down again my lord Cardinal, Suffolk, Buckingham, York and Somerset, Let us consult of proud duke Humphries fall, In mine opinion it were good he died, For safety of our King and common wealth. And so think I madame, for as you know. If our King Henry had shook hands with death, Duke Humphrey then would look to be our King, And it may be, by policy he works, To bring to pass the thing which now we doubt, The fox barks not when he would steal the lamb, But if we take him ere he do the deed, We should not question, if that he should live, No, let him die, in that he is a fox, Lest that in living he offend us more. Then let him die before the Commons know, For fear that they do rise in Arms for him. Then do it suddenly my Lords. Let that be my lord cardinals charge and mine. Agreed, for he's already kept within my house. How now sirrah, what news? Madame, I bring you news from Ireland, The wild Only my lord, is up in Arms, With troops of Irish Kerns that uncontrolled, Do plant themselves within the English pale, And burn and spoil the Country as they go. What redress shall we have for this my Lords? 'twere very good, that my Lord of Somerset That fortunate champion were sent over. To keep in awe the stubborn Irishmen, He did so much good when he was in France. Had York been there with all his far fetched Policies he might have lost as much as I. Yea, for York would have lost his life, before That should have revolted from England's rule. Yea, so thou mightst, and yet have governed worse than I. What worse than nought, than a shame take all. Shame on thyself, that wisheth shame.
