I give your Mistress? Didst not thou decree Contrary t'our compact, against my marriage? Why when was I your judge? just now, here. See your error! Then was I fast locked in Antonio's Cellar: Where making virtue of necessity, I drunk stark drunk; and waking, found myself Clothed in this farmer's suit, as in the morning. Didst not thou swear t'enter Antonio's house, And give me Flavia for my wife? and after, Before my own face, gav'st her to my son? Ha, ha, ha! Canst thou deny't? Ha, ha, ha! Have you got mistress Patience? ha, ha, ha! Is not this true? Ha, ha! Answer me. Ha, ha, ha wan! Was't not thus? I answer. First, I never was transformed, But guled, as you were, by th'ginger And those that called m'Antonio. To prove this true, The gentleman you spoke with, was Antonio, The right Antonio, safely returned from Barbarie. Oi me! what's this? Truth itself. Was't not thou That gav'st the sentence? Believe me no such matter: I near was gentleman, nor otherwise than what I am, unless 'twere when I was drunk. How have I been deceived! good Trincalo Pardon me. I have wronged thee. Pardon you? When you have beaten me to paste, good Trincalo Pardon me? I am sorry for't; excuse me. I am sorry I must excuse you. But I pardon you. Now tell me where's the plate and cloth of silver, The gold and jewels that th'ginger Committed to thy keeping? What Plate, what jewels? He gave me none. But when he went to change me, After a thousand circles and ceremonies, He binds me fast upon a form, and blinds me With a thick Table-napkin. Not long after Unbinds my head and feet, and gives me light: And then I plainly saw, that I saw nothing; The parlour was clean swept of all was in't. Ojh me? oi me! What ails you? Sir, what ails you? I am undone, I have lost my Love, my plate, My whole estate, and with the rest myself. Lose not your patience too. Leave this lamenting, And lay the Town; you may recover it. 'Tis to small purpose. In, and hold thy peace. WHere shall I find my Master to content him With welcome news? he's here; news! news! News of good fortune, joy and happiness! Cricca, my sadness is uncapable Of better tidings: I am undone most miserable! Offend not your good luck, you're now more fortunate Than when you rose this morning: be merry, sir, Cheer up yourself, youhave what you wished, fear nothing. May be Antonio newly repents himself, With purpose to restore my Flavia. Cricca, what is't? where's all this happiness? Locked in Antonio's Closet. All alone? Sure that's my Flavia. Is not Eugenio Suffered to enter? Antonio keeps the key: No creature enters but himself. all's safe And shall be so restored. O my sweet Cricca! And they that wronged you, most extremely sorry, Ready to yield you any satisfaction.
