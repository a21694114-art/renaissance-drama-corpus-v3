Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Broke you for this? Was it not cause too much? Send to inquire his state, most part of it, Lay two years mortgaged in his uncles hands: Why say it did, you might have known my mind; I could have soon restored it. I had I but seen any such thing performed why 'twould have tied my affection and contained me in my first desires, do you think i'faith, that I could twine such a dry, oak as this, had promise in your Nephew took effect: Why and there's no time past, and rather than my adversary should thus thwart my hopes, I would— But you'ave been ever full of golden speech, If words were lands, your Nephew would be rich. Widow, believe it I vow by my best bliss, Before these Gentlemen I will give in The mortgage to my Nephew instantly, Before I sleep or eat. we'll pawn our credit's widow, what he speaks shall be performed in fullness. Nay more I will estate him In farther blessings, he shall be my heir, I have no Son, I'll bind myself to that condition. When I shall hear this done, I shall soon yield, to reasonable terms. In the mean season, Will you protest before these Gentlemen, To keep yourself, as you are, now at this present. I do protest before these Gentlemen, I will be as clear then, as I am now. I do believe you, here's your own honest servant, I'll take him along with me. ay, with all my heart. He shall see all performed and bring you word. That's all I wait for. What have you finished Master Lucre? ha, ha, ha, ha! So, laugh Hoord, laugh at your poor enemy, do, the wind may turn you may be laughed at too, yes marry may you sir— ha, ha, Ha, ha ha, if every man that swells in malice, Could be revenged as happily as I: He would choose hate, and forswear amity. What did he say wife, prithee? Faith spoke to ease his mind,— Oh— o— o— You know now, little to any purpose. True, true, true. He would do mountains now. ay, ay, ay, I. Y'ave struck him dead Master Hoord. I and his Nephew desperate: I know't sirs I, Never did man so crush his enemy? My son in law, Same Freedom? where's my Nephew? O man in lamentation father? How! He thumps his breast like a gallant Dicer that has lost his doublet, and stands in's shirt to do penance: Alas poor gentleman. I warrant, you may hear him sigh in a still evening to your house at Highgate. I prithee send him in. Were it to do a greater matter, I will not stick with you sir, in regard you married my Mother? Sweet Gentlemen cheer him up, I will but fetch the mortgage, and return to you instantly. we'll do our best sir?— see where he comes, E'en joyless and regardless of all form.
