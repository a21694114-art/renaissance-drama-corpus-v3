Sir Vlrico, I humbly crave to know, What good success: my honest suit ensues? Master Pimos, in breete, the same to show, I fear, you both, my order will refuse: Lyros, that thinks he gives more than he should, And you, for that, you have not, what you would, It shall go hard, if that your award mistikes me. Well, go with me, and you the same shall see: I weight on you. My troubled heart with guiltiness aggrieved, Like fire doth make my ears and cheeks to glow: God Grant I scape this black day unreprieved, I care not how the game go to morrow. Well, I will set a face of brass on it, And with the rest, upon the King attend: Who even anon will hear in judgement sit, To heaven or hell some officers to sand. But soft, a prize, Gripax and Rapax I see, A share of their venture belongs to me. Nay, good honest Promoters let me go. Tush Iohn Adroines, we must not leave you so: What? an old hobclunch a wanton knave? You shall to the King. Mary Iohn Adroynes God save: The king? why he will not look of poor men. Yes, yes, and will spy a knave in your face. Will he so? then, good you he gone apace. And why? Lest in my face, he spy you too, Have you seen a daw, bebob two crows so? Well, come away sir patch. Leave, or by God I'll scratch. What wilt thou so? Yea, and bite too. Help Rapax, play the man. Nay, do both what you can. If that in bobs, their bargain be, In faith they share alone for me. What bytest thou hobclunch, Yea, that chull, and punch. O Lord God, my heart. Knaves, i'll make you fart. Hold thy hands Job, first, take this bob. To part this fray, it is high time, I can tell, My Promoters else of the roast will smell. O, my neck thou wilt break. Yea, Gods ames, cryst thou creak? How now my friends? why what a stur is this? Mary. What? Care they part, I'll make them pys. Hold, no more blows. Knaves, this honest man thank, That you scape so well. Friend be not to crank, I am an officer, and mean to know The cause, why you brauld thus, before I go: Your bobs show, that the same, you best can tell. I would your worship, felt the same as well, I then am sure, this blockhedded slave, For both his faults, double punishment should have. What faults? Mary, He will lie like a dog. How now you churl, your tongue, would have a clog, Say on: To show his first, and chiefest faughte: His Father's maid, and he are nought. What I? I By my Grandsire's soul, you lie. Peace: Friend, for this fault, thou must die.
