Thou art Endymion, then no more, heigho for him I die. What ails the Centre of my happiness, Whereon depends the heaven of my delight? Thine eyes the motors to command my world, Thy hands the axier to maintain my world. Thy smiles, the prime and springtide of my world. Thy frowns, the winter to afflict the world. Thou Queen of me, I King of all the world. Ah feeble eyes lift up and look on him, Is Rasni here? then droop no more poor heart, Oh how I fainted when I wanted thee? How feign am I, now I may look on thee? How glorious is my Rasni? how divine? Eunuches play hymns, to praise his deity: He is my Ioue, and I his Iuno am. Sunbright, as is the eye of summers day, When as he suits Spenori all in gold, To woo his Leda in a swan-like shape. Seemly as Galbocia for thy white: Rose-coloured, lily, lovely, wanton, kind, Be thou the labyrinth to tangle love, Whilst I command the crown from Venus' crest: And pull Onoris girdle from his loins, Enchased with Carbunckles and Diamonds, To beautify fair Aluida my love. Play Eunuches, sing in honour of her name, Yet look not slaves upon her wooing eyen, For she is fair Lucina to your king, But fierce Medusa to your base eye. What if I slept, where should my pillow be? Within my bosom Nymph, not on my knee. Sleep like the smiling purity of heaven, When mildest wind is loath to blend the peace, Meanwhile thy balm shall from thy breath arise, And while these closures of thy lamps be shut, My soul may have his peace from fancy's war. This is my Morane, and I her Cephalus. Wake not too soon sweet Nymph, my love is won: Catnies why stay your strains, why tempt you me? All hail unto Th'assirian deity. Priests why presume you to disturb my peace? Rasni, the destinies disturb thy peace. Behold amidst the addittes of our Gods, Our mighty Gods the patrons of our war. The ghost of dead men howling walk about, Crying We, We, woe to this City woe. The statutes of our Gods are thrown down, And streams of blood our altars do distain. Alas my Lord what tidings do I hear? Shall I be slain? Who tempteth Aluida? Go break me up the brazen doors of dreams, And bind me cursed Morpheus in a chain, And fetter all the fancies of the night, Because they do disturb my Aluida. Behold dread Prince, a burning sword from heaven, Which by a threatening armeiss brandished. What am I threatened then amidst my throne? Sages? you Magi? speak: what meaneth this? These are but clammy exhalations, Or retro grave, conjunctions of the stars, Or oppositions of the greater lights. Or radiatrous finding matter fit, That in the starry Sphere kindled be, Matters betokening dangers to thy foes, But peace and honour to my Lord the King.
