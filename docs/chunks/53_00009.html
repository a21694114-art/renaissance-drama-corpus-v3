Sir my name is mirth Beloved with lords and ladies of birth At every triumph I am them with They can me not once forbear And ye had sought this thousand year Such another ye shall not find wherefore I counsel you in my mind Let him dwell with you for one yer. At your request I am content Such a prete man for me were expedient And of his counsel fain would I here. Sir ye must sing and dance & make good cheer I would ye had some proper wench That were young and lusty at a pinch Here hele were not so broad as an inch She would quicken your courage Peace hath forbidden me the outrage He would set you at dotage Because he is old and nature is passed He would now ever man should fast if ye do so ye do but waste And unto you no meed A straw for him, ye have no need Of him to stand in awe or dread A merrier life now may ye lead Therefore be at your own liberty By my truth I may say to thee site I to him did assent Had I never merry day But lived in fear and dread alway Nothing to mine intent Another while I will me sport Sing and dance to my comfort. And among merry company do resort For that shall length your life Spare neither maid ne wife Take both and they come in your way Of with this lewd array it becometh you nought by this day By my troth even as you say ye marry now am I well apaid Me thinketh I am properly arrayed if I had a proper trul she should be asaid In the worship of the new year Rush up mutton for beef is dear Have and revel and chance: Now let us both sing and dance Will ye have a french round And thou shalt see me bounce above the ground hay with revel dash What prosperity is it come hereto What devil of hell hast thou to do Shall I not make merry when me list. yet I say beware of had I wist Hens ye knave or else thou shalt lick my fist I trow thy head would have some knocks Go set him in a pair of stocks That I him no more see. yet man I say remember thee And think what I to the have said. eschew evermore these ryatours' company And be ruled by reason as I thee bad Put from the these two parsons by whom thou art led Envy & misrule with their sinful & great abusion Which if thou wilt not forsake, will be thy confuison Avaunt lorden, and take this for a canclusion These men fro me thou shalt not separate Go out of my sight or by coxs passion I shall lay the fast in Newgate It is better to forsake them betime then to late This knave would have a broken pate
