Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Right well have ye said and we accept it so And so shall we reward you or we go hens But ye must take paciens till we have hard more That we may indifferently give sentens There may pass by us no spot of negligence But justly to judge each thing so upright That each man's part may shine in the self right Now sir by your faith if ye should be sworn Hard ye ever god speak so sins ye were borne So wise to gelitelly his words be showed. I thank his grace my sewt is well bestod. Sir what voyage intend ye next to go. I trust or inydlent to be at Syo. Ha ha is it your mind to sail at Syo Nay then when ye will, by lady ye may go And let me alone with this, be of good cheer Ye may trust me at Syo as well as here For though ye were fro me a thousand mile space I would do as much as ye were here in place For sins that from hens it is so far thither I care not though ye never come again hither. Sir if ye remember me when time shall come Though I requite not all, I shall deserve some. Now far you well & god thank you by saint Anne I pray you mark the fashion of this honest man He putteth me in more trust at this meeting here Then he shall find cause why this twenty year. God be here, now Chryst keep this company. In faith ye be welcome even very scantly Sir for your coming what is the matter. I would fain speak with the god Iupyter. That will not be but ye may do this Tell me your mind I am an office of his B ye so, marry I cry you mercy Your maystershype may say I am homely But sins your mind is to have reported The cause wherefore I am now resorted Pleaseth it your maystershype it is so I come for myself and such other more Rangers and keepers of certain places As forests, parks, purlieus and chases Where we be charged with all manner game Small is our profit and great is our blame Alas for our wages what be we the near What is forty shillings or five mark a year Many times and oft where we be fletting We spend forty pens a piece at a sitting Now for our vantage which chiefly is wyndefale That is right nought there bloweth no wind at all Which is the thing wherein we find most grief And cause of my coming to sew for relief That the god of pity all this thing knownuge May send us good rage of blustering and blowing And if we can not get god to do some good I would higher the devil to run thorough the wood The roots to turn up, the tops to bring under A mischief upon them and a wild thunder.
