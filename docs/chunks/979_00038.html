How? By Geometry? Yes sir, here I hold My Rapier, mark me, in a diameter To my body; that's the centre, conceive me. Your body is the centre, very good. And my hilt, part of the circumference. Well sir. Which hilt is bigger than my body. Then your whole body? Yes at such a distance. And he shall never hit me, whilst he lives. Where did you learn this? At the leager? No. No by this light: it is my own invention. I learned it in my travels. Very strange: You are a scholar. No: I would not be suspected of such a crime for a Million. But 'tis no sin to know Geometry; And by that, I can tell we shall ne'er fight. Not fight at all? I'll show you in Geometry, Two parallels can never meet: now we two Being parallels, for so we are, that is Equal in wit and valour, can never meet. And if we never meet, we shall ne'er fight. To prove your axiom false, see where he comes. I do defy him. Hang thee blustering son Of Aeolus, defy me! I'll tie up thy breath In bags, and sell it for a penny an ounce. Draw sir. Draw if he dares. Sure, this is the second part of the leaguer. 'twere best for me, to hide me in my cabin. will you resign your mistress? No, I scorn it. Unless you'll have her ta'en away by force. I see, this cannot be ended without blood. Captain, a word with you. What say you sir? I am afraid he comes with the black art. How you afraid? do not say so for shame. He has lain with an old witch at Sweden. And is grown stick free. Fie that you shall say so. I'll be resolved of that before I fight. Why, do you think that witches have such power? I marry do I, I have known one of them, Do more than that, when her husband has followed Strange women, she has turned him into a Bezer, And made him bite out his own stones. 'tis strange! I'll tell you another as strange as that, of one When a Vintner has sent her but ill wine, She has converted him into a Frog. And then conjured him into one of his butts, Where he has lived twelve months upon the lees, And when his old guests chance to come to see him, He has croaked to them, out at the bunghole. This is miraculous. There was a Lawyer That spoke against one of them at the bar. What did she then? Turned him into a Ram, And still that Ram retains his profession, Has many Clients, and pleads causes as well As some Lawyers in Westminster. Do you think, That he has had recourse to any such? I know not, but 'tis good to be mistrustful. He may have advantage in the encounter. There she comes, win her, and wear her.
