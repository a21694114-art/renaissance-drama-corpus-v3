Olinda, know'st not yet the treachery Of Cosma, she thy greatest enemy? Prithee let me see't: shouldst thou this liquour prove, I tell thee, friend, 'twill quench thy life and love. But so I'll temper't, it shall better please thee, And after few spent hours shall ever ease thee. 'tis beyond art, who there can give relief. Where patients hate the cure, more than the grief? Yes, by my art, before thouart 12 hours older, I'll ease thy heart, though never make it colder. Glaucilla and Olinda? I mar'le what mettle What leaden earth and water nature put Into these Nymphs, as cold, as dull, as frozen As the hard rocks they dwell on! But my Mistress she's all quicksilver, never still, still moving, Now is she with some shepherd or some fisher, And here she sets me to entertain all comers: This is the hour her lover's use to muster. But who should this be? is't you, old boy? Old ten i'th' hundred, are you the captain? boo, Beshrew your heart, you are a very naughty boy, I shake every joint of me. No shaking palsy, nor cramp has ta'en possession Of your nimble limbs ha, ha, he. Boy, where's thy Mistress? Where she would be. Where's that? Where you would be, What, in her bed? Ah old goat, do I smell you? yet in her bed? May not I speak a word or two with her what a fool 'tis? thou hast spoken twice a already I But I would speak them in her ear. I know your errand but I prithee tell me Fredocaldo How is't possible that all the bellows in love's father's shop Should kindle any fire in such a frost? thou know'st not what is love, I tell thee boy I love fair Cosma more than all her lovers. Now in my conscience he says true, this old wood Makes a brighter fire than the greenest ever: Conchylio thouart deceived, hast not seen That of thee May the lust of all the year Nipped with the hoary frost grows cold and chare? And oft October though the years declining With many dainty flowers is fairly shining For as the flaming sun puts out the fire So may the heat of love quench loves desires. Can this dotard do as well as speak, he might— I tell thee boy, when I was young— That was at the siege of Troy Now shall we have, more tales than ever poets made But what will you give me Fredocaldo If I help thee in the rocky cave, near to the myrtle grove To speak with Cosma all alone. If thou'lt do it, I'll give thee as fair an otter tamed for fishing as ever was in Sicily. Your hand on that: Ah old Saturn cold and dry we'll I'll do't But when Conchylio when? Within this hour expect her. Wilt thou be sure. Why did I ever deceive you? never never: believe me Fredocaldo I say believe me then. Farewell; I'll keep my promise.
