That reason is so great no man can debar Nevertheless that churllysh knave that carter After his fond opinion thinketh thus Himself more gentleman than us And therein he lieth for by expiens we see That gentle conditions most comenly be In them that be of noble blood borne For take twenty carters which never were before acquainted let them be to guider Take twenty straying gentlemen in like manner These churlish carters I dare well say will not agree to guider scant one day wt out chydyng quarrellyng or fyghtyng ychone will steal from other and be pyking And strife which of them at the skot shall pay lest And endeavour them who can play the knave best But these gentlemen I warrant you will study who can show to other most courtesy And of their gentleness will pfer to pay For the other and show what pleasures they may So touching gentleness I say surely Men of great birth use it most comynly There can be no truer saying nor sentence And the cause thereof we see by expience For these poor wretches that have no thing Must be nygardies churlyth and spayring But gentlemen be taught to be liberal And so they may be for they have where with all And as touching nobleness that argument which the ploughman made late puyth evident That gentlemen borne to land must needs be For suffyency of most nobility For beside God's gifts of grace & of nature As wit and bodily stryngh yet they be sure Of other riches as of land and rent To avoid need so they be more sufficient of themself than other poor people doubtless Then if need of strange help cause wretchedness And suffysauns be cause wretchedness A better reason no man can devise And yet farther I think likewise He that hath great abundance of riches May use liberality and gentleness And also it is ever necessary That some life in wealth and some in misery And let churllies babble and say what they will Hit hath been so ever and will be so still For it is almighty God's purveyance wise men of fools to have the governance And they that rule well I beseech Ihesu Send them good life & long to continue ye soferayns all discrete & excellent Before whom this dialog showed hath be Touching three points by weigh of argument Furst what is gentleness & what nobility And who should be chose to high authority This questions they be so high and sottell Few dare pnsume to define them well Yet I think now under your corrections The thing that maketh a gentleman to be is but virtue & gentle conditions which as well in poor men oft times we see As in men of great birth of high degree
