Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

I will indeed forsooth. And as for my Child, I hope she'll be ruled in Time, though she be foolish yet & not be carried away with a cast of Manchets, a Bottle of wine, or a Custard, and so I pray certify him. He do your errant effectually. Art thou his aunt— or his— Alas— I am a poor drudge of his. Faith and thou wert his Mother, he would make thee his drudge I warrant him. Marry out upon him, sir reverence of your mistress-ship here's somewhat for thy pains, fare thee well. 'tis more than he gave me since I came to him. How now, what prating have we hear? whispers, dumbshows? why Tomazin, go too— my shop is not altogether so dark as some of my neighbours, where a man may be made Cuckold at one end, while he's measuring with his yard at tother. Only commendations sent from Master Lethe your worshipful Son in law that should be. Oh, & that you like not he that can make us rich in custom, strong in friends, happy in suits, bring us into all the rooms a sundaies, from the leads to the seller, pop us in with Venison till we crack again, & send home the rest in an honourable Napkin— this man you like not forsooth? But I like him father. My blessing go with thy liking A number of our Citizens hold our credit by't to come home drunk, and say we ha' been at Court: then how much more credit is't to be drunk there indeed? Tut, thy Mother's a fool— pray what's Master Rerage whom you plead for so? Why, first he is a Gentleman. ay, he's often first a Gentleman that's last a beggar. My father tells you true, what should I do with a gentleman, I know not which way to lie with him. 'tis true too— thou know'st beside, we undo Gentlemen daily That makes so few of 'em marry with our Daughters, unless it be one green foot or other: next, M. Rerage has land & living, other but his walk i'th' street, & his snatching diet, he's able to entertain you in a fair house of his own, other in some nook or corner, or place us behind the cloth like a company of Puppets: at his house you shall be served curiously, sit down & eat your meat with leisure, there we must be glad to take it standing, & without either salt, cloth, or trencher, and say we are befriended too. Oh, that gives a Citizen a better appetite than his Garden. So say I Father, methinks it does me most good when I take it standing, I know not how all women's minds are: Faith I think they are all of thy mind for that thing, how now Falslight? I have descried my fellow Shortyard, alias Blastfield, at hand with the Gentleman.
