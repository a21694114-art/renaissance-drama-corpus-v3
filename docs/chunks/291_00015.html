Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

God ye good-morrow Gentlemen. God ye good den fair Gentlewomen. Is it god ye gooden I pray you. 'tis no less I assure you, for the bawdy hand of the dial is even now upon the prick of noon. Fie, what a man is this? A Gentleman Nurse, that God hath made for himself to mar. By my troth well said: for himself to mar quoth he? I pray you can any of you tell where one may find young Romeo? I can: but young Romeo will be elder when you have found him, that he was when you sought him. I am the youngest of that name for fault of a worse. Well said. Yea, is the worst well? mas well noted, wisely, wisely. If you be he sir, I desire some conference with ye. O, belike she means to invite him to supper. So ho. A bawd, a bawd, a bawd. Why what hast found man? No hare sir, unless it be a hare in a lenten pie, that is somewhat stolen and hoar ere it be eaten. And an old hare hore, and an old hare hore is very good meat in Lent: But a hare that's hoar is too much for a score, if it hore ere it be spent. You! come to your fathers to supper? I will. Farewell ancient Lady, farewell sweet Lady. Marry farewell. Pray what saucy merchant was this that was so full of his roperipe? A gentleman Nurse that loves to hear himself talk, and will speak more in an hour than he will stand to in a month. If he stand to any thing against me, Ile take him down if he were lustier than he is: if I cannot take him down, I'll find them that shall: I am none of his flurtgills, I am none of his skeins mates. And thou like a knave must stand by, and see every Iacke use me at his pleasure. I see no body use you at his pleasure, if I had, I would soon have drawn: you know my tool is as soon out as another's if I see time and place. Now afore God he hath so vexed me, that every member about me quivers: scurvy Iacke. But as I said, my Lady bade me seek ye out, and what she bade me tell ye, that I'll keep to myself: but if you should lead her into a fools paradise as they say, it were a very gross kind of behaviour as they say, for the Gentlewoman is young. Now if you should deal doubly with her, it were very weak dealing, and not to be offered to any Gentlewoman. Nurse, commend me to thy Lady, tell her I protest. Good heart: i'faith I'll tell her so: oh she will be a joyful woman. Why, what wilt thou tell her? That you do protest: which (as I take it) is a Gentleman like proffer.

God ye goodmorrow Gentlemen. God ye good den faire Gentlewomen. Is it god ye gooden I pray you. Tis no lesse I assure you, for the baudie hand of the diall is euen now vpon the pricke of noone. Fie, what a man is this? A Gentleman Nurse, that God hath made for himselfe to mare. By my troth well said: for himselfe to marre quoth he? I pray you can anie of you tell where one maie finde young Romeo? I can: but yong Romeo will bee elder when you haue found him, that he was when you sought him. I am the yongest of that name for fault of a worse. Well said. Yea, is the worst well? mas well noted, wisely, wisely. If you be he sir, I desire some conference with ye. O, belike she meanes to inuite him to supper. So ho. A baud, a baud, a baud. Why what hast found man? No hare sir, vnlesse it be a hare in a lenten pye, that is somewhat stale and hoare ere it be eaten. And an olde hare hore, and an olde hare hore is verie good meate in Lent: But a hare thats hoare is too much for a score, if it hore ere it be spent. You! come to your fathers to supper? I will. Farewell ancient Ladie, farewell sweete Ladie. Marry farewell. Pray what saucie merchant was this that was so full of his roperipe? A gntleman Nurse that loues to are himselfe talke, and will speake more in an houre than hee will stand to in a month. If hee stand to anie thing against mee, Ie take him downe if he were lustier than he is: if I cannot take him downe, Ile finde them that shall: I am none of his lurtgills, I am none of his skaines mates. And thou like a knaue must stand by, and see euerie Iacke vse me at his pleasure. I see no bodie vse you at his pleasure, if I had, I would soone haue drawen: you know my toole is as soone out as anothers if I see time and place. Now afore God he hath so vext me, that euerie member about me quiuers: scuruie Iacke. But as I said, my Ladie bad me seeke ye out, and what shee bad me tell yee, that Ile keepe to my selfe: but if you should lead her into a fooles paradice as they saye, it were a vere grosse kinde of behauiour as they say, for the Gentlewoman is yong. Now if you should deale doubly with her, it were verie weake dealing, and not to be offered to anie Gentlewoman. Nurse, commend me to thy Ladie, tell her I protest. Good heart: yfaith Ile tell her so: oh she will be a ioyfull woman. Why, what wilt thou tell her? That you doo protest: which (as I take it) is a Gentleman like proffer.
