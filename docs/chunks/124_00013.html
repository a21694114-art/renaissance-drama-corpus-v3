He is a crafty child, dally, but do not. Tush, I warrant thee, I am not so hot, Your words are too hard Sir, for me to construe. Then to be short, your rare beauty my heart hath wounded so, As (save your love, become my leech) I sure shall die with woe. I see no sign of death, in your face to appear, 'tis but some usual qualm you have, pitiful Dames to fears. Fair Lamia, trust me I feign not, betimes bestow some grace. Well, I admit it so, only to argue in your case. I am married, so that to set your love on me were vain: It sufficeth me, that I may your secret friend remain. A holy Hood, makes not a Friar devout, He will play at small game, or he sit out. Though for pleasure, or to prove me, these proffers you do move, You are to wise, to hazard life, upon my yielding love: The man is pained with present death, that useth wanton pleasure. To scape such pain, wise men, these joys, without suspect can measure, Furthermore, I have been (my Girl) a Lawyer to too long: If at a pinch, I cannot wrist the Law from right to wrong. If law you do profess. I gladly crave, In a cause or two, your advise to have. To resolve you, you shall command my skill, Wherefore like friends, let's common in good will, You are a merry man, but leave to jest, To morrow night, if you will be my Jest: At my poor house, you shall my causes know, For good cause, which I mean not here to show. Willingly, and for that, haste calls me hence, My suit till then, shall remain in suspense: Farewell Client, to morrow look for me: Your good welcome Sir, your best cheer will be. I told you erst, the nature of Phallax, Money, or fair Women, works him as wax: And yet I must commend your sober cheer, You told your tale, as if a Saint you were. Well (in seeréete, be it said) how so I seemed divine, I feared once, a blue gown, would have been my shrine. But now that pain is flayed, and pleasure keeps his hold, I know that Phallax will, my Fame henceforth uphold: To entertain which Jest, I will some dainty cheer prepare, Yet ere I go, in pleasant Song, I mean to purge my care. A due poor care, adieu, Go, cloy some helpless wretch: My life, to make me rue, Thy forces do not stretch. Thy harbour, is the heart, Whom wrong, hath wrapped, in woe: But wrong, doth take my part, With cloak of right in shoe. My faults, inquiry escape, At them the judges wink: Those for my fall that gape, To show my lewdness shrink. Then silly care go pack, Thou art no Jest for me: I have, and have, no lack, And lack, is shroud for thee.
