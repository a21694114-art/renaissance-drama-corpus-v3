will you but be so gracious to me sir? I shall be bound. You shall sir, To your bunch again, or I'll miss foully. Save you gent. save you. Now Polecat, what young rabbits nest have you to draw? Come, pray thee be familiar Knight. Away Fox, I'll send for Terriers for you. Thou art wide yet: I'll keep thee company. I am about some business; Indentures, If ye follow me I'll beat you: take heed, As I live I'll cancel your Coxcomb. Thou art cozened now, I am no usurer: What poor fellow's this? I am poor indeed sir. Give him money Knight. Do you begin the offering. There poor fellow, here's an angel for thee. Art thou in earnest Moorcraft? Yes faith Knight. I'll follow thy example: thou hadst land and thousands, thou spendst, and flungst away, and yet it flows in double: I purchased, wrung, and wierdraw'd for my wealth, lost, and was cozened: for which I make a vow, to try all the ways above ground. but I'll find a constant means to riches without curses. I am glad of your conversion Mr. Moorcraft: you're in a fair course, pray pursue it still. Come, we are all gallants now, I'll keep thee company; Here honest fellow, for this gentleman's sake, there's two angels more for thee. God quit you sir, and keep you long in this mind. Wilt thou persever? Till I have a penny. I have brave clothes a making, and two horses; canst thou not help me to a Match Knight, I'll lay a a thousand pound upon my crop-ear. Foot this is stranger than an Afrique monster, There will be no more talk of the Cleave wars Whilst this lasts, come, I'll put thee into blood. Would all his damned tribe were as tender hearted. I beseech you let this gent. join with you in the recovery of my Keys; I like his good beginning sir, the whilst I'll pray for both your worships. He shall sir. Shall we go noble Knight? I would fain be acquainted. I'll be your servant sir. Faith my sweet Lady, I have caught you now, maugre your subtleties, and fine devices, be coy again now. prithee sweetheart tell true. By this light, by all the pleasures I have had this night, by your lost maidenhead, you are cozened merely. I have cast beyond your wit. That gent. is your retainer Welford. It cannot be so. Your sister has found it so, or I mistake: mark how she blushes when you see her next. Ha, ha, ha, I shall not travel now, ha, ha, ha. Prithee sweet heart be quiet, thou hast angered me at heart. I'll please you soon again. Welford. I Welford, he's a young handsome fellow, well bred and landed: your sister can instruct you in his good parts better than I by this time. Uds'foot, am I fetched over thus? Yes i'faith. And over shall be fetched again, never fear it.
