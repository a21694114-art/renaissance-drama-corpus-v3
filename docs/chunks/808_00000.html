Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

BEssus, the KING has made a fair hand on't, has ended the wars at a blow, would my sword had a close basket hilt to hold wine, and the blade would make knives, for we shall have nothing but eating and drinking. We that are commanders shall do well enough. Faith Bes. such commanders as thou may, I had as live set thee Perdue for a pudding i'h dark, as Alexander the great. I love these jests exceedingly. I think thou lov'st them better than quarreling Bessus, He say so much i'thy behalf, and yet thou art valiant enough upon a retreat, I think thou wouldst kill any man that stopped thee, and thou couldst. But was not this a brave combat Mardonius? Why, didst thou see't? You stood with me. I did so, but methought thou wink'st every blow they struck. Well, I believe there are better Soldiers than I, that never saw two Princes fight in lists. By my troth I think so too Bessus, many a thousand, but certainly all that are worse than thou have seen as much. 'twas bravely done of our King. Yes, if he had not ended the wars, I am glad thou darest talk of such dangerous businesses. To take a Prince prisoner in the heart of his own Country in single combat. See how thy blood cruddles at this, I think thou wouldst be contented to be beaten in this passion. Shall I tell you truly. I. I could willingly venture for it. umh, no venture neither good Bessus. Let me not live, if I do not think it is a braver piece of service, then that I'm so famed for. Why art thou famed for any valour? I famed, ay, I warrant you. I am very heartily glad on't. I have been with thee ever since thou cam'st ath' wars, and this is the first word that ever I heard on't, prithee who fames thee? The Christian world. 'tis heathenishly done of them, in my conscience thou deserv'st it not. Yes, I ha' done good service. I do not know how thou mayst wait of a man in's Chamber, or thy agility in shifting a trencher, but otherwise no service good Bessus. You saw me do the service yourself. Not so hasty sweet Bessus, where was it, is the place vanished. At Bessus desperate redemption. Bessus desperate redemption, where's that. There where I redeemed the day, the place bears my name. Prithee who christened it. The Soldier: If I were not a very merely disposed man, what would become of thee: one that had but a grain of collar in the whole composition of his body would send thee of an errand to the worms, for putting thy name upon that field: did not I beat thee there i'h head, a'th' troops with a truncheon, because thou wouldst needs run away with thy company, when we should charge the enemy.
