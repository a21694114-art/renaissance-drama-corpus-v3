Sir this is ynglande lying here And this is skotlande that joineth him near compassed a bout every where with the occian see round And next from them westwardly Here by himself alone doth lie Ireland that wholesome ground Here than is the narrow seey To Calyce and Boleyne the next weigh And flaunders in this ꝑte Here lieth fraunce next him joining And spayn southward from thence standing And portyngale in this quart This country is called Italye Behold where Rome in the myddis doth lie And Naples here be yond And this little See that here is Is called the Gulf of Uenys And here venys doth stand As for almayne lieth this way Here lieth denmarke and norway And northward on this side There lieth Iselonde where men do fish But be yond that so cold it is No man may there abide This See is called the great occian So great it is that never man Coude tell it sith the world began Till now within this twenty year westward be found new lands That we never hard tell of before this By writing nor other meanys yet many now have been there And that country is so large of room Much longer than all cristendome without fable or guile For diverse mariners have it tried And sailed straight by the cost side Above .v. thousand mile But what commodities be within No man can tell nor well Imagine But yet not long a go Some men of this country went By the king's noble consent It for to search to that intent And could not be brought thereto But they that were they venteres Have cause to curse their mariners Falls of promise and dissemblers That falsely them be trayed which would take no pain to sail farther Than their own list and pleasure wherefore that voyage and diverse other Such kaytyffes have destroyed O what a thing had be than if that they that be english men Might have been the furst of all That there should have take possession And made furst building & habitation A memory perpetual And also what an honourable thing Both to the realm and to the king To have had his dominion extendyng● There into so far a ground which the noble king of late memory The most wise prince the vij Herry Caused furst for to be found And what a great meritoryouse deed It were to have the people instructed To live more virtuously And to learn to know of men the manner And also to know god their maker which as yet live all bestly For they nother know god nor the devil Nor never hard tell of heaven nor hell writing nor other scripture But yet in the stead of god almight The honour the son for his great lygg● For that doth them great pleasure Building nor house they have none at all But woods/ cotꝭ/ and cavys small No marvel though it be so For they use no manner of iron Nother in toll nor other weapon That should help them thereto Copper they have which is found In diverse places above the ground
