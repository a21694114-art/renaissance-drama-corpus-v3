I must walk With the French stick, like an old verger for you, O, Chief, call me to Hell again, and free me. Do you murmur now? Not I, Sr. What do you take Mr. Devile, the height of your employment, In the true perfect Escudero? When? What do you answer? To be able, Madam, First to inquire, than report the working, Of any Lady's physic, in sweet phrase, Yes, that's an act of elegance, and importance. But what above? O, that I had a goad for him. To found out a good Corn-cutter. Out on him! Most barbarous! Why did you do this, now? Of purpose to discredit me? you damned Devil. Sure, if I be not yet, I shall be. All My days in Hell, were holidays to this! 'Tis labour lost, Madam? HE is a dull fellow Of no capacity! Of no discourse! O, if my Ambler had been here! I, Madam; You talk of a man, where is there such another? Mr. Devile, put case, one of my Ladies, here, Had a fine brach: and would employ you forth To treat 'bout a convenient match for her. What would you observe? The colour, and the size, Madam. And nothing else? The Moon, you calf, the Moon! I, and the Sign. Yes, and receipts for proneness. Than when the Puppies came, what would you do? Get their nativities cast! This's well. What more? Consult the Almanac-man which would be least? Which cleanliest? And which silentest? This's well, madam! And while she were with puppy? Walk her out, And air her every morning! Very good! And be industrious to kill her fleas? Yes! He will make a pretty proficient. Who, Coming from Hell, could look for such Catechising? The Devil is an Ass. I do acknowledge it. The top of woman! All her sex in abstract! I love her, to each syllable, falls from her. Good madam give me leave to go aside with him! And try him a little! Do, and I'll withdraw, Madam, With this fair Lady: read to her, the while. Come, Sr. Dear Chief, relieve me, or I perish. Lady, we'll follow. You are not jealous Sir? O, madame! you shall see. Stay wife, behold, I give her up here, absolutely, to you, She is your own. Do with her what you will! Melt, cast, and form her as you shall think good! Set any stamp on! I'll receive her from you As a new thing, by your own standard! Well, Sir! But what ha' you done i' your Dependence, since? O, it goes on, I met your Cousin, the Master— You did not acquaint him, Sr? Faith, but I did, Sr. And upon better thought, not without reason! He being chief Officer, might ha' ta'en it ill, else, As a Contempt against his Place, and that In time Sir, ha' drawn on another Dependence. Not, I did find him in good terms, and ready To do me any service.
