Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Revedg for me my Lord, nay that cannot be, unless a strange devission may be had, For I that am as nearly knit to Vallenger as body to the soul, cannot suppose an injury, But think his loss, To be my misery and chiefest cross. Hath he not sought thy blood thou foolish girl? He sought no more than I can freely give, and sacrifice to death so he may live. Did he not keep a harlot to despite thee. And if he mend that fault he soon may right me. He gave thy ornaments to deck his trull. And my consent did ratify his gift. Well he hates thee. To try my patience, all that he did or gave, Or did contrive conserneth me, his act was my allowance. Now since myself, accuse not, but excuse. Since she that was supposed to be wronged, Doth right that wrong, be just and set him free, For I protest I know no injury. She knows no injury, my Lord she lies, Sir God-frey since your daughter doth Remit his fault, methinks you should forgive him, Forgive him, no not I the wretch shall never boast, That he hath braved old God-frey in a just And honest cause. I'll touch him I, I and touch him to the quick, No less then for his neck verse will I touch him, Doctor stand forth and to my prince and peers, Say what thou knowst of Sentloes' bloody death Then this I bow before your Majesty, That Vallenger corrupted me by gold, to poison Anabel, and Sentloe to. Intents are nothing till they come to acts. and mistress is not Sentloe dead in act. Sentloe is dead, my masters bloody death, should quicken justice in your majesty. thou shalt have justice to thy own content. O how I grieve to see her brinish tears, Water the Crimson roses of her cheeks. Had not my wit prevented this before, Nor you nor she, had never sorrowed more. You come my son: what said I? No my shame: O let his blood my liege redeem the same. Young Vallenger thou art here, Accused for Anabell, And murdering Sentloe that is dead, Speak, art thou guilty. I or Noah. Guilty in both my Lord, and here for both Ready to pay the penallty of my life, though in my soul and conscience I am clear, Of Sentloes' death, yet welcome happy lot, That so shall rid my life of that foul spot. Alas poor soul, how grief and his disgrace, Doth make him desperate, behold his face. From thence speaks truth, as from an Orackle, That he is innocent, though his words accu e him. Well Vallenger attend thy sentence. Pronounce not sentence yet, right royal king. What lawful stay canst thou impart to us. A stay to straighten justice and the truth, My conscience charged with this heinous crime, Makes me confess though to my hearts true grief, That by thenticements of lewd Florence and her jousts, I should have murdered Sentlove, my dear master, I therefore think that through her wickedness She is the cause of all these villainies.
