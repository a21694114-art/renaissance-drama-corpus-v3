Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Wondrous sir; he can transshape his spirits, if fame Lie not, into what form he please, to imitate me, Or personate you; he could produce a Devil In shape of this good ancient Gentlewoman. A Devil sir like me? H'hath done't in habit of a Constable, And may do't in a gentlewoman's; for his commons In the Friars he cares not, he can for a need Conjure his bread from Spain, from France his wine, His diet from the utmost of the Earth, Thus hath his fame given out. Would we might see him to make proof of his cunning. And as I told thee Nan, be swayed by me, I'll make him thine, employ my industry, Counsel and pains, to wean him by degrees From his wild course of desperate Melancholy: I'll seek him out, persuade him, lay thy love As open as the midday: hope in me And doubt not to prevail. Sir, I am yours. Thou knowest he loves me; I was still his friend, And friend with friend what cannot he perform? Make thyself mine, I'll cross thy Mother's workings, And fool that Lords attempts; only be you of a more Temperate humour, and more stayed, observing but what I Shall project for you, and doubt not my performance. You in all things Have proved yourself a Father: I'll henceforth Strive to express a Daughter. Then enough. Here's now my Husband, he can speak it all. But ere I question that, of you, fair Mistress, Let me be first resolved; is your Brain yet In a more settled temper? Sir, it is; methinks I am new wakened from a Dream, in which I long have slumbering lost myself. And now art found. I am, thank Heaven, and my good Father's counsel and advice. I held it wife, a deed of charity, & did it for the Lords sake. Sir, henceforth I shall observe you better. You now have wreathed me with a Crown of hopes. Made me again thy Mother, and this work, Your ever patient and obedient wife. Marry Heaven continue it wife, 'tis but need; In conscience thou hast been froward long enough. These things so well succeeding, pray resolve us In what form doth this Devil haunt your house. A woman's sure. So I told you, sure your Mothers. Troth she was somewhat shrewish like my wife, But yet I think not hers. Your reason sir? This apparition was the full portrait of a young beauteous Lady. On my life Acadna then. Acadna? what's Acadna? A harmless Spirit fashioned from the Air, And yet assuming substance, shape and form, That where she loves, doth all the Offices Of a fair Lady: can supply with gold. And all things to man useful. So I have heard; but this cannot be she, For this had with her beauty so much terror, So much affright and horror in her looks, Such a confused noise, with hellish sounds Able to drive the senses retrograde; Turn reason into madness, and invert Capacity to fury; that, I vow I would not to be heir of all the World Endure like night again.
