Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

He might as well ha' called me Bethlem Gabor; Let's walk on still. Ha Borgia, 'tis he, my noble friend returned, Welcome, a thousand welcomes in thy bosom; Why do you wear this strangeness in your face, Can a few years render me lost to Borgia. Sure he does call me Borgia. Thou wert Unkind in thy departure, with what devotion Could I have been a partner in thy travel And have unequal fortune, being absent Why was not I summoned to wait upon thee In any part o'th' world, but again welcome, When arrived ye. I am very likesome body, They all mistake me thus, we ha' seen no friends since. Is this Gentleman. One dear in my acquaintance. I am your servant, thy unexpected sight Will glad Mercutio's heart, your noble father Who hath long mourned your absence. Good old man, He will rejoice to see him here no doubt on't. Remember my father's name. You have lost an Uncle, but all his estate Your Father is possessed of. Dead! His age Was fit for heaven, his wealth is added to Your fathers to make yours the greater fortune. Take it upon you. Pardon noble friend, I was persuaded time and absence had Wrought such a change upon my face, that no man Would know me again, but though I may to some Appear a stranger, I must stand discovered To a friend's penetrating eye, again Possessed your faithful servant, what's my name. Dear Borgia. Borgia I must remember you. Shall I wait on you to your father Sir. What were I best to do. Your sisters grown a gallant gentlewoman, The only beauty of Urbin, and waits on The Duchess, her soft lips will seal your welcome. A handsome gentlewoman, go. He does but jest, they are all witches here. No more jeers. Go or be lame for ever. Is she grown Worthy that hour you express, and has The Duchess taken her to grace. 'tis more than whispered, The favourite loves her. Who. He that was cause Of your remove Ursini. Cry you mercy, I know him well enough; but does he love her? 'tis rumoured so i'th' court. You will be tripping, Your own heels up. I hope my sister's honest. Who Cornelia. Remember that name too. Let me alone To remember her, Cornelia for more Security, I'll put her in my table book. There is no Lady in the preserves A clearer fame, as modest as she's fair, And so ingenious. I would excuse Her modesty, but she may be converted In time; though sturdy Oak we know, and so forth You speak a welcome Character. Your father It seems his willing ear hath catched the news Of your arrive. Kneel down. See if he first knows me, 'tis a wise father now knows his own child. My dearest Borgia, comfort of my age. My joy of soul, a father's prayers and blessing Make thee a happy man, my eyes must speak Part of my joy in tears, welcome from Naples.
