You heard he with the bottles say, he thought we should come too late, what abundance of people here is. But what had he in those bottles? I know not. Why, Ink good man fool: Ink, what to do? Why, the King look you, will many times call for those bottles, and break his mind to his friends. Let's take our places quickly, we shall have no room else. The man told us he would walk a foot through the people. I marry did he. Our shops are well look to now: S'life yonder's my Master I think. No, 'tis not he. Lord, how fine the fields be, what sweet living 'tis in the Country. ay, poor souls, God help 'em; they live as contentedly as one of us. My husband's cousen would have had me gone into the Country last year, wert thou ever there? ay, poor souls, I was amongst 'em once. And what kind of creatures are they for love of God? Very good people, God help 'em: Wilt thou go with me down this summer, when I am brought a-bed? Alas, 'tis no place for us. Why prithee? Why, you can have nothing there; there's nobody cries brooms. No? No truly, nor milk. Nor milk, how do they? They are feign to milk themselves i'th' Country. Good Lord: but the people there I think will be very dutiful to one of us? ay, God knows will they, and yet they do not greatly care for our Husbands. Do they not, alas? In good faith I cannot blame them: for we do not greatly care for them ourselves. Philip I pray choose us a place. There's the best forsooth. By your leave good people a little: What's the matter? I pray my friend do not thrust my Mistress so, she's with child. Let her look to herself then, has she not had thrusting enough yet; if she stay shouldering here, she may hap to go home with a Cake in her belly. How now goodman squitterbreech, why do you lean so on me? Because I will. Will you sir saucebox? Look if one have not struck Philip, come hither Philip, why did he strike thee? For leaning on him. Why didst thou lean on him? I did not think he would have struck me. As God save me la, thou art as wild as a Buck, there is no quarrel, but thou art at one end or other of it. It's at the first end then; for he will never stay the last. Well stripling, I shall meet with you. when you will. I'll give a crown to meet with you: At a bawdy house. ay, you are full of your roguery; but if I do meet you it shall cost me a fall. The King, the King, the King, the King: Now, now, now, now. God preserve your Majesty.
