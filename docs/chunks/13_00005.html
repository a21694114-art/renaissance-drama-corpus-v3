Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Marry no man can make a better reason For that is a sure & a true conclusion For if a child when he is furst born were not holp & covered he were soon lorn He hath no strength to help him never a dell yet bestis have power to help themself well So considering man's body in deed A best is more noble & man more wretched Be cause he hath need of many more things Than bestis have to help of their lyffyngis Also man must daily labour & sweet To get him sustenance as drink & meet The ground he must dig & the bestis kill For breed and meet his body to fill Grapis frutis and herbis norssh diligently To make good drynkis to refresh his body But all brute bestis have covering natural sufficient to cover their bodies with all And find their food ever on the ground ready with out any pain labour or study So every man by reason of his body Is more wretched & in more misery Than bestis be/ yet thus not withstanding Man is most noble of creaturis living Not by his body for that is impotent But by his soul being so excellent For by reason of his soul intyllectyve He subde with all other bestis alive And compelleth all other bestis that be By his wit to relieve his necessity But bestis have no wit themself to defend Nor can get no more than god hath them send For take any best that weryth here And do clip it of bare against winter That best hath no manner of policy To get other covering for his body Of cloth nor skins nor hath no wit To put it upon him though one have made it Nor can build no house nor kyndyl no fire To warm his body if need should require But yet a man hath wit & understanding For to help himself in every such thing So man for his soul intellectual Is most noble creature of bestis all That is a very good & preguant reason yet me thynkith thou makest a degression From the argument that we furst began which was to prove who was most gentleman which we disputed I would thou haddist heard it Tussh I hard what ye said everi whit Then show thy reason therein or thou go Nay be god I have some what else to do I must go by me a halporth of gross The spokes of my cart therewith to dress Trow ye that I will leave my business For your babeling pomp & foolishness Nay by scent marry I will not do so For I can now to the merket go And for an haipeny as much gross by As shall cost me in our town a penny And I tell the plainly which out any boast A halfpenny is as well saved as lost
