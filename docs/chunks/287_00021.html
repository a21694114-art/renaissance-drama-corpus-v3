Why so hath this, both by his Father and Mother. Better it were they all came by his Father: Or by his Father there were none at all: For emulation, who shall now be nearest, Will touch us all too near, if God prevent not. O full of danger is the Duke of Glouster, And the Queen's Sons, and Brothers, haught and proud: And were they to be ruled, and not to rule, This sickly Land, might solace as before. Come, come, we fear the worst: all will be well. When Clouds are seen, wisemen put on their cloaks; When great leaves fall, than Winter is at hand; When the Sun sets, who doth not look for night? Untimely storms, makes men expect a Dearth: All may be well; but if God sort it so, 'Tis more than we deserve, or I expect. Truly, the hearts of men are full of fear: You cannot reason (almost) with a man, That looks not heavily, and full of dread.
