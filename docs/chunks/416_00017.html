Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

I fair good Silvio? can you call me fair? That am not fair a whit unto your eyes. Or fair or soul, did you not see my dog? Answer to this, or I am quickly gone. Still thou art froward unto her that thee adores, Who would believe that in that smooth aspect Were harboured such rugged thoughts. Thou through These savage woods and rocky hills pursuest A beast that flies thee, and consumest thyself In tracing out thy greyhounds steps: and me Thou shun'st and dost disdain that loves thee so. Ah leave these does that run so fast away, Take hold of me thy preordained prey. Nymph, I Melampo came to seek, not to lose time, Farewell. Do not so shun me cruel Silvio, I'll tell thee news of thy Melampo man. Thoviests Dorinda. (Do.) Silvio, I protest By that dear love that me thy handmaid makes, I know where thy Melampo is that coursed the do. How did he lose her? Both dog and do are in my power. Both in your power? Why doth it grieve you then That I them hold that do adore you so? Dear Dorinda, quickly give me him. See wavering child, am I not fortunate? When a beast and a dog can make me dear to thee. Good reason too, but yet her I'll deceive. What will you give me? Two guilded apples Which my mother gave me yesterday. I want no apples, and perhaps I could Thee better-tasted give; didst thou not thus Disdain my gifts. What wouldst thou have, a kid, A lamb? Ah but my father gives me no such leave. Nor kids, nor lambs do I desire, it is thy love My Silvio which I seek. Wilt thou nought but my love? Nought else. I give it thee. Now my dear Nymph Give me my dog and do. Ah that thou knewst That treasure's worth whereof thou seemest so liberal, Or that thy heart did answer to thy tongue. Hear me fair Nymph, thou ever tellest me of A certain love, I know not what it is. Thou dost desire I should thee love, and so I do. As far forth as I can, or understand, Thou callst me cruel, and I know not cruelty. Wretched Dorinda, how hast thou placed thy hopes In beauty, feeling ne'er a spark of love? Thou lovely boy art such a fire to me, And yet burns not thyself. Thee under human shape O daintie mother, did the Cyprian dame Bring forth, thou hast his arrows and his fire. Well know my breast both burnt and wounded too, Get but his wings unto thy shoulders, and New Cupia shall thou be, were't not thy heart Is made of rocky frozen Icy shelf, Thou wantedst nought of love, but love itself. Tell me, what kind of thing is this same love?
