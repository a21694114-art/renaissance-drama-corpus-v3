Can they Cant, or Mill? are they masters of their Arts? Not bachelors these, they cannot have proceeded so far; they have scarce had their time to be lousy yet. All the better; I would be acquainted with them while they are in clean life, the I'll do their tricks the cleanlier. We must have some music than, and take out the Wenches. Music, we'll have a whole poverty of pipers, call cheeks upon the Bagpipe, and Tom Ticklefoot with his Tabor; see where he comes! I, and all the good wenches of Windsor; after him, yonder is Prue o'the Park, And Frances o'the Castle; And long Meg of Eton; And Christian o' Dorny. See the miracle of a Minstrel. he's able to muster up the smocks of the two Shires; And set the Codpieces and they by th'ears at pleasure. I cannot hold now, there's my groat, let's have a fit for mirth sake. Yes, and they'll come about us for luck sake. But look to our pockets, and purses, for our own sake. I, I have the greatest charge; gather the money. Come Girls, here be Gipsies come to town, let's dance'em down. Sweet Doxies, and dels, My Roses, and Knells, Scarce out of the shells, Your hands nothing else. We ring you no knells With our Ptolemy's bells, Though we come from the fells, But bring you good spells, And tell you some chances, In midst of your dances, That fortune advances, To Prudence, or Frances; To Sisly, or Harry, To Roger, or Mary, Or Peg of the Dary; To Maudlin, or Thomas, Than do not run from us, Although we look tawny, We are healthy, and brawny, What ere your demand is, we'll give you no jaundis. Say you so old Gipsy? 'slid these go too't in rhymes; this is better than canting by tone half. Nay, you shall hear; peace, they begin with Prudence, mark that. The wiser Gipsie's the Marry. Are you advised? Yes, and i'll stand too't, that a wise Gipsy (take him at time o'year) is as pollique a piece of flesh, as most Justices in the County where he stalks. To love a Keeper, your fortune will be; But the Doucets better than him, or his fee. Ha' Prue, has he hit you i'th' teeth with a sweet bit? Let her alone, she'll swallow well enough; A learned Gipsy. You'll hear more hereafter. Mary, and i'll listen; who stands next? Jack Cockrell. You'll ha' good luck to horseflesh o' my life, You ploughed so late with the Vicar's wife. A Prophet, a prophet, no Gipsy; or if he be a Gipsy, a divine Gipsy. Mark Frances, now she's going too't, the virginity o' the Parish. Fear not, in hell you'll never lead Apes; A mortified maiden, of five 'scapes. Birlady he touched the virgin string there a little too hard, they are arrant learned men all I see; what say they upon Tom, Clod, List. Clods feet will in Christmas go near to be bore, When he has lost all his hob nails at Post and pair.
