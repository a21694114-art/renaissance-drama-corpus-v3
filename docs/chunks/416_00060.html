If thou a father wert? I am a father man, A tender father of an only son: Yet were this same, my Siluioes' head, my hand Should be as ready for't as 'tis for this: For he this sacred habit shall unworthy wear, That to a public good, his private doth prefer. O let me kiss him yet before he die. Thou mayst not man. Art thou so cruel son? Thou wilt not answer thy sad father once. Good father hold your peace. O wretched we The holocaust contaminate o Gods. The life you gave, I cannot better give, Then for her sake, who sole deserves to live. Oh thus I thought his father's tears would make Him break his silence. Wretch with error have I done the law of silence, quite I had forgot. On Ministers, why do we stay so long? Carry him to the Temple back to th'noly Cell, There take again his voluntary vow. Then bring him back, and bring new Water too, New Wine, new Fire: dispatch, the sun grows low. But thank thou heavens thou aged impudent, Thou art his father? if thou wert not: well, (I swear by this same sacred habit on my head I wear) Thou shouldst soon taste how ill I brook thy boldness. Why, know'st thou who I am▪ know'st thou that with This rod I rule affairs both humane and divine? I cry you mercy holy sacred Priest. I suffered thee so long, till thou growest insolent. Knowest thou not Rage that justice 〈◊〉 up, The longer 'tis delayed, the greater 'tis? Tempestius fury never waigned rage, In breasts magnanimus, but that one blast Of Generous effect could cool the same: But it I can not grace obtain, let me Find justice yet, you can not that deny, Law makers be not freed from the Laws: I ask you justice, justice grant me then, You are unjust, if you Mirtillo kill. Let me then know how I can be unjust? Did you not tell me it unlawful was To sacrifice a Stranger's blood▪ I told you so, And told you that which heavens did command. He is a Stranger you would sacrifice. A Stranger, how? is he not then thy son? Let it suffice, and seek no further now. Perhaps because you not begot him here. Oft he lest knows, that most would understand. here we the kindred mean, and not the place. I call him Stranger, for I got him not. Is he thy son, and thou begots him not? He is my son, though I begot him not. Didst thou not say that he was borne of thee? I said he was my son, not borne of me. Extremity of grief hath made thee mad. If I were mad, I should not feel my grief. Thou art ore-madd, or else a lying man. A lying man will never tell the truth. How can it be son, and not son at once? The son of love, and not of nature he's.
