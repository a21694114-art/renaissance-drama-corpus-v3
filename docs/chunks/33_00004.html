Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Sir ye may show your ware for your mind But I think ye shall no profit find Well though this your neigh acquit no cost Yet think I not my labour lost For by the faith of my body I like full well this company Up shall this pack for it is plain I came not hither all for gain Who may not play one day in a week May think his thrift is far to seek devise what pastime ye think best And make ye sure to find me priest Why be ye so universal That you can do what so ever ye shall. Sir if ye list to appose me What I can do then shall ye see. Than tell me this be ye perfit in drinking perfit in drinking as may be wysht by thinking Then after your drinking how fall ye to winking Sir after drinking while the shot is tynking Some heads be swimming but mine will be sinking And upon drinking mine eyse will be pynking For winking to drinking is always lynking. Then drink and sleep ye can well do But if ye were desired thereto I pray you tell me can you sing Sir I have some sight in singing. But is your breast any thing sweet. What ever my breast be, my voice is meet, That answer showeth you a right singing man Now what is your will good father than. What helpeth will where is no skill And what helpeth skill where is no wit. For will or skill what helpeth it Where froward knaves be lacking will Leave of this curyosytie And who that list sing after me. This liketh me well so mot I the. So help me god it liketh not me Where company is met and well agreed Good pastime doth right well in deed But who can sit in dalliance Men sit in such a variance As we were set or ye came in Which strife this man did first begin Alleging that such men as use For love of god not and refuse On for to go from place to place A pilgrimage calling for grace Shall in that pain with penitence Obtain discharge of conscience Comparing that life for the best Enduction to our endless rest Upon these words our matter grew For if he could avow them true As good to be a gardener As for to be a pardoner But when I hard him so far wide I then approached and replied saying this that this indulgence Having the foresaid penitence dischargeth man of all offence With much more profit than this pretence I ask but two pens at the most I iwis this is not very great cost And from all pain without despair My soul for his keep even his chair And when he dieth he may be sure To come to heaven even at pleasure And more than heaven he can not get How far so ever he list to let
