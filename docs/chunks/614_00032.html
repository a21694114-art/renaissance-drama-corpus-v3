Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

What's he comes with him? Why Hoord, that married lately the widow meddler. Oh, I cry you mercy sir. Now gentlemen visitants? how does master Dampit? faith here he lies e'en drawing— in sir, good canary as fast as he can sir, a very weak creature truly, he is almost past memory. Fie Master Dampit: you lie lazing a-bed here, and I come to invite you to my wedding dinner, up, up, up. whose's this master Hoord! who hast thou married in the name of foolery. A rich widow. A Dutch widow. A rich widow,— one widow meddler. Medler she keeps open house. She did I can tell you in her other husband's days, open house for all comers, horse and man was welcome, and Room enough for 'em all. There's too much for thee then, thou mayst let out some to thy Neighbours. What? hung alive in chains O Spectacle, bed staffs of steel, O monstrum, horrendum, Inform, Ingens cvi Lumen ademptum, O Dampit, Dampit, here's a Just judgement, shown upon usury, extortion, and trampling Villainy. This exc'llent, thief rails upon the Thief. Is this the end of cut throat Usury, Brothel, and blasphemy? now mayst thou see what Race a Usurer runs. Why thou Rogue of universality, do not I know thee? thy Sound is like the cuckoo, the Welsh Ambassador, thou cowardly slave that offers to fight with a sick man when his weapons down: rail upon me in my naked bed? why thou great Lucifer's little vicar, I am not so weak but I know a knave at first sight, thou Inconscionable Rascal, thou that goest upon middlesex juries, and will make haste to give up thy verdict, because thou wilt not lose thy dinner, are you answered? An't 'twere not for shame.— Thou wouldst be hanged then. Nay you must exercise patience Master Gulf, always in a sick-man's Chamber. he'll quarrel with none I warrant you, but those that are bedrid. Let him come Gentlemen, I am armed, reach my close stool hither. Here willbe a sweet fray anon, I'll leave you gentlemen. Nay we'll along with you, Master Gulf. Hang him usuring rascal. Push, set your Strength to his, your wit to his. Pray Gentlemen depart, his hours come upon him, sleep in my bosom, sleep. Nay we have enough of him i'faith, keep him for the house.— Now make your best. For thrice his wealth, I would not have his breast. A little thing would make me beat him, now he's asleep. Mass then willbe a pitiful day when he wakes. I would be loath to see that day, come. You overrule me gentlemen i'faith. Nay uncle, let me prevail with you so much, i'faith go, now he has invited you, I shall have great joy there, when he has borne away the widow. Why la, I thought where I should find you presently; Uncle, a my troth, 'tis nothing so. What's nothing so sir, is not he married to the widow. No by my troth is he not Uncle.
