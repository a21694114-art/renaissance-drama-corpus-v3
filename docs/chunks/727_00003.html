A very Musical motion, and I agree to it. Sing that sing can, for my part I will only while you sing, keep time with my gestures. A la more du France. NIght as well as brightest day hath her delight, Let us then with mirth and Music deck the night, Never did glad day such store Of joy to night boqueath. Her Stars than adore, Both in Heaven, and here beneath. Love and beauty, mirth and Music yield true joys, Though the Cynics in their folly count them toys. Raise your spirits near so high, They will be apt to fall: None brave thoughts envy, Who had ere brave thought at all. joy is the sweet friend of life, the nurse of blood, Patron of all health, and fountain of all good: Never may joy hence depart, But all your thoughts attend, Nought can hurt the heart, That retains so sweet a friend. Silence sirs, here comes Silvanus' god of these Woods, whose presence is rare, and imports some novelty. Let us give place, for this place is fitter for Deities then us. THat health which harbours in the fresh aired groves, Those pleasures which green hill and valley moves, Silvanus the commander of them all, Here offers to this State Imperial; Which as a homager he visits now, And to a greater power his power doth bow, With all, thus much his duty signifies: That there are certain Semideities, Belonging to his Sylvan walks, who come Led with the Music of a Sprightly drum, To keep the night awake and honour you, (Great Queen) to whom all Honours they hold due, So rest you full of joy, and wished content, Which though it be not given, 'tis fairly meant. STay Goddess, stay a little space, Our poor Country love to grace, Since we dare not too long stay you, Accept at our hands, we pray you, These mean presents to express Greater love, than we profess, Or can utter now for woe Of your parting hastened so. Gifts these are, such as were wrought By their hands, that them have brought, Homebred things, which they presumed, After I had them perfumed With my flowery incantation, To give you in presentation At your parting, come feat Lasses With fine curtsies, and smooth faces, Offer up your simple toys To the Mistress of our joys; While we the sad time prolong With a mournful parting song. CAn you the Author of our joy So soon depart? Will you revive, and strait destroy, New mirth to tears convert? O that ever cause of gladness Should so swiftly turn to sadness! Now as we droop, so will these flowers Barred of your sight. Nothing avail them heavenly showers Without your heavenly light. When the glorious Sun forsakes us, Winter quickly over-takes us. Yet shall our prayers your ways attend, When you are gone; And we the tedious time will spend, Remembering you alone. Welcome here shall you hear ever, But the word of parting never.
