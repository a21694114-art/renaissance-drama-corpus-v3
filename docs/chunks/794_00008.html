Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

If there be any Gentleman, that, for the accomplishing of his natural endowments, entertains a desire of learning the languages, especially, the nimble French, majest-like Spanish, courtly Italian, masculine Dutch, happily-compounding Greek, mystical Hebrew, and physical Arabic; or that is otherwise transported with the admirable knowledge of foreign policies, complemental behaviour, natural dispositions, or whatsoever else belongs to any people or country under heaven; he shall, to his abundant satisfaction, be made happy in his expectation and success, if he please to repair to the sign of the Globe. Good, good; I'll monopolize this commodity; when I shall have so many tongues to woo, I will not doubt to obtain Astronomia. SIr, you did that by a Poetica licentia. SO, Grammaticus, you'd fain Rule me still:— Et nos ergo manum ferulae subduximus. Nay, Poeta, you must not abuse him that hath been your Master, he hath been your Master, Ergo, you must not abuse him. Why, how now, Logicus? will you be the Neptune, to calm these Seas with your three-forked Mace? I thought you could spit nothing but Aristotle. Aristotle? saucy boy? Aristotelis libri sunt omne genus elegantia referti; pro Omnis generis. Nay, Poeta, we must grant you the eloquence: Nobis non licet esse tam disertis disertos. Why how, no, Logicus? hast thou caught the itch of Grammaticus? I should rather have thought, thou wouldst have infected him. How now? boys talk? by the soul of Priscian, A praeceptore vapulabis. Nay, then i'faith: A trepido vix abstinetira Magistro. What? insolent? Faciam vt meique ac huius diei, ac loci, semper memineris. Melancholico, do thou crack an argument with clog-head, there. I'll do my best to crack his pate, if I can. He bites, he bites: O, do you scratch, you coward? Yes, Sir, because you have the itch. To him, Melancholico. Nay, let me alone, I warrant you: we are at it, tooth and nail. Well, Poeta, Refero ad Senatum. Will you come again, Sir! Non si me obsecres. I believe thee, i'faith; Logicus, will you return? I see no reason for it: Ergo, I won't. O, have we broke off one of the forks of your Mace? he most valiantly now runs away upon two feet: Stay, here comes Choler, Grammaticus his man. Who was that ran away last there? Logicus? Yes. Did you beat him? Yes. And who was the other? my Master? Yes Did you beat him? Yes, Sir: what say you to that? What say I to that? marry, I say, I would have fought as long as I could have stood, if you had not left beating of my Master. Oh! is that all! Domini similis es; farewell, valiant Champion. Oh! is that all? Domini similis es; farewell, valiant Champion. How? baffled? by my masters Ferula, I'll quarrel with the next man I meet, who ere he be: and yonder comes Sanguis, Medicus his man; but he looks as if he would say somewhat; I'll therefore stand aside first, and hear what he'll say.
