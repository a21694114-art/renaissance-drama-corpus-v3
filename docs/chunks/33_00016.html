By the mass learn to make courtesy Courtesy before and courtesy behind him And thenon each side the devil blind him Nay when I have it perfitly Ye shall have the devil and all of courtesy But it is not soon learned brother One knave to make courtesy to another Yet when I am angry that is the worst I shall call my master knave at the first. Then would some master perhaps clowtye But as for me ye need not doubt ye For I had liefer be without ye Then have such business about ye. So help me god so were ye better What should a beggar be a ietter It were no whit your honesty To have us twain jet after ye. Sir be ye sure he telleth you true If we should wait this would ensue t wolde be sayd truste me at a worde Two knaves made courtesy to the third Now by my troth to speak my mind sins they be so loath to be assyned To let them lose I think it best And so shall ye live best in rest Sir I am not on them so fond To compel them to keep their bond And since ye list not to wait on me I clearly of waiting discharge ye. Marry sir I heartily thank you. And I like wise I make god avow Now be ye all even as ye began No man hath lost nor no man hath won Yet in the debate wherewith ye began By way of advise I will speak as I can I do perceive that pilgrimage Is chief the thing ye have in usage Whereto in effect for love of Chryst Ye have or should have been entyst And who so doth with such intent Doth well declare his time well spent And so do ye in your pretence If ye procure thus indulgence Unto your neighbours charitably For love of them in god only All this may be right well applied To shewell you both well occupied For though ye walk not both one way Yet walking thus this dare I say That both your walks come to one end And so for all that do pretend By aid of god's grace to ensue Any manner kind of virtue As some great almyse for to give Some in wilful povetie to live Some to make high ways and such other works And some to maintain priests and clerks To sing and pray for soul departed These with all other virtues well marked All though they be of sundry kinds Yet be they not used with sundry minds But as god only doth all those move So every man only for his love With love and dread obediently Worketh in these virtues unyformely Thus every virtue if we list to scan Is pleasant to god and thankful to man
