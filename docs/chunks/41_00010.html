Come, are me blessing, pretty boys apace I will not bow sure▪ to such a foolish face. Axe blessing I say, and make me no more ado. Unsemely were it, we Prelates should do so. For no compulsion will I do it by sweet Marye. I must fetch ye in, there is no remedy A naughty horsons, have I brought ye up hitherto? And know not your father, ye shall drink both ere I go No more at this time, forsooth we cry amercye. Down on your knees than, & axe me blessing shortly. Bless me gentle father, for sweet saint charity. Arise noughtyknaves, God let ye never to the Though among ourselves, we murmure, brag, & face Sometime for lucre, sometime for the higher place Yet for advantage, in this we all agree To blind the rulers, and deceive the commonalty. Art advised of that, by the Mess we are in deed Yet of our knaveries, the foul & will nevertake heed. To labour with a spade Our colour would it fade We may not with that trade We love so much our ease We must live by their sweat, And have good drink and meat When they have not to eat The substance of a pease. We lead them in the dark And so their conscience mark That stutdye they are and stark, In every wicked evil We teach idolatry And laughed full merely To see each company Run headlongs to the devil. Ifwe may have the tithings And profitable offeryages We came not to what doings They customably fall We are such mercenaries And subtle proprietaries As from the flock all carries The wolle, skin, flesh and all. In our perambulations We look for commendations And lowly salutations In temple, house and street. Our lousy latin hours In boroughs and in bowers The poor people devours And tread them under feet. I am ambition, whose bysposycyan Is honour to appete I gape for empire, and worship desire As Minos did in Crete I look up Aloft, and love to lie soft Not caring for my flock Have Iones the fleece, with pigs, lambs, and gese They may go turn a Sock.
