The sound is honey, but the sense is gall: The sweeting bless me with a cheerful look. How can mine eyes dart forth a pleasant look, When they are stopped with floods of flowing tears. If tongue with grief, and eyes with tears be filled, Say Virgin, how doth thy heart admit, The pure affection of great Soliman? My thoughts are like pillars of Adamant, Too hard to take an new impression. Nay then I see my stooping makes her proud, She is my vassal, and I will command, Coy Virgin knowest thou what offence it is, To thwart the will and pleasure of a king? Why thy life is done, if I but say the word. Why that's the period that my heart desires. And die thou shalt, unless thou change thy mind. Nay then Perseda grows resolute, Soliman's thoughts and mine resemble, Lives paralyse that never can be joined. Then kneel thou down, And army hands receive the stroke of death, doomed to thyself by thine own wilfulness. Strike, strike, thy words pierce deeper than thy blows. Brusor hide her, for her looks withhold me, O Brusor thou hast not hid her lips, For there sits Venus with Cupid on her knee, And all the Graces smiling round about her, So craving pardon that I cannot strike. Her face is covered over quite my Lord. Why, so. O Brusor, seest thou not her milk white neck, That Alabaster tower, 'twill break the edge of my keen scimitar, And pieces flying back will wound myself. Now she is all covered my Lord. Why now at last she dies. O Christ receive my soul. Hark Brusor she calls on Christ, I will not send her to him, Her words are music, The self same music that in ancient days, Brought Alexander from war to banqueting, And made him fall from skirmishing to kissing. No my dear Love would not let me kill thee, Though Majesty would turn desire to wrath, There lies my sword, humbled at thy feet, And I myself that govern many kings, Entreat a pardon for my rash misdeed. Now Soliman wrongs his imperial state, But if thou love me, and have hope to win, Grant me one boon that I shall crave of thee, whate'er it be, Perseda I grant it thee, Then let me live a Christian Virgin still, Unless my state shall alter by my will, My word is past, and I recall my passions, What should he do with crown and Empery, That cannot govern private fond affections, Yet give me leave in honest sort to court thee, To ease, though not to cure, my malady: Come sit thee down upon my right hand here, This seat I keep void for another friend: Go janissaries call in your Governor, So shall I joy between two captive friends, And yet myself be captive to them both, If friendship's yoke were not at liberty: See where he comes my other best beloved. My sweet and best beloved. My sweet and best beloved: For thee my dear Erastus have I lived.
