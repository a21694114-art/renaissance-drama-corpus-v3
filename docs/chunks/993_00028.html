Zounds they are come, and have been here with me. Traitorous Iulianus, and his ravished daughter, An army of Moores, of Turks and infidels. Your fancies trouble you, they are but coming, Too near in that, make up to your soldiers, Full twenty thousand now will follow you and more. The moor's a coming, & the devil too that must Succeed me in my last monarchy, take arms and fight, The fiends shall know they have not played me right. O for a private place to be hanged in; when all hope's gone, welcome despair; which way soever the day goes, I'm sure this is my way; If the King overcome, I shall be hanged for Jacinta's escape, if she rise, I fall in recompense of her wrongs. All my grief is, I want an heir to have my purse and clothes, one that would take the pains for me, an honest hangman were now as good a companion as I would desire to meet with; I have lived a Lord, and I would be loath to die an executioner. Murder is come to light; Oh sister how hast thou overthrown our honourable house before it was well covered; oh ambitious sister, half a share in a Lord would not content thee, thou would have all or none, now thou hast none, for thou hast killed thy Lord and husband. I was a Lord, although a bawdy Lord. I was a Lord's brother, although a bawdy Lords brother. O Lechery, how hast thou puffed me up and undone me. O Lechery, thou hast battened me a while, and then spoilt me. Ha? what art thou? Partly honourable, partly miserable. Give me thy hand. Give me thy halter then. Art thou a hangman then? ay, and a mad one, but now I droop, and am ready to drop into the budget. Look here's work for thee, here's clothes, and here's money, wout thou take the pains to hang me? I have lived a lord's brother, and would be loath to die a hangman. Do not desire to die, live till thou diest of thine own accord. 'tis my desire, but I want a cord of mine own, prithee lend me thine. Let me persuade thee to be charitable to thyself, spare thyself, and hang me, I have been a Pander, know'st thou what a Pander is? In brief a knave; more at large thus; he's a thing that is poor, He waits upon a whore, When she's sick, he's sore, In the streets he goes before, At the chamber waits at door, All his life a runs o'th' score, This I know, and know no more. All this I'll add to it, He wears long lock, And villainous socks, Many nights in the stocks, Endures some knocks, And a many of mocks, Eats reversions of cocks, Yet lies in the flocks, Thrives by the smocks, And dies with the pox. All this I have been, and now desire to be hanged for't.
