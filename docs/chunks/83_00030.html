And of a ring and token. That Suresby I spied, did more than half suspect, That I my faith to Gavin Goodluck did reject. But there was no such matter dame Custance in deed? If ever my head thought it, God send me ill speed. Wherefore I beseech you, with me to be a witness, That in all my life I never intended thing less, And what a brainsick fool Ralph Roister Doister is, yourself know well enough. Ye say full true iwis. Because to be his wife I ne grant nor apply, Hither will he come he sweareth by and by, To kill both me and mine, and beat down my house flat. Therefore I pray your aid. I warrant you that. Have I so many years lived a sober life, And showed myself honest, maid, widow, and wife, And now to be abused in such a vile sort, Ye see how poor Widows live all void of comfort. I warrant him do you no harm nor wrong at all. No, but Mathew Merrygreek doth me most appall, That he would join himself with such a wretched lout. He doth it for a jest I know him out of doubt, And here cometh Merrygreek. Then shall we here his mind. CUstance and Trusty both, I do you here well find. Ah Mathew Merrygreek, ye have used me well. Now for altogether ye must your answer tell. Will ye have this man, woman? or else will ye not? Else will be come never bore so brim nor tossed so hot. But why join ye with him. For mirth? or else in sadness The more fond of you both hardly the matter guess. Lo how say ye dame? Why do ye think dame Custance That in this wooing I have meant aught but pastance? Much things ye spoke I wot, to maintain his dotage. But well might ye judge I spoke it all in mockage, For why? Is Roister Doister a fit husband for you? I dare say ye never thought it. No to God I vow. And did not I know afore of the insurance Between Gavin Goodluck, and Christian Custance? And did not I for the nonce, by my conveyance, Read his letter in a wrong sense for dalliance? That if you could have take it up at the first bound, We should thereat such a sport and pastime have found, That all the whole town should have been the merrier. Ill ache your heads both, I was never wearier, Nor never more verte since the first day I was borne. But very well I wist he here did all in scorn. But I feared thereof to take dishonesty. This should both have made sport, and showed your honesty And Goodluck I dare swear, your wit therein would low. Yea, being no worse than we know it to be now. And nothing yet to late, for when I come to him, Hither will he repair with a sheeps look full grim, force and violence to drive you to yield.
