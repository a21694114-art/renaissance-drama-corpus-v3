The broad eye of the Heavens still attend you Sir. And grant that the sweet Fairies may nightly put money in your shoes Sir. And sweep your house clean Sir. And make you the rich husband of many wives. And the blessed father of many children. The gods of the night send you happy dreams. And that you may never pare your nails upon a Friday. And that the horse-shoe may never be pulled from your threshold. And that your Stables may be always free from the queen of the Goblins. That your nose may never bleed only three drops at a time. That a yellow Death-mould may never appear upon your hand, or any part of your body. That you may never stumble at your going out in the morning. That you may— Be rid of you Varlets. What Egyptian darkness has seized upon your faces? we are indeed from Egypt-land, and't please your good vrship: Brother, by the Ruffian, I think this is a quire cove, he cuts such quire whidds: Good Sir, if you be a Gentry cove, vouchsafe some small Win or but a Make, for we have neither Lower, nor Libbeg, nor Libkin. No by Salomon, unless it be Strommell sometimes in a Skipper; we had rather Maund than Mill to keep us from Trining. Good Gentry cove vouchsafe us a little Lower, or some old duds, as a Castir or a Commission. Marry if I had a Commission, I knew what to do with you. Ah, your good vrship, to cover our Quarommes, that our wants may not drive us to the Chates— let me see your Famble good Master. My Famble Villain? This is almost as bad as the language of Logicus. Ah your good vrship! it is the Gypsy language: the vrship of the gods bless your fair glaziers, and look out with your merciful eyen. Gentle Ruler of this place, if so you be, vouchsafe to favour us in the way of truth for the God's cause. Somewhat towards a meals meat, Well and Wisely bestow upon us, and the Go-ads reward you for't. Ah good Master well and wisely, give me but an old sheet against the cold, or an old Petticoat or smock of my Mistress's (Heaven save her life) for my poor Doxy. Good Sir give but a cup of your best drink well and wisely. The gods save the King and his Counsel, and the governors of this place; you shall have a fair wife Master, and many children. Ha! a fair wife and many children? how know'st thou that? what's thy name? Physiognomus, good Master. And thine? Cheiromantes, and't like your good vrship. Physiognomus, and Cheiromantes? Why what can you do? We can tell the will of the heavens good Master; we can tell your fortune, Master. We can tell the will of the heavens good Master; we can tell your fortune, Master. My fortune? why what's my fortune? You shall have a very fair wife. Shall have? thou mean'st, Would have. No History ever made mention of so fair a one; she shall be as beautiful as the Stars.
