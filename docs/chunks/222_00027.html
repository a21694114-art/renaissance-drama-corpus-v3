A Clifford, a Clifford, God save the King. How like a feather is this rascal company Blown every way, But that they may see there wants no valiancy in me, My staff shall make way through the midst of you, And so a pox take you all. Go some and make after him, and proclaim, That those that can bring the head of Cade, Shall have a thousand crowns for his labour. Come march way. Lord Somerset, what news hear you of the rebel Cade? This my gracious lord, that the lord Say is done to death, And the city is almost sacked. God's will be done, for as he hath decreed, so it must be: And be it as he please, to stop the pride of those rebellious men. Had the noble Duke of Suffolk been alive, The rebel Cade had been suppressed ere this, And all the rest that do take part with him. Long live King Henry, England's lawful King, Lo here my Lord, these rebels are subdued, And offer their lives before your highness feet. But tell me Clifford, is their captain here? No, my gracious lord, he is fled away, but proclamations are sent forth, that he that can but bring his head, shall have a thousand crowns. But may it please your majesty, to pardon these their faults, that by that traitor's means were thus misled. Stand up you simple men, and give God praise, For you did take in hand you know not what, And go in peace obedient to your King, And live as subjects, and you shall not want, Whilst Henry lives, and wears the English crown. God save the King, God save the King. Come let us haste to London now with speed, That solemn processions may be sung, In laud and honour of the God of heaven, And triumphs of this happy victory. Good Lord how pleasant is this country life? This little land my father left me here, With my contented mind, serves me as well, As all the pleasures in the court can yield, Nor would I change this pleasure for the court. Sounes here's the Lord of the soil stand villain, thou wilt betray me to the King, and get a thousand crowns for my head, but ere thou goest, i'll make thee eat iron like an ostrich, and swallow my sword like a great pin. Why saucy companion, why should I betray thee? Is't not enough that thou hast broke my hedges, And entered into the ground without the leave of me the owner, But thou wilt brave me too? Brave thee and beard thee too, by the best blood of the realm, look on me well, I have eat no meat this five days, yet and I do not leave thee and thy five men as dead as a door nail, I pray God I may never eat grass more.
