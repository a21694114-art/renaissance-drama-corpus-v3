'tis Mr. Frederick my Lady's nephew. What of him? Is come from the University. By whose directions? It seems my Ladies. Let me speak with him Before he see his Aunt, I do not like it. Mr. Frederick welcome I expected not So soon your presence, what's the hasty cause? These letters from my Tutor will acquaint you. Welcome home sweet Mr. Frederick. Where's my Aunt? she's busy about her painting, in her closet, The Outlandish man of Art, is copying out Her countenance. She is sitting for her picture. Yes sir, and when 'tis drawn, she will be hanged Next the French Cardinal in the dining room, But when she hears you're come, she will dismiss The Belgic gentleman to entertain Your worship. Change of air has made you witty. Your Tutor gives you a handsome character Frederick, and is sorry your aunt's pleasure Commands you from your studies, but I hope You have no quarrel to the liberal arts, Learning is an addition beyond Nobility of birth, honour of blood Without the ornament of knowledge is A glorious ignorance. I never knew more sweet and happy hours Than I employed upon my books, I heard A part of my Philosophy, and was so Delighted with the harmony of nature, I could have wasted my whole life upon't. 'tis pity a rash indulgence should corrupt So fair a Genius, she's here, I'll observe. My most loved Aunt. Support me, I shall faint. What ails your Ladyship? Is that Frederick, In black. Yes Madam, but the doublets Satin. The boy's undone. Madam you appear troubled. Have I not cause? Was not I trusted with Thy education boy, and have they sent thee Home like a very scholar. 'twas ill done howe'er they used him in the University, To send him to his friends thus. Why sir, black (I or 'tis the colour that offends your eyesight) Is not within my reading any blemish, Sables are no disgrace in Heraldry. 'tis coming from the College thus, that makes it Dishonourable, while you wore it for Your father, it was commendable, or were Your Aunt dead, you might mourn and justify. What luck I did not send him into France, They would have given him generous education, Taught him another garb to wear his lock, And shape, as gawdy as the Summer, how To dance, and wag his feather ala mode, To compliment, and cringe, to talk not modestly Like I forsooth, and no forsooth, to blush And look so like a Chaplain, there he might Have learned a brazen confidence, and observed So well the custom of the country, that He might by this time have invented fashions For us, and been a benefit to the Kingdom Preserved our Tailors in their wits, and saved The charge of sending into foreign Courts For pride and antic fashions, observe, In what a posture he does hold his hat now.
