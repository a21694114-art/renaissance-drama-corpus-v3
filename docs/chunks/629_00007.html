I like not this, 'tis neither right nor straight done with no law-trick, nor no good conceit, but here 'a comes. With Autumn in his bosom, pray God she shake not down his leaves, and leave him to make the Duke his father a bald reckoning. Peace and observe. Though I be a stranger to you, Yet am I well acquainted with your humour. A Lady cannot live about the Court Without the envy of your Epigrams, If she be pleasant, she is counted light, If ruddy, painted, if her ruffs be thick, They ask much poking. Trust me, love hath killed That wormwood humour, bring th'authority Of one true tongue to prove my poesy, Ever wronged woman. And you'll do't again? That's a good wench. I pray who ought that pen? That in a stammering iambic vain, Glanced at Emilia's loose and gaudy train. And broke broad jests upon her narrow heel, Poked her Rebates, and surveyed her steel, tide her fringed garters, bought her words by weight Still good in law, and an upright conceit. Do you not know that man? Suppose I do? You did but flout them, and you'll flout me too You wondered not Cornelia being so fair, Would be beholding to her Laundress dress hair. I know not this. True, nor that Florymell, Became a page's habit passing well, used single Rapier, took the rouse and swore, Of all land Beasts she could not brook the Boar, Nor that Count Lurdo, coming to a fray, Brought not the worth of one poor hair away. More than his own. That jest comes near to me, 'tis overgrown now, a wants authority. Nor that Melina whom your Muse renowns Wore out as many suitors as new gowns. And the same day that she was married, Upon her finger wore her maidenhead. In likeness of a Diamond. Indeed 'twas said, That for Ring Melina sold her head. But you're all Ovid's. They are bawdy mates, Touch Lawyers too, indeed abuse all states, Poets are wanton, and no doubt enjoy, Their fair Corynna's, though perhaps less coy. Suppose some do? Each one in this agrees to scandal us. And talk of lawyer's fees. Grant me but love, & with my utmost power, I will defend your sex's stainless honour: And with my pen dipped in the juice of gall, Be his soul's terror, that hereafter shall, Cast ink at honour's garments, or aspire To name your sex with an unhallowed fire. I take your word. Then seal it with a kiss. That kiss my Lord deserves an Epigram. Sit down good Uncle. An unbidden guest Should bring a stool along. A woman's jest. You mean a lie, for women use to lie. But not like Scholars by authority. Yet they may produce lying authority, and so cannot Poets, Nor Lawyers neither. Are you a Lawyer? i'faith Madam, he hath sit on the skirts of law any time this thirty years. Then he should be a good Trencherman by his profession.
