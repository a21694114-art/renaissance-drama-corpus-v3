Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

See where she sits, in whom we must delight, Beware she sleeps: no noise for waking her. asleep? why see how her alluring eyes, With open looks do glance on every side. O eyes more fair than is the morning star. Nature herself is not so lovely fair. Let us with reverence kiss her Lily hands, And by deserts in service win her love. Sweet Dame, if Stesias may content thine eye, Command my Neat, my flock, and tender Kids: Whereof great store do overspread our plains, Grant me sweet Mistress but to kiss thy hand. No Stesias no, Learchus is the man: Thou mirror of Dame Nature's cunning work, Let me but hold thee by that sacred hand, And I shall make thee our Utopian Queen, And set a guilded chaplet on thy head, That Nymphs and Satyrs may admire thy pomp. These twain and I have fortunes all alike. Sweet Nature's pride, let me but see thy hand, And servant like, shall Melos wait on thee: And bear thy train: as in the glorious heavens, Perseus supports his love Andromeda: Whose thirty stars, whether they rise or fall. He falls or riseth, hanging at her heels. O then to bless the love of Iphicles, Whose heart doth hold thee dearer than himself? Do but behold me with a loving look, And I will lead thee in our solemn dance, Teaching thee tunes, and pleasant lays of love. No kiss? nor touch? nor friendly look? What churlish influence deprives her mind? For Nature said, that she was innocent, And fully fraught with virtuous qualities: But speak sweet love: thou canst not speak but well. She is not tongue tied, that I know by proof. Speak once Pandora to thy loving friends. Rude knaves, what mean you thus to trouble me? She spoke to you my masters, I am none of your company. Alas she weeping sounds: Gunophilus O help to rear thy Mistress from the ground. This is the very passion of the heart, And melancholy is the ground thereof. O then to sift that humour from her heart, Let us with roundelays delight her ear: For I have heard that Music is a mean, To calm the rage of melancholy mood. What songs? what pipes? & fiddling have we here? Will you not suffer me to take my rest? What shall we do to vanquish her disease? The death of that were life to our desires: But let us go, we must not leave her thus. Saturn hath laid foundation to the rest: Whereon to build the ruin of this dame: And spot her innocence with vicious thoughts, My turn is past, and Iupiter is next. A Ioue principium, sunt & iovis omnia plena. Now Iupiter shall rule Pandora's thoughts, And fill her with Ambition and Disdain: I will enforce my influence to the worst, Least other Planets blame my regiment.
