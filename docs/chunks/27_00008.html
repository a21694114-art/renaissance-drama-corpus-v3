Than thou here openly/ to god dost promise ye sir and ever/ look that ye abstain Not only from deed/ but also from the assent So that ye commit/ neither of them twain if ye will observe/ the high commandment For surely ye may/ not be cleped innocent Nor guiltless of sin/ as for as I can find if once ye assent/ to folly in your mind. This is an hard word/ sister that ye have spoken An hard word surely/ and an heavy sentence But think ye goddies/ commandment broken For a light tryfull/ and matter of insolence Alas have ye such a spiced conscience That will be entryked/ with every merry thought Leave it woman leave it. For it is nought And man as for you/ ye shall not take that way That manner of observance/ is to hard and straight ye must attempt the world/ and therein assay Whether ye can live/ after that endrayte These. two. folk/ harp both on refrayte and ever enbesyeth them/ to rebuke you of sin That never was spotted/ ne found guilty therein take none heed of them/ their words be but wind and as for this time/ I command them to silence And set us see now/ how prately ye can find By sage policy/ and worldly prudence To maintain the state/ in honour and reverence that ye shall be in/ while ye in the world dwell Speke of this matter/ and ponder it well first me seemeth necessary/ to provide What manner folkys/ your servants shall be For surely ye are/ nothing accompanyde according/ to a man of your degree ye have here with you. two. parson's or. iii. That pleaseth you happily/ in the best wise yet it appeareth not so/ to every man's ges. What man is this? Reason sir my chief conselour And this innocency/ my nurse hitherto And sensuality that other/ by whom I have power To do/ as all sensate beasts do But reason and innocency/ chiefly these two Have the hole rule/ and governy of me To from eke is subdued/ my sensuality For cartayne sir/ reason hath done me wrong More than ever he shallbe able to recompense God knoweth sir I thought/ the season very long Till we were brought/ unto your presence But now I pray you/ to annul the sentence That nature gave unto me/ by reasons advise to my great hurt/ and uttyr preindyce And sir I ask/ none amends earthly But that reason/ may have a check mate A little knack/ a little prayty congee His haut courage/ some thing to abate For hitherto/ he hath kept great estate And had of me/ the over hand and strengar But ye not disposed/ I will suffer it no longer Thou hast had great wrong/ and that is pity For if thou be the parson/ that I take the fore thou sholdyst be/ as honourable as he
