I was about it, here. It angered me to the soul, to hear 'em begin to talk so malapert. Pray thee perform it, and thou winnest me an Idolater to thee, everlasting. Will you go in, and hear me do it? No, I'll stay here. Drive 'em out of your company, 'tis all I ask: which cannot be any way better done, then by extolling Dauphine, whom they have so slighted. I warrant you: you shall expect one of 'em presently. What a cast of kestrels are these, to hawk after Ladies, thus? ay, and strike at such an Eagle as Dauphine. He will be mad, when we will tell him. Here he comes. O Sir, you are welcome. Where's thine Uncle? Run out o'doores in's Nightcaps, to talk with a Casuist, about his Divorce. It works admirably. Thou wouldst ha' said so, and thou hadst been here! The Ladies have laughed at thee, most Comically, since thou went'st, Dauphine. And asked, if thou wert thine Uncle's keeper? And the brace of baboons answered, yes; and said, thou wert a pitiful poor fellow, and didst live upon posts: and hadst nothing but three Suits of Apparel, and some few Benevolences that Lords ga'thee to fool to hem, and swagger. Let me not live, I'll beat 'em. I'll bind 'em both to grand madam's Bed. posts, and have 'em baited with Monkeys. Thou shalt not need, they shall be beaten to thy hand, Dauphine. I have an Execution to serve upon 'em, I warrant thee shall serve: trust my plot. ay, you have many plots! So you had one, to make all the Wenches in love with me. Why, If I do not yet afore night, as near as 'tis; and that they do not every one invite thee, and be ready to scratch for thee: take the mortgage of my wit. 'Fore God, I'll be his witness; thou shalt have it, Dauphine: thou shalt be his Fool for ever, if thou dost not. Agreed. Perhaps 'twill be the better estate. Do you observe this Gallery? or rather Lobby, indeed? Here are a couple of Studies, at each end one: here will I act such a tragicomedy between the Guelphs, and the Ghibellines, Daw and La-Foole— which of 'em comes out first, will I seize on: (you two shall be the Chorus behind the Arras, and whip out between the Acts, and speak.) If I do not make 'em keep the peace, for this remnant of the day, if not of the year, I have failed once— I hear Daw.: Hide, and do not laugh, for God's sake. Which is the way into the Garden, trow? O, Iack Daw. I am glad I have met with you. In good faith, I must have this matter go no further between you. I must ha' it taken up. What matter, Sir? Between whom?
