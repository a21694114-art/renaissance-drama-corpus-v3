This makes me think that I have been to cruel, How got he this from of Lucina's arm? Faith in a mummery, and a pair of false dice, I was one of the mummers myself, simple as I stand here. I rather think it cost him very dear. I so it did, for it cost Ferdinando his life. How so? After we had got the chain in mummery, And lost our box in counter cambio, My master wore the chain about his neck, Than Ferdinando met us on the way, And reviled my master, saying he stole the chain, With that they drew, & there Ferdinando had the prickado. And whether fled my poor Erastus then? To Constantinople whether I must follow him, But ere he went, with many sighs and tears, He delivered me the chain, and bade me give it you, For perfect argument that he was true, And you too credulous. Ah stay, no more, for I can here no more. And I can sing no more. My heart had armed my tongue with injury, To wrong my friend, whose thoughts were ever true, An poore Erastus how thy starres maling: Thou great commander of the swift winged winds, And dreadful Neptune bring him back again, But Aeolus and Neptune let him go, For here is nothing but revenge and death, Then let him go, i'll shortly follow him, Not with slow sails, but with loves golden wings, My ship shall be borne with tears, and blown with sighs So will I soar about the Turkish land, Until I meet Erastus my sweet friend. And then and there, fall down amid his arms, And in his bosom there pour forth my soul, For satisfaction of my trespass past. Fair Love, according unto thy command, I seek Erastus and will combat him. I seek him, find him, bring him to my sight, For till we meet, my heart shall want delight. My petty fellow, where hast thou hid thy master, Marry sir in an armourer's shop, Where you had not best go to him. Why so, I am in honour bound to combat him, ay sir, but he knowing your fierce conditions, Hath planted a double cannon in the door, Ready to discharge it upon you, when you go by, I tell you for pure good will. In Knightly courtesy, I thank thee, But hopes the coistrel to escape me so, Thinks he bore cannon shot can keep me back: Why wherefore serves my targe of proof, but for the bullet That once put by, I roughly come upon him, Like to the wings of lightning from above, I with a martial look astonish him, Then falls he down poor wretch upon his knee, And all to late, repents his surquedry. Then do I take him on my finger's point, And thus I bear him through every street, To be a laughing stock to all the town. That done, I lay him at my mistress feet, For her to give him doom of life or death.
