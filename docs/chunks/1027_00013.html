Sirrah, sirrah, sirrah, I smell a Rat behind the hangings? Here's nobody, ha? are there no Trunks to convey secret voices? Your Lordship has a pair on. I do not like that face i'th' arras, a my conscience he points at me, a pox upon this treason, I have no stomach to't, I do see myself upon a Scaffold, making a pitiful speech already, I shall ha' my head cut off, seven years ago I laid my head upon a wager I remember, and lost it; let me see, it shall be so, 'tis good policy to be armed, Rogero, imagine I were a Traitor. How sir? ay but say imagine, we may put the case, and that I were apprehended for a Traitor. Heaven defend. Heaven has something else to do, than to defend Traitors: I say, Imagine I were brought to the bar. Good my Lord, you brought to the bar? I will beat you, if you won't imagine at my bidding: I say, suppose I now were at the bar to answer for my life. Well sir. Well sir, that's as it happens, you must imagine I will answer the best I can for myself, conceive I prithee, that these chairs were judges most grave and venerable beards and faces at my arraignment, and that thyself wert in the name of the Duke and state to accuse me what couldst thou say to me? I accuse your good honour? for what I beseech you? For high Treason you blockhead. I must be acquainted with some particulars first. Mass thou sayest right: why imagine, d''ee hear? you must but imagine, that some great-man had a conspiracy against the Duke's person, and that I being an honest Lord, and one of this great man's friends, had been drawn in, for that's the plain truth on't, 'twas against my will, but that's all one: Well, thou understandest me, show thy wit Rogero, scratch thy nimble pericranium, and thunder out my accusation ex tempore: Here I stand Signior Depazzi, ready to answer the indictment. Good my Lord it will not become me, being your humble servant. Humble Coxcomb, is't not for my good? I say, accuse me, bring it home, jerk me soundly to the quick Rogero, tickle me as thou lov'st thy Lord; I do defy thee, spare me not, and the devil take thee if thou be'st not malicious, Why then have at you, first Signior Depazzi, thou art indicted of high Treason, hold up thy hand, guilty, or not guilty? Very good. Nay, very bad sir, answer I say, guilty or not guilty? Not guilty. 'tis your best course to say so, well imagine I rise up the Duke's most learned in the Laws, and his nimble tongued Orator, have at your Signior. Come, come on sir, here I stand.
