Wounds and hearts who can abide this, Nay ye vile villain I will dress you therefore, your lazy bones I pretend so to bliss, That you shall have small lust to prate any more. Behold the Image of incipient fools, There are not a few even now of thy property, Until you be put into poverties schools: ye will not forsake this foolish insolency. Nay soft, with thee I have not made an end. The Just against lust must always contend, Therefore I purpose to wrestle with thee, Who shall have the victory, straightway we shall see. When thou wilt by his flesh: I shall hold the wag I know that lust useth not little to brag: Thou shalt find me as mighty as Sampson the strong yea the battle of lust endureth long. Wounds and flesh, I was almost down on my back, But yet I will wrestle till my bones crack. The end of thy presumption now doth appear, yet do what thou canst I will not lie here, No by his wounds you old doting knave, thinkest thou that lust will be made a slave, I shall meet you in Smithfield or else other where, By his flesh and blood, I will thee not forbear. Not of my power I do thee expel But by the might of his spirit that dwelleth in me, Inordinate lust with the just may not dwell, And therefore may not I accompany thee. Well goodman just, it is no matter, But in faith I pretend not with thee to flatter, Though from thy company depart I must, I shall live in as much wealthiness I trust. Where moste wealth is and most dilectation, There Lust is commonly of most estimation: For where as wealth wanteth idleness doth slay But where idleness is Lust parteth the slake. Thus have you seen the conflict of the just, Which all good men ought to use and frequent For horrible are the fruits of inordinate lust, Which in some case resemble Hybra the serpent, Whose head being cut of an other riseth incontinent. So one of lusts cogitations being cut away There riseth up an other, yea many we may say: It is requisite therefore that every degree Against this his lust, both strive and contend, And though at the first he seem sturdy to be, The Lord will convince him for you in the end. Your cause unto him therefore holy commend, Labouring to avoid all inordinate lust, And to practise in life, to live after the just. I can remember since Noes ship Was made and builded on Salisbury plain, The same year the weathercock of Paul's caught the pip So that Bow bell was like much woe to sustain, I can remember I am so old.
