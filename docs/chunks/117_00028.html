Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Come on fellow Diccon chalbe even with thee now. Thou wilt not stick to do this Diccon I trow. No by my father's skin, my hand down I lay it? look as I have promised, I will not denay it, But Hodge take good heed now, thou do not beshit me. And gave him a good blow on the buttock. gog's heart thou false villain dost thou bite me? What Hodge doth he hurt the or ever he begin. He thrust me into the buttock, with a bodkin or a pin, I say Gammer, Gammer? How now Hodge, how now: God's mait Gammer gurton. Thou art mad ich trow. Will you see the devil Gammer. The devil son, god bless us. Chould iche were hanged Gammer. Marry see ye might dress us. Chaue it by the mass Gammer What not my needle Hodge? Your Needle Gammer, your needle. No fie, dost but dodge. Cha found your needle Gammer, here in my hand be it. For all the loves on earth Hodge, let me see it. Soft Gammer. Good Hodge. Soft ich say, tarry a while. Nay sweet Hodge say truth, and do not me beguile. Cham sure on it ich warrant you: it goes no more astray Hodge when I speak so fair: wilt still say me nay: Go near the light gammer this well in faith good luck: Chwas almost undone: 'twas so far in my buttock 'tis mine own dear needle Hodge, sickerly I wot Cham I not a good son gammer, cham I not, Christ's blessing light on thee, hast made me for ever Ich knew that ich must find it, else choud a had it never By my troth gossip gurton, I am even as glad As though I mine own self as good a turn had: And I by my conscience, to see it so come forth, Rejoice so much at it, as three needles be worth. I am no whit sorry to see you so rejoice. Nor I much the gladder for all this noise: Yet say gramercy Diccon, for springing of the game. Gramercy Diccon twenty times, o how glad cham, If that chould do so much, your masterdom to come hither, Master Rat, good wife Chat, and Diccon together: Cha but one half penny, as far as iche know it, And chil not rest this night, till ich bestow it. If ever ye love me, let us go in and drink. I am content if the rest think as I think! Master Rat it shallbe best for you if we so do, Then shall you warm you and dress yourself too. Soft sirs, take us with you, the company shallbe the more, As proud comes behind they say, as any goes before, But now my good masters since we must be gone And leave you behind us, here all alone: Since at our last ending, thus merry we be, For Gammer Gurton's needle's sake, let us have a plaudite.
