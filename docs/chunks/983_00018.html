Well Sirrah? Slave, Thou unpolished piece of clay, how dar'st thou thus Uncivilly use a young Gentleman Whose friends and kindred I have known to be Worthy of more respect than thou of scorn, Which both come near to infinite? Very good. And do you know his friends and kindred then? Would thou didst know thy betters half so well, untutored dunghill.— In what state you sit? Stand up, or else I'll make thee lie for ever. Are you in earnest or in jest? How think you? You great Rigs-norton you, do you stand still And see your only Father wronged thus? ha?— Well, if I do not fit your cap for this (If it be made of wool) when you tithe Lambs, I'll never go to Church more, if th'whole flock Has any worse than other 'tshall go hard But some of them shall fall unto your lot. Alas I doubt he knows me His eyes so dwell upon me. Come my boy, What will you go with me? Thanks to my stars; He knows me not. Boy will you dwell with me? Thou shalt have dumpling Boy, enough, and Bacon Shall be so deep in fat, that thou mayst wade Up to the chin in lard: Salute your Master. And kiss your master's daughter that's the next Thing you must practise. You his Master, Hempseed? Truly methinks I could e'en love this Boy 'Tis such a pretty thing; Father, I pray you Good Father, let him dwell with us. No more, Peace, so he shall. Hands off you lease of Sheepskins. No, I will dwell with this old Gentleman. Well said, sweet youth. But on this condition, That you will use me like a Gentleman Of quality and worth, for I must tell you With tears, howe'er my fortunes are dejected Now, I do come of no mean house nor blood. Fear not my boy, thou shalt have cause to thank me: Follow; my maids shall presently unpage him, And hang woman on his back. But I do hope That some kind God or other will find out Some means for my escape; if not (I've said it) This hand shall make a passage for my soul To leave this body. Boy, do you come? I come. What is he gone?— hi-day! what again? Let me be hanged, my dog and my whole Family, My Wife and all, I'll put her in, if I Do not so' your buttocks Minion; I'll break you of this trade of making children Before your time, if I can find a willow Within a mile of an Oak. What shall I do? oh what shall I do? what shall I do? My father's gone to get a rod, what shall I do? Oh, oh, here comes my mother.— Placenta, you have heard my cares, my griefs And which hath caused them all, you know my love, Now by those tender years, by that first ray Of blessed light these infant eyes received Upon those vigilant knees, I do conjure thee Forsake me not in these my miseries
