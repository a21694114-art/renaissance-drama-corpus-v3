I not gave it, Mother; You have done wrong. than; I know how to place My gifts, and where; and when to found my seasons To give, not throw away my Courtesies. Count you this thrown away? What's ravished from me I count it worse; as stolen: I lose my thanks. But leave this quest: they fit not you, not me, Maudlin, Contentions of this quality. How now? Your Stag's returned upon my shoulders, He has found his way into the Kitchen again: With his two Legs, If now your Cook can dress him; 'Slid, I thought the Swine'ard would ha' be at me, He looks so big! the sturdy Churl, lewd Lorel! There Scathlock, for thy plines, thou hast deserved it. Do you give a thing, and take a thing, Madam? Not, Maudlin, you had imparted to your Neighbours; As much good do't them: I ha' done no wrong. The Spit stand still, no Broaches turn Before the fire, but let it burn Both sides, and haunches, till the whole Converted be into one Coal. What Devils Pater noster mumbles she? Stay, you will hear more of her witchery The Swilland Dropsy enter in The Lazy Cuke, and swell his skin; And the old Mort-malon his shin Now prick, and itch, withouten blind. Speak out Hag, we may hear your Devil's Matins. The Paene, we call S. Antons fire The Gout, or what we can desire, To cramp a Cuke, in every limb, Before they dine, yet; seize on him. A soul ill Spirit hath possessed her. O Karol, Karol, call him back again. Her thoughts do work upon her, in her slumber. And may express some part of her disease. Observe, and mark, but trouble not her ease. O, ô. How is't Amie? Wherefore start you? O Karol, he is fair, and sweet. What than? Are there not flowers as sweet, and fair, as men? The Lily is fair! and Rose is sweet! I', so! Let all the Roses, and the Lilies go: Karol is only fair to me! And why? Alas for Karol, Marian, I could die. He singeth sweetly too! What than? Are there not Birds sing sweeter far, than Men? I grant the Linnet, Lark, and Bul-finchsing, But best, the dear, good Angel of the Spring, The Nightingale. Than why? than why, alone, Should his notes please you? I not long ago took a delight, with wanton kids to play, And sport with little Lambs a Summer's Day! And view their frisks! me thought it was a sight Of joy, to see my two brave Rams to fight! Now Karol, only, all delight doth move! All that is Karol, Karol I approve! This very morning, but— (I did bestow (It was a little 'gainst my will, I know) A single kiss, upon the silly Swain, And now I wish that very kiss again. His lip is softer, sweeter than the Rose His mouth, and tongue with dropping honey flows. The relish of it was a pleasing thing.
