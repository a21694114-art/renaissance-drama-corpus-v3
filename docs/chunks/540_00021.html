What seek ye Lords? What mean these loud Alarms, in the still silence of this hunnied night? King we seek thee. And more we seek thy Crown. Why Princely brothers is it not our own, That 'tis ours we plead the law of kings, The gift of heaven, and the antiquity on earth, Election from them both. We plead our powers & strength, we two must reign. We were borne to rule, and homage we disdain. Do not resign, good King. How saucy Lord? I'll keep still thy Crown. I say that word shall cost old Cornwall's life. Tush this for care. tyrant's good subjects kills and traitors spare. Wilt thou submit thy Crown? Dread sovereign, no. He hates his own life that adviseth so. I hate all traitors, and had rather die, Then see such wrong done to his sovereignty. Give up thy state to these two princely youths, and thy resignment shall preserve thy life. Wilt thou so much wrong both thyself and wife? Hast lived a king, and canst thou die a slave, A royal seat, doth ask a royal grave, Though thousand swords thy present safety ring, Thou that hast been a Monarch, die a king. Whether he live or die, thou sure shalt be no longer Queen, but vassal unto me, I'll make ye now my drudge. How minion, thine? Thart no more Queen, thy husband must resign. Resign, to whom? I am one. And I another. Canst be so base to see a younger brother, Nay two young Boys placed in thy throne of state, And thou their sudden in their trains to wait, I'll die before I endure it. So shall all, that do not prostrate to our homage fall. Shall they not brother king? They shall by heaven. Come kill me first. Nay make the number even, And kill me to, for I am pleased to die, Rather than this endure. The third am I. Nay strike her first. Rage give my fury way. Strike valiant brother king. Yet he are me, stay. Be brief for God's sake then, O heaven, that men so much should covet care, Sceptres are golden baits, the outsides fair: But he that swallows this sweet sugared pill, 'twill make him sick with troubles that grow still: Alas you seek to ease me being wearied And lay my burden on your able loins, My unambitious thoughts have been long tired, With this great charge, and now they rest desired, And see the kind youths coveting my peace, Bring me of all these turmoils free release. Here take my Crown. Wilt thou be made a stale, Shall this proud Woman, and these boys prevail? Shall I for them be made a public scorn, Oh hadst thou buried been, assoon as borne, How happy had I been. Patience sweet wife, Thinkst thou I praise my Crown above thy life, No take it Lords, it hath my trouble been, And for this Crown, oh give me back my Queen. Nay she's bestowed on me. Then what you please, Here take my trouble, and resign your ease.
