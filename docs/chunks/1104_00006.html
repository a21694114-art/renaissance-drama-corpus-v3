I do not counsel that, see him reclaimed first, Which will not prove a miracle, yet Marry I am afraid 'twill vex thee horribly To stay so long. No, no Aunt, no believe me. What was your dream to night? for I observed ye Hugging of me; with good, dear, sweet Tom. Fie Aunt, Upon my conscience. On my word 'tis true wench: And then ye kissed me Marry, more than once too, And sighed, and O sweet Tom again: nay, do not blush, Ye have it at the heart wench. I'll be hanged first, But you must have your way. And so will you too, Or break down hedges for it: Dorothea, The welcomest woman living: how does thy brother? I hear he's turned a wondrous civil gentleman Since his short travel. Pray heaven he make it good Alice. How do ye friend, I have a quarrel to ye, Ye stole away, and left my company. O pardon me, dear friend, it was to welcome A brother, that I have some cause to love well. Prithee how is he? thou speak'st truth. Not perfect: I hope he will be. Never: has forgot me, I hear wench, and his hot love too: Thou wouldst howl then. And I am glad it should be so; his travels Have yielded him variety of Mistresses, Fairer in his eye far. O cogging rascal. I was a fool, but better thoughts I thank heaven. Pray do not think so, for he loves you dearly, Upon my troth most firmly: would fain see you. See me friend? do you think it fit? It may be, Without the loss of credit too: he's not Such a prodigious thing, so monstrous, To fling from all society. His so much contrary To my desires, such an antipathy That I must sooner see my grave. Dear friend, He was not so before he went. I grant it, For than I daily hoped his fair conversion. Come, do not mask yourself, but see him freely, Ye have a mind. That mind I'll master then. And is your hate so mortal? Not to his person, But to his qualities, his madcap follies, Which still like Hydra's heads grow thicker on him. I have a credit friend, and maids of my sort, Love where their modesties may live untainted. I give up that hope then: pray, for your friend's sake, If I have any interest within ye, Do but this courtesy, accept this Letter. From him? The same: 'tis but a minutes reading, And as we look on shapes of painted devils, Which for the present may disturb our fancy, But with the next new object lose 'em, so If this be foul, ye may forget it, 'pray: Have ye seen it friend? I will not lie: I have not, But I presume, so much he honours you, The worst part of himself was cast away When to his best part he writ this.
