Well, give me leave to answer you for that, Were set here to please all customers, Their humours and their fancies:- offend none: We get by many, if we lose by one. May be his mind stood to no more than that, A penworth serves him, and 'mongst trades 'tis found, Deny a pen'orth, it may cross a pound. Oh, he that means to thrive with patient eye, Must please the devil, if he come to buy. O wondrous man, patient 'bove wrong or woe, How blessed were men, if women could be so. And to express how well my breast is pleased, And satisfied in all:- George fill a beaker. I'll drink unto that Gentleman, who lately Bestowed his money with me. God's my life, We shall have all our gains drunk out in beakers, To make amends for penniworths of lawn. Here wife, begin you to the Gentleman. I begin to him. George, fillit up again: 'twas my fault, my hand shook. How strangely this doth show? A patient man linked with a waspish shrew. A silver and gilt beaker! I have a trick to work upon that beaker, sure 'twill fret him, it cannot choose but vex him. Seig▪ Castruchio, in pity to thee, I have a conceit, will save thy 100. Ducats yet, 'twill do't, & work him to impatience. Sweet Fluello, I should be bountiful to that conceit. Well 'tis enough. Here Gentleman to you, I wish your custom, you're exceeding welcome. I pledge you Seig. Candido,- here you, that must receive a 100. Ducats. I'll pledge them deep i'faith Castruchio, signor Fluello? Come: play't off: to me, I am your last man. George, supply the cup. So, so, good honest George, Here signor Candido, all this to you. Oh you must pardon me, I use it not. Will you not pledge me then? Yes, but not that: Great love is shown in little.
