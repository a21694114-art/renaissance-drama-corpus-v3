Sir, he shall yield you all the honour of a competent adversary, if you please to undertake him— I am priest for the encounter. Me? challenge me? What! my Master, sir? 'Slight, Monsieur, meddle with me, do you hear? but do not meddle with my Master. Peace, good squib, go out. And stink, he bids you. Master? Silence, I do accept him. Sit you down, and observe. Me? He never professed a thing at more charges. Prepare yourself, sir. Challenge me? I will prosecute what disgrace my hatred can dictate to me. How tender a travailers spleen is? comparison, to men, that deserve least, is ever most offensive. You are instructed in our chartel, and know our weapons? I appear not without their notice, sir. But must I lose the prizes, Master? I will win them for you, be patient. Lady, vouchsafe the tenure of this ensign. Who shall be your stickler? Behold him. I would not wish you a weaker. Sound musics. I provoke you, at the bare Accost. Excellent comely! And worthily studied. This is th' exalted Foretop. O, his leg was too much produced. And his hat was carried skiruily. Peace; Let's see the monsieurs Accost: Rare! Sprightly, and short. True, it is the french curteau: He lacks but to have his nose slit. He does hop. He does bound too much. The second bout, to conclude this weapon. Good, believe it! An excellent offer! This is called the solemn bandstring. Foe, that cringe was not put home. 'Sfoot, he makes a face like a stabbed LUCRECE. Well, he would needs take it upon him, but would I had done it for all this. He makes me sit still here, like a baboon as I am. Making villainous faces. See, the French prepares it richly. ay, this is yclept the serious trifle. 'sblood, 'tis the horse-start out o' the brown study. Rather the bird-eyed stroke, sir. Your observance is too blunt, sir. judges, award the prize. Take breath, sir. This bout hath been laborious. And yet your Critic, or your Besso'gno, will think these things foppery, and easy, now. Or rather mere lunacy. For, would any reasonable creature make these his serious studies, and perfections? Much less, only live to these ends? to be the false pleasure of a few, the true love of none, and the just laughter of all? We must prefer the Monsieur, we courtiers must be partial. Speak, Guardian. Name the prize, at the bare Accost. A pair of wall-eyes, in a face forced. Give the Monsieur. AMORPHUS hath lost his eyes. I! is the palate of your judgement down? Gentles, I do appeal. Yes master, to me. The judges be fools. How now, sir? Tie up your tongue, Mongrel. He cannot appeal. Say you, sir? Sir you still, sir. Why, so I do. Do not I, I pray you? Remercy, Madame, and these honourable Censors. Well, to the second weapon, The better regard: I will encounter you better. Attempt. Sweet Honour. What says my good Ambition?
