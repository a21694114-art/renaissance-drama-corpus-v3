Mopsa, O insufferable wrong, make thine own natural child thy bawd, here's an excellent pattern for wives to learn to scold by What mistress Amason, ha' you such a cocking spirit, honest Women cannot keep their husbands at home for you: 'tis not for nothing now I see, that the Duchess looks yellow on you, but i'll tear that painted whores face of yours (by this light) and yet I will not scold neither. Madam, i'll mad you with a vengeance. Touch not the prince. On your allegiance forbear, what means this outrage, cannot our private walks be privileged from your wild contentious. how fares the prince. How cheers my good Zelmane? Zelmane, no Gods my judge my liege, I am Manasses, miserable Manasses, your husband's scribe-major madam. Manasses. A fool. My man. And my dear head, alas sweet love, what makest thou here. Mary work for the hangman, and the Duke be not the more merciful. There's some deceit in this, Dametas, where's Hippolita? ay, ay, there's some knavery in this: Mopso where's Hippolita? doubtless there's some villainy in this, Mepso where's Hipolita? there's no plain dealing in this, Manasses where's Dorus? Answer directly, where's Hippolita? Alas madam I know not, whilst I almost melted myself with digging of gold in Diana's oak, I left her in my wives charge And whilst I ran to Manasses, thinking to take my husband & his wife in the manner, I left Hipolita in my daughter's chamber and whilst I came to Adonis' chapel to be tossed in my marriage blankets with Dorus, I left my little dog pearl plucking daisies: Who sent you to Diane's oak to dig gold? who sent you to take your husband in Manasses house? dorus. who sent you to Adonis' chapel. dorus: And who turned you into this shape: They that I fear have made gulls of us all, Zelmane, and dorus: we are all simply gulled, and see where the Sun scarce half ready, skips from his Eastern bed, smiling at our gullery: Come where's this lusty wit-master. the keeper of this love-lottery, This gallant luventus of fourscore, that like my Lady of the Lake, displays against all comers. May a couple of plain witted princes have a sight of your prizes: Where be these Ladies ha? ha your wits had such a skirmishing that the two maids have lost their heads in the conflict. Heads, I and bodies to my Lord, and all at one shot, and which is worse our wits are so scattered with the terrible blow that to be plain we are scarce our own men again. then you have had some knocking, so it appears by the story my Lord: How say you my lady, what owl sings out of that ivy bush was your wit knighted in this last action: I am 〈...〉 ch a fool, I love my lord, I am no knight, I am Manasses, they made a plain fool. the only were, for the guarded fool is out of request: but faith my liege how did your opposites behave themselves, did they win the Wenches fair at the point?
