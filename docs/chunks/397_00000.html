Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Go, walk the horses, wait me on the hill, This is the Hermit's Cell, go out of sight: My business with him must not be revealed, To any mortal creature but himself. I'll wait your honour in the cross highway. Do so: Hermit devout and reverend, If drowsy age keep not thy stiffened joints, On thy unrestful bed, or if the hours Of holy Orisons detain thee not, Come forth. Good morrow son, good morrow, & God bless thee Huntingdon, A brighter Gleam of true Nobility Shines not in any youth more than in thee. Thou shalt be rich in honour, full of speed, Thou shalt win foes by fear, and friend, by meed. Father, I come not now to know my fate, Important business urgeth Princely Richard, In these terms to salute thy reverent age. Read and be brief, I know some cause of trust, Made him employ me for his messenger. A cause of trust indeed true honoured youth, Princes had need in matters of import, To make nice choice fair Earl, if I not err, Thou art the Prince's ward. Father I am his ward, his Chamberlain & bedfellow. Fair fall thee honourable Robert Hood, Wend to Prince Richard, say though I am loath, To use my skill in Conjuration: Yet Skinke that poisoned red cheeked Rosamond, Shall make appearance at the Parliament, He shall be there by noon assure his Grace. Good morrow Father, see you fail him not, For though the villain did a horrible deed, Yet hath the young King Richard, and Earl Iohn, Sworn to defend him from his greatest foes. God's benison be with thee noble Earl. A dew good father, holla there, my horse? Up, spur the kicking jade, while I make speed To Censure Skinke out of his Hermit's weed; Lie there religion keep thy M. grave, And on the fair trust of these Prince's word To Court again Skinke: but before I go, Let mischief take advise of villainy, Why to the Hermit letters should be sent, To post Skinke to the Court incontinent: Is there no trick in this? ha let me see? Or do they know already I am he? If they do so, faith westward then with Skinke: But what an ass am I to be thus fond, Hear lies the Hermit whom I dying found Some two months since, when I was hourly charged With Hugh the Crier and with Constables, I saw him in the ready way to heaven, I helped him forward, 'twas a holy deed; And there he lies some six foot in the ground, Since when, and since, I kept me in his weeds. O what a world of fools have filled my Cell; For Fortunes, runaway, stolen goods; lost cattle, Among the number, all the faction That take the young King's part against the old; Come to myself to hearken for myself,
