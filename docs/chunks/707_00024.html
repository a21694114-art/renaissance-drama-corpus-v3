Man wherefore dost not swear? Reach me a book. Let me before I swear, on my notes look, I'll tell you the very day; pray hold my staff, Till I draw out my false eyes. Draw thy heart out an't wilt: thou mayst well say thy false eyes. The day: August, 14. That's now, be damn, and so away. On this day (August, 14.) I swear I paid Into these hands, 200. crowns in gold. Zounds not in silver: by this book I had none. One of you two is perjuriously forsworn. He, he, as I am true Curstian man. He swears, To your own hands he paid them. Else let that eye, Which sees me play false, scourge my perjury With fearful stripes. O justice I fallen down dead▪ Would I had lost all▪ though I had been cozened, Rather than thou thy soul. He bleeds at mouth. See his staff (beating the earth, for heaven loves truth Is burst in shivers, and that gold he swore paid to me, lies scattered on the flore. He comes again, the devil will not receive him. Take him away, we'll punish him for this crime. Beg his office: you a Courtier? I have a suit to your highness. What be't count Spendola? Master, look up man, In this black trance had thy soul flyen away, I had wrought hard and made a holiday. Lose not a minute (pew-fellow) leave him not yet, I have whales here too, lie playing in the net. I'll take this gold at venture, (sweet young King,) For all this hellhound owes me. Do, and be gone. I am paid: the devils turned Puritan I fear, (methinks) to hear his own child swear. The office of this perjured Bartervile, I frankly give away, dividing it To the Count Spendola, and our worthy friend Brave 〈◊〉 here; farm it to whom you please. We thank your Highness. Who bids most, he buys it. If to his life▪ the devil gives longer lease, To build more work for hell; go see, & from him of what he owes us. That strict account I'll take. Show him no favour. If now thou art a just King, keep thy word, With thy poor subjects. How now uncle? why This is thy day to hear the poor man's cry: And yonder's crying enough, at thy Court gates; Five hundred white heads, and scarce 10, good hats, Yet haberdashers too, of all trades some, Crying out they are undone. Undone, by whom? marry, look: by such as you are, who go gay, wear't out, book down more, set to their hands but never pay; Never in dear years was there such complaining Of poor starved servants, or (when plagues are reigning) Mourn orphans so and widows, as those do That owe these sorrowful papers. Pray how can I To their complainings add a remedy?
