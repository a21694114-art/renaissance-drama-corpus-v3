On my faith I think the moon be at the full For frantic tansyes be then most plenty full Which are at the pride of their spring in your head So far from our matter he is now fled As for the wind in any instrument It is no parcel of our argument We spoke of wind that cometh naturally And that is wind forced artyfycyally Which is not to purpose but if it were And water indeed right nought could do there Yet I think organs no such commodity Whereby the water should banished be And for your bagpyppes I take them as nyfuls Your matter is all in fancies and tryfuls. By god but ye shall not tryfull me of so If these things serve not I will rehearse more And now to mind there is an old proverb come One bushel of marche dust is worth a kings ransom What is a hundredth thousand bushels worth than. not one mite for the thing self to no man. Why shall wind every where thus be object Nay in the high ways he shall take a effect Where as the rain doth never good but hurt For wind maketh but dust and water maketh dirt Powder or syrup sir which lick ye best Who licketh not the tone may lick up the rest But sure who ever hath assayed such syppes Had liefer have dusty eyes then dirty lips And it is said sins afore we were borne That drught doth never make dearth of corn And well it is known to the most fool here How rain hath pryced corn within thy. vii. year. Sir I pray the spare me a lyteell season And I shall privily conclude the with reason Put case on summer's day without wind to be And ragious wind in winter days two or three Much more shall dry that one calm day in summer Then shall those three windy days in winter Whom shall we thank for this when all is done The thank to wind nay thank chiefly the son And so for drought of corn thereby encres The son doth comfort and ripe all doubtless And oft the wind so layeth the corn god wots That never after can it ripe but rot If drought took place as ye say yet may ye see little helpeth the wind in this commodity But now sir I deny your principal If drought ever were it were impossybyll To have any grain for, or it can grow Ye must plough your land harro we and sow Which will not be except ye may have rain To temper the ground and after again For springing and pluming all manner corn Yet must ye have water or all is forlorn If ye take water for no commodity Yet must ye take it for things of necessity For washing for showering all filth cleansing Where water lacketh what bestly being In brewing, in baking, in dressing of meat
