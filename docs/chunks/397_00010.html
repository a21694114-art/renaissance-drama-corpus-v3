A plague upon you for a damned rogue, The Porter of the Fleet will surely know me. Good neighbours bring this honest fellow thither, there's for his pains a crown if he say true, And for your labour there's as much for you. Why Ma Ma Madam, I am Re Re Redcap the Porter's son. Thou hast no wrong in this, farewell good fellow. Best speaking to Prince Richard? no I'll try And face out Redcap if the slave were by. Make them drink Block. Come to the Buttery bar, stitty stitty stammerer, come honest Constable, hay the watch of our town, we'll drink trylill I saith. Lupus in fabula my Noble Lord, See the old fox Sir Richard Faukenbridge. We'll fit him well enough, second us Robin. I'll fit you well enough for all your hope, Leave quaffing sirrah, listen to their talk. O while you live beware, two are sooner seen than one: beside, bear a brain Master, if Block should be now spied, my Madam would not trust this sconce neither in time nor tide. Well leave me, now it buds; see see, they kiss. Adieu good old sinner, you may recover it with a sallet of parsley, and the herb patience, if not sir you know the worst, it's but even this. Madam, what you desire I not deny, But promise Gloster's life and liberty, I beg but love. When doth she give her alms? Fair honourable Prince. Nay then they speed. My soul hath your deserts in good esteem. Witness these goodly times that grace my head. But were you the sole Monarch of the earth, Your power were insufficient to invade, My never yielding heart of chastity. Sayest thou so Mall, I promise thee for this, I'll owe thy cherry lips an old man's kiss; Look how my Cockerill droops, 'tis no matter, I like it best when women will not flatter. Nay but sweet Lady. Nay but gracious Lord, do not so much forget your Princely worth, As to attempt virtue to unchastity. O noble youth!
