GOod morrow. So to you, fair Shepherdess. What news in our Arcadia? I know none: For well you wots it is no news with us, That men should prove inconstant. Think you so? Thought's free. I pray can you define me Thought? Let me bethink myself, I think I can: For I have thought of many things ever now. But can you guess what I think? I (perhaps) May jump with your conceit, come near't at lest. Of colours there are none so opposite As white and black: and of the Elements Than fire and water none more contrary: Nor is there aught so antipathy' de in men, As what they think and speak. Now let me help you: men's thoughts like Courtiers cloaks are often shifted, And change as often as they are truly sifted. This than hath been the cause of women's sorrow; Men think to day ill, to do worse to morrow: Witness Amphrisa's servant. Pity 'tis, ●o fair a body, and so sweet a soul should be so foully dealt with. Her false Lover ●nkindely hath forsook her. That's the reason shee's grown into so deep a melancholy. wonder any woman dare trust man, ●nce, like as the Chameleons change themselves ●nto all perfect colours saving white; they can to all humours frame their speech, ●e only to prove honest. You say well. ●t as no worms breed where they feel no warmth, ●o Vultures watch where they can find no prey; ●o Pirate roves but where he hope's for spoil: none of these false servants wait, but where they find a yielding Mistress. Indeed light minds are caught with little things, ●nd Fancy smells to Fennell. But Amphrisa Is held to be the wisest shepherdess That life's in our Arcadia. But I have herded, Late wit and cheated wisdom to be counted Next neighbours unto folly. shepherds now, The holier that they seem in outward show, The hollower are their hearts. By subtle sophistry (As I have herded) the best Philosophy May be perverted. And men's flatteries Are just like Circe's richeses, which can turn Vain glorious fools to Asses, credulous Fools To Woodcocks, pretty wanton Fools to Apes, And proud Fools into Peacocks. But among these, Amphrisa had no place. See, here she comes That for herself can answer. But 'tis sin In us, not to be answered, thus to suffer her To pale the cheerful blood in her far cheeks, Through wilful passion. Which I'll not endure. Than rouse her from these dumps. You're sad, Amphrisa: Sweet may we know the cause? Amphrisa. You have prevented A strange conceit which somewhat troubled me; But by your interruption almost lost. Nay recollect yourself, pray let us hear't.
