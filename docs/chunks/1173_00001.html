Where is your Governor Hilts? Basquet must do it. Basquet shall be called: Hilts, can you see to rise? Ch'm not blind Sir With too much light. Open your t'other eye, And view if it be day. I can spy that At's little a hole, as another, through a Millstone. He will ha' the last word, though he talk Bilke for't. Bilke? what's that? Why nothing, a word signifying Nothing; and borrowed here to express nothing. A fine device! Yes, till we hear a finer. What's your device now, Cannon Hugh? In private. Lend it your ear; I will not trust the air with it; Or scarce my Shire; my Cassock sha' not know it; If I thought it did, I'll burn it. That's the way, You ha' thought to get a new one, Is't worth it? Let's hear it first. Than harken, and receive it. This 'tis Sir, do you relish it? If Hilts Be close enough to carry it; there's all. It i'no sand? nor Buttermilk? If't be, Ich'am no zive or watering pot, to draw Knots i' your ' casions. If you trust me, so: If not, praforme it your zelves.' Cham no man's wife, But resolute Hilts: you'll vind me i'the Buttery. A testy Clown: but a tender Clown, as wool: And melting as the Wether in a Thaw: he'll weep you, like all April: But he ' ull roar you Like middle March afore: He will be as mellow, And tipsy too, as October: And as grave, And bound up like a frost (with the new year) In Ianuary; as rigid, as he is rustic. You know his nature, and describe it well; I'll leave him to your fashioning. Stay, Sir Hugh; Take a good Angel with you, for your Guide: And let this guard you homeward, as the blessing, To our devise. I thank you Squires-worship, Most humbly (for the next, for this I am sure of.) O for a Choir of these voices, now, To chime in a man's pocket, and cry chink! One doth not chirp: it makes no harmony. Grave Justice Bramble, next must contribute; His charity must offer at this wedding: I'll bid more to the Basin, and the Bride-ale; Although but one can bear away the Bride. I smile to think how like a Lottery These Weddings are. Clay hath her in possession; The Squire he hopes to circumvent the Tile-Kill: And now, if Justice Bramble do come of, 'Tis two to one but Tub may lose his bottom. Why, 'tis thirty year, e'en as this day now: Zin Valentine's day, of all days cursined, look you; And the same day o' the month, as this Zin Valentine, Or I am vowly deceived. That our High Constable, Mr. Tobias Turf, and his Dame were married. I think you are right. But what was that Zin Valentine? Did you ever know 'em, Goodman Clench? Cle. Zin Valentine, He was a deadly Zin, and dwelled at Highgate, As I have heard, but it was avore my time: He was a Cooper too, as you are. Medlay, An In-an-In: A woundy, brag young vellow: As th' port went o'hun, than, and i' those days.
