what'll you do? you ha' done too much already With your prick-seame, and throughstitch. Mr. Needle, I pray you sit not fabling here old tales, Good Mother Chair, the Midwife, but come up. How now Nurse, where's my Lady? In her Chamber Locked up, I think: she'll speak with no body. Knows she o' this accident? Alas Sir, no; Would she might never know it. I think her Ladyship Too virtuous, and too nobly innocent, To have a hand in so ill-formed a business. Your thought Sir is a brave thought, and a safe one, The child now to be borne is not more free, From the aspersion of all spot, than she? She have her hand in plot, 'gainst Mr. Practice. If there were nothing else, whom she so loves? Cries up, and values? knows to be a man Marked out, for a chief Justice in his cradle? Or a Lord Paramount; the head o' the Hall? The Top or the Top-gallant of our Law? Assure yourself, she could not so deprave, The rectitude of her Judgement, to wish you Unto a wife, might prove your Infamy, Whom she esteemed that part o' the Commonwealth, And had up for honour to her blood. I must confess a great beholdingness Unto her Ladyship's offer, and good wishes. But the truth is, I never had affection, Or any liking to this Niece of hers. You foresaw somewhat than? I had my notes, And my Prognostics. You read Almanacs, And study 'em to some purpose, I believe? I do confess, I do believe, and pray too: According to the Planets at sometimes. And do observe the sign in making Love? As in Phlebotomy. And choose your Mistress By the good days, and leave her by the bad? I do, and I do not. A little more Would fetch all his Astronomy from Allestree. I tell you Mr. Compass, as my friend, And under seal, I cast mine eye long since, Vpo' the other wench, my Lady's woman, Another manner of piece for handsomeness, Than is the Niece (but that is sub sigillo, And as I give it you) in hope o' your aid, And counsel in the business. You need counsel? The only famous Counsel, o' the kingdom, And in all Courts? That is a Jeer in faith, Worthy your name, and your profession too, Sharp Mr. Practice. Not, upo' my Law, As I am a Bencher, and now double Reader, I meant in mere simplicity of request. If you meant so. Th'affairs are now perplexed, And full of trouble, give 'em breath, and settling, I'll do my best. But in mean time do you Prepare the Parson. (I am glad to know This; for myself liked the young Maid before, And loved her too.) Ha' you a Licence? Not; But I can fetch one strait. Do, do, and mind The Parson's pint t'engage him— the business; A knitting Cup there must be. I shall do it.
