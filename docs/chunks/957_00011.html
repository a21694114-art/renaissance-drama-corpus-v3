Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

O the ork the huge huntie-puntie. Up cancrone I tell thee we have scaped him. I tell thee Sirocca we have not scaped him, he has eat us up These fishers are new returned from fishing, and know not that Atyches has slain the ork, I'll ork them. Ah Sirocca I would this Ork were in Neptune's belly, that will suffer such a worm to live in his dominions, I am a very macherell if the very name be not worse to me then, three nights cold fishing. methinks I am colder too than I was before. Let me strike then before the iron be key cold What hardy fishers dare approach this shore Untrod by men this twenty years and more. Good now Conchylio do not the Norke we did eat the golden apples; we. What old Cancrone? I am sorry for your chance The best that I advise you is that you return round about the Cape presently before the Ork smell you (if he were within twelve score he might wind them) foh. Nay I shall be devoured. Pluck out a good heart man. If I could do so I might save the ork a labour, that Will be done to my hand I know I shall be devoured. Why man. Why my grandsire was deflowered, and they say deflowering goes in a blood. If I rid you both of this fear will you worship me. O worshipful water-wight. O Neptune's father. O Glaucus' Mother. Why then thus; my deities oracle gives you answer thus When 2 famous fishers fall upon this sand Let them for fear of mighty ork, leave seas sail home by land I have not pinched them for measure I have given them Oracle up to the elbows. Sail there's your office Scrocca, you must go: By land, there's your office go you. What can you not expound. Drag up your boat and homeward cross this shore we are all made, I understood you sir, but I did not know your meaning. Pull you the boat at nose I'll lift at the arse. Manners lack this is a land voyage, I am master. Hoh; roh; droh;, Horka, Corka, Suga ponto; the monster comes down under the boat turn it over I'll help: Retire thou sacred monster (creepe on) These sweet souls are no food for thee (on on) 'Tis time these souls were spent they begin To stink, retire thou great god Neptune's scourge Retire I say while this twin tortoise passes And dare not once to touch these fish flesh asses. Hah, ha, he, farewell good tortoise, what good foutch? haddock Hare and Cod? you shall walk with me I'll be your Ork: yet i'll carry the Cod to my mistress Cosma. I know she loves it well: let Conchilio be turned into an Oyster if he would not play the Ork every day for such sport, it shall go hard but I'll with my friend Cancrone yet once again. Happy happy fisher's swain If that ye knew your happiness Your sport tastes sweeter by your pains, Sure hope your labour relishes Your net your living, when you eat Labour finds appetite and meat.
