Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

My name's Dick Bowyer. Then, master Dick Bowyer, after my hearty commendations, adieu: but as for the rest, I shall, I say no more, I shall— How now, Core? how can you answer your being a tippling, when you should stand Sentinel? Believe me, Captain, I had but a whiff or two: for I was passing dry. Thou art always dry: the whoreson malt-worm has a throat like the burning Climb, or a glass-maker's Furnace: But your remove from thence has saved you from the bolts. How now, what water-spaniel have we here? 'tis Lieutenant Nod. Captain, deride me not: I protest, I came by this mischance by good service, by following a spy, that came to discover our army. O notable Rogue! did not I find thee asleep, and threw thee into a ditch? Was't you? by this light, I took you for a spy. Yet saw me not, no more than a Molewarp: this is an egregious Rogue. Yes, I saw you well enough, and I did but try how you would use me. By this flesh and blood, many one that lies in his grave, was not half so senseless: but the Watch breaks up, every one to his quarter, away. 'tis true, they are gone together, and I am sent to watch Prince Ferdinand, and the Earl of Pembroke: this way they went; but they are got out of sight. I were very bad to be a Hound, that have no better a sent: if they were hares, as they are men, I should think them squatted: but hushed, here comes one of them. The morn look red: red morns do threat a storm, That storm shall light on Pembroke or myself. This is our meeting place: here runs the stream That parts our camps: the time consorts the place; And (Pembroke) it thy reputation hold, It is thy q. to enter. Here comes the other, this is luck upon luck: now will I run and fetch my mistress, the Lady Katharine, to part their fray. Good end succeed my early heaviness: Three times my feet, as loath to guide me hither, Have stumbled in a plain and even way, My sword forsook his scabbard once or twice, Blood from my nostrils thrice hath spouted forth, And such a dimness overruns my sight, That I have ta'en a tree to be a man, And questioned with it about serious things. This is the place where I must meet my friend. Yonder he stands. Good morrow, Ferdinand. Good morrow to thy death: draw, Pembroke, draw, The ground thou treadest upon, must be thy grave. Draw upon Ferdinand? ay, upon me. Dally not, Pembroke, I am bent to fight, And that with thee for the best blood thou bearest. You have some reason for this resolution. My will.
