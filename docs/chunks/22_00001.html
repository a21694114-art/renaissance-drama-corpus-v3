Which seen in themself and further considering The same to the redress was cause of their assemble And also that we evermore being Beside one puissant power of deite Of wisdom and nature so noble and free From all extremities the mean dividing To peal and plente each thing attempering. They have in conclusion wholly sundered Into our hands as much concerning All manner of wethers by them engendered The full of their powers for term everlasting To set such order as standeth with our pleasing Which things of our part no part required But of all their parts right humble desired. To take upon us whereto we did assent And so in all things with one voice agreeable We have clearly finished our foresaid parleament To your great wealth which shallbe firm and stable And to our honour far inestimable For sing their powers as ours added to our own Who can we say know us as we should be known. But now for fine the rest of our intent Wherefore as now we hither are descended. Is only to satisfy and content All manner people which have been offended By any wether meet to be amended Upon whose complaints declaring their grief We shall shape remedy for their relief. And to give knowledge for their hither resort We would this afore proclaimed to be To all our people by some one of this sort Whom we list to choose here amongst ally Wherefore each man advance and we shall see Which of you is most meet to be our eryer. Brother hold up your torch a little higher Now I beseech you my lord look on me first I trust your lordship shall not find me the worst. Why what art thou that approachest so nigh. Forsooth and please your lordship it is I. All that we know very well, but what I. What I, some lay I am I pierce I But what manner I so ever be I I assure your good lordship I am I. What manner man art thou show quickly. By god a poor gentleman dwelleth her by. A gentleman thyself bringeth witness nay Both in thy light behaviour and array But what art thou called where thou dost resort. Forsooth my lord master Merry report. Thou art no meet man in our business. For thine appearance is of to much lightness. Why can not your lordship like my name Mine apparel nor my name neither. To none of all we have devotion. A proper lycklyhod of promotion Well than as wise as ye seem to be Yet can ye see no wisdom in me But sins ye dispraise me for so light an elft I pray you give me leave to praise myself And for the first part I will begin In my behaviour at my coming in Wherein I think I have little offended For sewer my courtesy could not be amended
