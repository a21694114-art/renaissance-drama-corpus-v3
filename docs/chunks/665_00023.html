Five believe me Sir. Five of one, oh devil! what limb of him but a complete Villain, A tongue profaner than Idolatry: His eye a Beacon, fixed in his place: Discovering ills, but hood winked unto grace, Her heart a nest of vice, kept by the Devil, His good is none at all, his all, is evil, Oh the father, Gallants, yonder's the most hard favoured news walks the streets, seven men going to their graves that died with drinking and bisseling. Good still, nay, than I see the devil has some power over a woman more than a man, seven! t'wll be more anon. Now I beseech Bacchus my puppy has not over seen himself. This is very strange. And as true a report I assure you. Out all as, where's my Gosip? oh woman! have you not heard the news? Yes, I have heard on't. Oh woman, did your child's child ever see the like, nine men to be buried too day, that drunk healths last night. Better and better, goodness never mends so fast in the carrying: nine! They say one is your guest Philantus, And all I dare swear, whom i'll revive again Well, he was a proper man i'faith. I, and had good skill in pricksong, yet he had a fault in his humour, as none are without (but Puritans:) he would swear like an Elephant, and stamp and stare (God bless us) like a playhouse book-keeper, when the actors miss their entrance. Nay hark ye sir, I can brook much injury, but not that, meddle with me, but not with my trade, she is mine own, she's meus, tuus, suus, no man's else, I assure ye we are sure together. Sure ye are together sir, but is your wife, your trade? you mean to live upon your wife then. The fool has some wit though his money be gone. Sir, I hope ye are not offended, I assure ye would be loath to offend the least hair of your caput, sissiput, or occiput. Occiput: what mean you by occiput? The former part of your head. The former part of your head, why I hope I have not an occiput, in the former part of my head, signor Sernulus, what means be by it? The signification of the word only a mounts to this, the former part of your head. The fool is jealous, prithee feed it. 'Slid I cannot be so sussified, I pray you signor what means he by occuput? No hurt veriely, only, the word signifies, and the reason is (saith Varro) being a great deriver from originals it is called occiput, for that the former part of the head looks likest the Ox. Likest the Ox, by gad, if ere I come to talk with that Varro, i'll make him show a better reason for it. But howsoever, it proceeded from me all in kindness.
