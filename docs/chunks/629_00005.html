Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

This same strange thing i'th' likeness of a woman, Tastes of much wit, though I not love her sex, I'll arm my thoughts to crack a jest with her. What, gravelled lulio? No, but run a Land: Is your wit's shipping any better manned? Yes, will you board it? No, I dare not venture: Make but a shot in jest and you may enter. You are a Scholar. I have seen some Schools. You came not over i' the last fleet of Fools. You took my room up. I pray take't again, we'll have no women fools sail amongst men. Your wit's much currish. Why 't bites not you, It feeds on fools flesh, so wisemen adieu Please you accept the curtsy of the town? I need not, I have curtsies of mine owns there's one for you. How chance your wit's so free? Only to outgo jadish company. Here are none such. Take heed, for if you tire, she'll keep her pace and leave you in the mire. A woman's feature, but a scholars tongue In quick discourse, Philosophers near wondered More at the strange conception of the winds, Than I admire how she attained this wit: Did not true learning make the soul divine, She hath spoke enough to make me convertine. My loves are sound, & wait but your reply, A short lined accent, either no or I. I am not too severe, nor yet so kind, To fall for every idle puff of wind. But farewell, i'll take counsel of my pillow, Pity fresh youth should wither in green willow. Appoint the place sweet, i'll not miss mine houte. At the three fools. I'll meet. And make up four. Sweet words, kind looks, what? and a parting kiss. Words, looks and lips cry all, the wench is his. I am possessed devil, love persuades my mind, That if to him, to me she'll prove more kind. What's Iulio made of? hadst thou soul or sense, Thou wouldst not prentice thy affections, Nor tie thy fortunes to a stranger's love. A little liking my Lord, a jerk a trick or so, but no pure love I protest, but be impartial, cast of the furred-gown of hate, and speak out of the naked Doublet and hose of judgement: is she not worthy to be beloved? nay, might not she and I live passing well together? Yes, if to live in bondage be no hell, I think you two, might do exceeding well. Well my Lord, because i'll be no example of self-will, i'll break off our meeting at the three Fools, and send for her to Court, where i'll put all my love into one quart of Maligo, & your melancholy humour into another, and he that hath done last, shall for penance give her a kick a the lips, and a pipe of Tobacco be my witness, that's all the love I bear her.
