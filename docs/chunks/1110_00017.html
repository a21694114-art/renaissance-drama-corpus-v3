Two Friars shut from their Covents, wanting harbour, Begged lodging with such importunity They would not be denied; at length I pitied them, And yet to make them sure, I locked them fast Up in the Straw-loft, and see, here's the Key, theyare fast enough for starting. Dost thou know them? friar Bernard, and friar Iohn. friar Bernard, and friar Iohn? th'only good Lads That I desired to meet with; I beshrew thee They had no better welcome; go, unlock, Entreat them hither, we will have one cup To th'health of all their Covent. Call them up? what time shall we to bed then? Time enough, early betimes, nay, quick Nan, friar Iohn there? The bonniest Lad e'er wore portace in a string, Or mumbled Mass, or Matins; I but wished And see, 'tis come to pass; the other to, Though he be strict of life, yet will sometimes Be merry, if he like his company; But my kind honest baldpate, Friar Jack, I shall be glad to see him; welcome faith, With reverence, Father, to your gravity, Be not offended if Friar Iohn and I Be, not exceeding compass, a little merry, And play with the Pot; fill some in. To your bellies pots and all. Excess, my Host, can purchase no excuse, But modest mirth, transgressing not his true And lawful bounds, is good and commendable; Now give us leave to bid you welcome home. Thanks good Friar Bernard, Now as I live, would I had some good cheer, No better than I would pay for; but she tells me There's nothing to be compassed. The worse luck; and yet I smell a supper. Not so much as a cantle of Cheese, or crust of bread, That can this night be come by (for your throats, I'll rather see you choked.) But any fare that would content the stomach. Say you so? I'll try what I can do. What a leering eye the Friar cast towards the Cupboard? Bid my Dame provide Cloth, Salt, and Trenchers. But Friar Iohn, how shall we come by meat? For such provision trust to my Art. By Art, can that be done? Yes, by Art Magic. Oh profane, and fearful! Art Magic! didst thou ever study that? It is against our Order. But not as I will order it, fear it not. Can Friar Iohn Conjure? For a Supper, or so, but never durst deal further; Will you furnish a Table fit for meat? Nan, when I say? We shall have now some fooling. What doth my Novice mean? Good Father peace, no hurt to you nor me. Nor any here? That's furnished with a stomach. Doemones, et Cacodoemones conjuro vos, surgite et venite. No man stir, is the Cloth laid, and all things fit for meat? You see to please you, we can do any thing. Now let me see, What Christian Climate yields us the best bread? Oh, the best wheat's in Spain; what say you now To a couple of Cheat Loves baked in Madrid, And brought into this Chamber?
