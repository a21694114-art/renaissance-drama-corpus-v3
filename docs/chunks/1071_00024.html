Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Not all this while once see me. His occasions, perhaps enforce his absence. His occasions: Unless he find occasion of new Love. What could enforce such absence from his spouse: A I growne fowle and blacke, since my espousals. It should not seem so; For the shop is daily, Customed with store of Chapmen, such as come To cheapen Love. O no, I am myself? But Chartly he is changed. You know that Gentleman. Escape him if thou canst. He cannot, I arrest you; At whose suit. Not at mine own, that's dashed, I love thee not. Thou art a Spaniard, Gipsy, a mere Blackamoor: Again, I say I love thee not. A blackamoor, a Gipsy? Sure I am changed indeed, and that's the cause My Husband left me so, this Gentleman Once termed me beautiful, how look I Ioseph. As well as ere you did, fat, fresh, and fair. You lie boy, pocket that, and now be gone. And what shall then become of my Mistress. I'll wait upon your Mistress. I know you will not wait on such a Gipsie. Yes Luce on such a Gipsie: Boy, abi abi. Abide fir, you need not fear that I have no purpose to leave her. Now you are going to the wedding-house. You are bid to be a Bride maid, are you not. What wedding sir, or whose? Why Chartley's; Luce hath he been thy friend so long, and would not bid thee to write on his Bride. Why look'st thou red and pale, and both, and neither. To Mr. Chartley's Bridals, why, to whom, Should he be married. To Grace of Gracious street. To Gratiana? Beshrow you sir you do not use me well, To buzz into mine ears these strange untruths: I tell you sir, 'tis as impossible That they two should match: as Earth and Heaven to meet. You'll not believe it, pray then hark within, The Nuptial music echoing to their joys. But you give credit to no certainties: I told you but a tale, a lie, a fable? A monstrous, a notorious idle untruth, That you were black, and that I loved you not. And you could credit that. Who's telltruth now. Know you that man, or know you that fine Virgin: Whom by the arm he leads. I'll not endure't: Heaven give you joy sir: I thank you: Luce? Look to the Maid she faints. Grace, come not near her Grace. Father keep off, on Gentlemen apace. she's troubled with the falling sickness, for Oft hath she fallen before me. Nay if it be no otherwise, on gentlemen. Let those with her strive to recover her. Keep off, the disease is infectious: If it were in a man, it were nothing, but the falling sickness in a woman is dangerous. My other father in Law, now shall I be utterly shamed, If he assure to know me, I'll out face him.

A Blackemore, a Gipsy? Sure I am chang'd indeed, and thats th cause My Husband left mee so, this Gentleman Once tearmd mee beautifull, how looke I Ioseph.
