O to court? farewell. If you see one in a yellow taffeta doublet, cut upon carnation valour, a green hat, a blue pair of velvet hose, a gilt rapier, and an orange tawny pair of worsted silk stockings, that's I, that's I. Very good, farewell. Ho, you shall know me as easily, I ha' bought me a new green feather with a red sprig, you shall see my wrought shirt hang out at my breeches, you shall know me. Very good, very good, farewell. Marry in the mask 'twill be somewhat hard. But if you hear anybody speak so wittily, that he makes all the room laugh; that's I, that's I. Farewell good signor. Advance the music's prize, now capering wits, Rise to your highest mount; let choice delight Garland the brow of this triumphant night. 'sfoot, a sits like Lucifer himself. Good sweet Duke, first let their voices, strain for musics price. Give me the golden harp: faith with your favour, i'll be umpiress. Sweet niece content: boys clear your voice & sing. By this gold, I had rather have a servant with a short nose, and a thin hair, then have such a high stretched minikin voice. Fair niece, your reason? By the sweet of love, I should fear extremely that he were an Eunuch. Spark spirit, how like you his voice? Spark spirit, how like you his voice? So help me, youth, thy voice squeaks like a dry cork shoe: come, come; let's hear the next. Trust me, a good strong mean, Well sung my boy. Hold, hold, hold: are ye blind, could you not see my voice coming for the harp. And I knock not division on the head, take hence the harp, make me a slip, and let me go but for nine pence. Sir Mark, strike up for master Balurdo. judgement gentlemen, judgement. Wast not above line? I appeal to your mouths that heard my song. Do me right, and dub me knight Balurdo. Kneel down, and i'll dub thee knight of the golden harp. Indeed la, do, and i'll make you Lady of the silver fiddlestick, Come, kneel, kneel. My troth, I thank you, it hath never a whistle in't. Nay, good sweet cuz raise up your drooping eyes, & I were at the point of To have & to hold, from this day forward, I would be ashamed to look thus lumpish. What my pretty coz, 'tis but the loss of an odd maidenhead: shall's dance? thou art so sad, hark in mine ear. I was about to say, but i'll forbear. I come, I come, more than most honeysuckle sweet Ladies, pine not for my presence, i'll return in pomp. Well spoke sir Ieffrey Balurdo. As I am a true knight, I feel honourable eloquence begin to grope me already. Faith, mad niece, I wonder when thou wilt marry?
