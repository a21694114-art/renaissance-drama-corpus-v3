Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Nay let him come between two and three cozen, for we love to sit long at dinner i'th' City. Come sweet cousin. Nay cozen, keep your way cozen, Good cousin I will not i'faith cozen. Why how now sirrah, upon twelve of the clock, & not the cloth laid yet— must we needs keep Exchange time still. I am about it forsooth. You're about it forsooth; you're still about many things, but you near do one well, I am an Ass to keep thee inh' house now my husbands at sea, thou hast no audacity with thee, a foolish dreaming lad, fitter to be in the garret, then in any place else, no grace, nor manly behaviour; when didst thou ever come to me, but with thy head hanging down, oh decheerefull prentice, uncomfortable servant: pray heaven the Gull my cousin has so much wit left, as to bring master Taylbee along with him; my comfort, my delight, for that was the chiefest cause I did invite him, I bade him bring what Gentlemen he pleased to accompany him, as far as I durst go, why may he not then make choice of master Taylbee, had he my wit or feeling he would do't. where's my sweet cousin here, does she lack any guess? Ever such guess as you, you're welcome cozen. I am rude Lady. You're most welcome sir. There will be a gallant here anon coz, He promised faithfully. Who is't master Taylbee? Master Taylbee, no master Goldstone. mayst Goldstone; I could think well of that Goldstone, wert not for one wild trick he has. What's that Lady? In jest, he will pawn his punc es for suppers. That's a wild part in him i'faith, & he were my brother. Pray gentlemen sit a while, your dinner shall come presently. Ye mistress Newcut, at first give me a trip, A close bite always asks a secret nip. My cousin here is a very kind natured soul i'faith, in her humour. Puh you know her not so well as I coz, I have observed her in all her humours you near saw her a little waspish I think. I have i'faith. Puh, then yea near saw pretty humour in your life, I can bring her into't when I list. Would you could i'faith. Would I could? by my troth and I were sure thou couldst keep thy countenance coz, what a pretty jest have I thought upon already to entertain time before dinner. preach coz what is't, I love a jest a life i'faith. Ah but I am jealous you will not keep your countenance i'faith. Why yea shall see a pretty story of a humour. Faith i'll try you for once, you know my cousin will wonder when she comes in to see the cloth laid and near a salt upon the board. That's true i'faith. Now will I stand a while out of sight with it, and give her humour play a little.
