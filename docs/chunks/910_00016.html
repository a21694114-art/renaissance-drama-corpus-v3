Then here lies the point; That as thou go'st with her unto the fount, Thou use thy best cunning to make her coming, And hear Aminta speak; mean time my care Shall be to make Aminta meet you there. I fear my task will be the hardest of both. Onn then o' God's name. Yes, I go; but Thirsis, We were discoursing of an other matter. If mine eye fail me not, yond same should be Aminta, that comes hitherward; 'tis he. NOw shall I see what Thirsi' has done for me; And if he have done nothing, ere my woes Melt me'into nothing, I'll go kill myself Before the proud face of that cruel maid, That so delights to see my hearts deep wound Made by her murdering eyes, as sure it can Please her no less, to see her sad command Fulfilled on my own breast with my own hand. News, news Aminta, happy news I bring thee; Clear then thy brow, and cast thy griefs away. What is't thou sayest Thirsis, what bringst thou me, Life, or death? new joy, or new misery? I bring thee life and joy, if thou but dare To go and meet them; but I tell thee true Thou must not faint but play the man Aminta. Why against whom should I advance my force? Suppose the Nymph thou lov'st were in a wood That (wal'de about with mountains of sharp briers) Were full of Tigers, and of greedy Lions, Wouldst thou go thither? Yes, more cheerfully, Then village-lasse to the dance o'holly days. Were she engaged 'mongst troops of armed thieves, Wouldst thou go thither? Yes more greedily, Then runs the thirsty Hart to the cool streeme. O but a harder task asks greater labour. Why I would pass through the devouring torrents, When the dissolu'de snows down the mountain's rain: And headlong run t'ingulph them in the main: Or through the fire; or indeed down to hell; If any place a hell may termed be, That shall contain so heavenly a thing as she. But prithee tell me all. Here then. Say on. Silvia ' at a Fount, starnaked, and all alone Attends thy coming; darest thou now go thither? Silvia? and all alone? and stays for me? Yes all alone, unless haply there be Daphne ', who thou knowst is all in all for thee. (ah do not Naked? I, naked, but. But what? Mangle me thus. Why but she does not know That you should meet her there; though (as I say) she'll there attend you, do but haste away. Bitter conclusion; that infects, and poisons What ever sweet thy former speeches promised, Why with such art Dost thou delude me, cruel as thou art? Is't not enough Thinkest thou for me thus full of grief to be, But thou must come to mock my misery? Be ruled by me Aminta, and be happy; What should I do?
