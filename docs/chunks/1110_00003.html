Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

'Twas well scented, Nor shalt thou lose by't: there's a share for thee; Acquaint me with the further Circumstance, In which I would be perfect. I do not like this shuffling. What Roger, all amort, methinks thouart off o'th' hooks? Yes faith, and Henges too, I'm almost desperate, And care not how I am. Faith I was never on a merrier pin, Nor my breast lighter hearted. More Rogue thou. Ha? So, and if thou be'st not pleased with that, Thou must prepare for worse. Thou wilt not swagger, Hodge? Faith scarce with thee, thou art not worth my anger Are we not of one House? Yes, but I hope we ne'er shall be of one heart, My fellow no better than a Pander? Pander? Yes, would that were all, a Traitor to that House Where thou hadst thy first Bread, and almost thy being; Thy making I am sure, but ne'er that poison That swells so in thy bosom: My good old Master, Heaven rest his soul, uprising, and down lying, Kept twenty stout tall fellows, whom thy baseness Hath shrunk to two already; if this Riot To which thou still persuadest the young man hold, We shall be none, he nothing. I could answer thee, but that I spy two Gentlemen, With whom I have business of my Masters. That's the man. And to me none more welcome. Is it done? All's plotted, we want money, sell we must, The rate we live at must have fresh supply; I'll give you the whole project. Get you together, for a true-love knot Tied by the Devil, may his Dam unloose it, For I shall never: a Scrivener, an Usurer, and a Pimp, All joined in one, to ruin a young hopeful Gentleman. Now are they turning an old three men's Song, We be knaves all three, mount your notes aloft; For he that I named last will bear the burden; A load best fitting him, you'll find him one day A fellow of excellent carriage, he'll bear with you, Because he loves you so: So now all's plotted And there's a Lordship, or a Manor at least Gone at one lift: but what to do with money, Of which the least is like to come to him That hath most interest in't; some to the Pot, Part to the Pipe, not least share to the smock: And what remains to gaudify the back, Must fill the cheater's pocket; oh my old Master, Should thy soul see this, it would suffer for't, As my heart bleeds to think of't. But did she break so with you? Worse than a shopkeeper, that hath Run long behind hand. Slight you so? S'foot, like a puff, a Feather; 'tis my Lord Must deal in wholesale with her Lady ware, And I am quite cashiered. Troth I commend you, that, so loving her, You do not take't to heart.
