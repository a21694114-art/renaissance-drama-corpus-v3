Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

I would my brother had been better advised, than to give his Land to a Prodigal. Fitter indeed the right Heir should have had it: you might do well to turn your compassion upon him: a poor injured Gentleman, and stands equal in your blood. No I'll marry a wife, and get an heir of mine own, I have made a motion to Master Little good the Usurer, about one of his daughters, and we are partly agreed, I am going to ask her good will in it. Look you sir. Pray stand by, here he comes with his train. Is it not well resolved Captain? Yes by the soul of Hercules, 'tis a good foresight, to sell all and prevent misfortunes. The world's full of uncertainties: Land may be barren, servants deceitful, make money I say, & what a man spends with his friends, shall ne'er perish. I say by the heart of valour, that man lives best at ease, that has no money at all. What shall he do then Lieutenant? By the faith of a soldier, for the exercise of his wits he may do any thing: if all trades fail he may turn Pimp, 'tis a noble profession to live by, if he can perform that office well, he need ask no more of his Genius. Body of me, nor no better preferment. As I am a sinner 'tis a good science, a mathematical mystery of undermining holds, and when the breach is open, be the first man that shall enter. But I think there be so many of them, they can hardly live one for another. As I am virtuous 'tis grown into credit, and you have very good men that study it. Good Knights and Squires that have thrived by it. Stab me, what sullen Saturn is that, looks so oblique upon us, as I am Martial I will confront his aspect. Good Captain be appeased, it is my Uncle, I can not avoid him: let me entreat your absence for a while, meet me at the horse-shoe. Fire of my blood you shall rule me: come Lieutenant. Shall I speak or hold my peace? e'en which you please, good Uncle. I 'tis all one to you, for any impression I shall make: would I could refrain to take notice of thee, but still nature over sways me, and affection breaks out into counsel but to no purpose. Troth uncle youth will have his swing. I upon a Gallows, if you hold on, that will be the end of you. That I should live to see my brother's goods so misspent, the life of his labours sucked out by such Horseleeches. Horseleeches, do you know what you say? no, you do not apprehend the worth that dwells in these men: to see how a man may be mistaken in the distinction of virtue. Nay sir, 'tis as I told you, you may as soon recall an arrow when 'tis flying, or a stone from precipice, as reclaim him.
