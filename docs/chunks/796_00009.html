Seruiteur, life of my life, 'tis out: Be it what it will, stand on thine even feet; Then gloves and garters both are quickly won. Brother, you have lost. Then know I who must win. I'll not believe you, I will have it scanned, Let them that know speak true: is it good French. je suis vostre tres-humble seruiteur. The verse is true, consisting of five feet; The case is plain in common law: no book Can save you: Sergeant thou hast won; the garters They are thine. Younker, 'tis well; and your gloves too; Both gloves and garters, they are fairly won; Scratch not your head, it's but a Iacobin At most; Come let us in to Flutterkins, A cup and toast will do me now no hurt. Why then farewell, I care not I for Beer, My Captain gave me some Canary wine: The Churl he shall not ride the Gentleman. You shall not flinch, if that your cap be wool, You shall along; What, would you leave us so; Turned mitcher, that were not tolerable. I will go with thee, do but promise me, Rightly to construe those lines of Ovid's, Which thou readst but now. I will, then let us hence; I thank great Ioue that blessed me so this day; How others speed, I bear the prize away. This way he went, and here they were ere while, But now they are fled like birds that cut the air, With clipping wings, and leave no trace behind. But Hans my nose is quick and sharp of scent, Like those great Bears in nova Zembla found, Whose smelling sense was better than their sight. I cannot see, but smell where they are gone. Whether dost thou think? straight to Flutterkins, My jovial Host that longs to see my face, My beauteous face, my proper phisnomy; I soon dispatched, sold all my corn at once, And bought my meat. As straight as Circe's wand, Not looking back, as oft Meander doth, What of Meander? now my things be done, I care not, i'll go where thou wilt; let's on; But what's Meander? man, or maid, or wife. A river fool, didst never see a play, Or hear these verses which I have by heart. What's he that talketh of the banks of Poe, And of the milk-white swans that in Meander swam I'll down from hence and scour the Stygian lake: To raise a fiend shall make his soul to quake. O terrible, my Mistress sent you that, And bid you drink it, not to hoard it up. An English shilling: heigh; did she indeed? Why widgeon, thinkst thou I would give thee't else? I'll drink her health upon my bended knees, Until the Welkin roar, and ground looks blue; Two shillings have I, not one penny spent; I bless my stars, good fortune set me free; This days mine own.
