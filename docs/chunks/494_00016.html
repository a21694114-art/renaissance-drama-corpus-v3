Who's there? Dick Bowyer? Some call me so, what then? Pembroke salutes thee. Oh, good morrow, my Lord, good morrow. I prithee, Captain, sawst thou Ferdinand, Son to Navarre, as thou didst walk the round? even now, my Lord, he passed along this way. Himself alone? or had he company? Nay questionless, he was alone, my Lord. Couldst thou discern his face? how did he look? Faith, scurvily, my Lord, like a green cheese, or the inside of a rotten pompion. There is Crowns for thee to drink. I thank your Lordship. To see the difference between these French Curs, and our English Cavaliers: there's as much bounty in them, as there's Marchpane in a dish of Almond butter. I might have stood here till my teeth chatter in my head, ere the other lancepresado would have said, Here, Captain Bowyer, there's a cardecue, to wash down melancholy. But had I known as much, I would have basted him, till his bones had rattled in his skin. Come, sir, you shall answer your walking before our Captain. Well, sirs, take heed what you do, I am a PRINCE's man, if you stay me upon the Kings high way, I can lay fellowship to your charge. But sirrah, we can lay Treason to thine, for being without the word. Without the word! O pernicious Frenchman! without the word! why, I have called thee Villain, him Rascal; this, Slave; that, Rogue; and am I still without the word? ay, sir, the word that must serve your turn, the Watchword. Faith, you're like to watch this twelvemonth, ere you have any other words at my hands. How now masters, what calf are you dragging to the slaughterhouse there? ha? A straggler, and a spy: Captain, I pray examine him. So, Lieutenant Core, you are crept from your cups at last, I'll talk with you anon: but, sirrah, to you: From whence come you? I came, sir, from the King of France's camp. So, what's your name? My name, sir, is Bow wow. S'hart, what a name's that! the Hedgehog mocks us: Bow wow, quotha? what kin art thou to the generation of Dogs? No dog, sir, would you should know it, though I be encompassed with curs. Zounds, he calls us curs, hang the hodge-podge up in a fathom or two of match. Not you, sir, I call not you so: I know you to be a very insufficient ill-spoken Gentleman. Well, sirrah, whom do you serve? My master, sir, is the Lady Katharine, the French king's daughter: I have been abroad about some business of hers, and am now going back again. An honourable Lady, sir: let him go: 'tis against the law of arms to stay him. Stand of: but soft, I do not know your name, sir, that my Lady may give you thanks.
