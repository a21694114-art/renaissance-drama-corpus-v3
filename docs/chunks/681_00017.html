Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Enough, ere long, the air shall ring with shrieks. And sad lament of those, whom Horror strikes. Horror adieu, These three, are Ushers to our Deity, Only vast Ruin here attends on us, And is a follower of our high designs: Ruin thou faithful servant to grim War, Now teach thy murdering shot to tear men's limbs, Thy brazen Cannos how to make a breach, In a fair cities bosom; teach thy fierce To climb the tops of houses; and thy mines, To blow up Churches in th'offended sky. Consume whole groves and standing fields of Corn In thy wild age, and make the proud earth groan, Under the weight of thy confusion. This and much more shall Ruin execute. meanwhile we'll steep our sinewy feet in blood And dance unto the Music of the field, Trumpets for trebles, bases, bellowing drums. Broils Envy bred, but War shall end those brawls, Deaf war that will not hear a word of Peace: Sharp pikes shall serve for subtle lawyers pens The merchants silks shall turn to shining steel, In steed of false-yard sticks, large horsemen's staves, Shall measure out true patterns of their graves. Sirrah what set you up there? Text bills for Plays. What Plays in time of Wars? hold sirrah there's a new plot. How many mean you shall come in for this? Player 'tis press money. Press money, press money, alas sir press me, I am no fit Actor for th' action. Text bills must now be turned to Iron bills. And please you let them be dagger pies. Sir Oliver's men; the last Players took the towns reward like honest men. Those were a couple of coney-catchers that Cousin Majors, and have no consort but themselves, But we are a full company, and our credit with our Master known. meanwhile there's press-money, for your reward, No (I thank your worship) we mean not to trouble your town at this time. Well Masters, you that are master-sharers, Must provide you upon your own purses, Alas sir, we Players are privileged, 'tis our Audience must fight in the field for us, And we upon the stage for them. Sir as concerning halfe a score angels Or such a matter for a man in my place. Those days are out of date. The more's the pity sir, Well, I have a Brewer to my Ingle, he'll furnish me with a horse great enough. Faith I'll e'en past all my ballads together, And make a coat to hold out pistol-proof; I marvel what use I should make of my Ingle, The hobby-horse-seller. Faith make him sell a whole troop of horse To buy thee one. Sirs, if these soldiers light upon our playing 'parel, they'll strut it in the field, and flaunt it out. Well sirs, I have no stomach to these wars, Faith, I have a better stomach to my breakfast. A shrewd morning's work for Players,
