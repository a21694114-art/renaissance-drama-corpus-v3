Come, Sir, here's a place for you to preach in. Will you put in your leg? That I will, cheerfully. O' my conscience a Seminary! Well my Masters, I'll leave him with you; now I see him bestowed, I'll go look for my goods, and Numps. You may, Sir, I warrant you; where's the t'other Bawler? fetch him too, you shall found 'em both fast enough. In the midst of this tumult, I will yet be the Author of mine own rest, and not minding their fury, sit in the stocks, in that calm, as shall be able to trouble a Triumph. Do you assure me upon your words? may I undertake for you, if I be asked the question; that you have this warrant? What's this fellow, for God's sake? Do but show me Adam Overdoo, and I am satisfied. He is a fellow that is distracted, they say; one Trouble-all: he was an officer in the Court of Pie-poulders, here last year, and put out on his place by justice Overdoo. Ha! Upon which, he took an idle conceit, and's run mad upon't. So that ever since, he will do nothing, but by justice Overdoo's warrant, he will not eat a crust, nor drink a little, nor make him in his apparel, ready. His wife, Sirreverence, cannot get him make his water, or shifted his shirt, without his warrant. If this be true, this is my greatest disaster! how am I bound to satisfy this poor man, that is of so good a nature to me, out of his wits! where there is no room left for dissembling. If you cannot show me Adam Overdoo, I am in doubt of you: I am afraid you cannot answer it. Before me, Neighbour Bristle (and now I think on't better) justice Overdoo, is a very parantory person. O! are you advised of that? and a severe justicer, by your leave. Do I hear ill o''at side, too? He will sit as upright o'the bench, an you mark him, as a candle i'the socket, and give light to the whole Court in every business. But he will burn blue, and swell like a boil (God bless us) an he be angry. I, and he will be angry too, when his list, that's more: and when he is angry, be it right or wrong; he has the Law on's side, ever. I mark that too. I will be more tender hereafter. I see compassion may become a justice, though it be a weakness, I confess; and nearer a vice, than a virtue. Well, take him out o' the stocks again, we'll go a sure way to work, we'll ha' the Ace of hearts of our side, if we can. Come, bring him away to his fellow, there. Master Busy, we shall rule your legs, I hope, though we cannot rule your tongue.
