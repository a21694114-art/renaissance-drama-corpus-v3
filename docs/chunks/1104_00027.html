Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

If this be true, thou little tyny page This tale that thou tell'st me Then on thy back will I presently hang A handson new Levery: But if this be false, thou little tyney page As false it well may be Then with a cudgel of four foot long I'll beat thee from head to toe. Will the boy come. He will sir. Time tries all ther. Here he comes now himself sir. To be short Thomas Because I feel a scruple in my conscience Concerning the demeanour, and a main one And therefore like a Father would be satisfied, Get up to that window there, and presently Like a most complete Gentleman, come from Tripoli. Good Lord sir, how are you misled: what fancies (Fitter for idle boys, and drunkards, let me speak't And with a little wonder I beseech you) Choke up your noble judgement? You Rogue Launcelet, You lying rascal. Will ye spoil all again sir. Why, what a devil do you mean? Away knave, Ye keep a company of saucy fellows Deboshed, & daily drunkards, to deavoure ye, Things, whose dull souls, tend to the Cellar only, Ye are ill advised sir, to commit your credit. Sirrah, sirrah. Let me never eat again sir, Nor feel the blessing of another blew-coate If this young Gentleman, sweet Master Thomas Be not as mad as heart can wish: your heart sir, If yesternights discourse: speak fellow Robin And if thou speakest less than truth. 'tis strange these varlets. By these ten bones sir, if these eyes, and ears Can hear and see. Extreme strange, should thus boldly Bud in your sight, unto your son. O deu guin Can ye deny, ye beat a Constable Last night. I touch Authority ye rascal? I violate the Law? Good M. Thomas. Did you not take two Wenches from the Watch too And put 'em into pudding lane? We mean not Those civil things you did at M. Valentine's The Fiddle, and the fa'las, O strange impudence? I do beseech you sir give no such licence To knaves and drunkards, to abuse your son thus: Be wise in time, and turne'em off: we live sir In a State governed civilly, and soberly Where each man's actions should confirm the Law, Not crack, and canzell it. Lancelot du Lake Get you upon adventers: cast your coat And make your exit. Pur la mour de dieu Pur me no purse: but pur at that door, out sirrah I'll beat ye purblind else, out ye eight languages, My blood upon your head. Purge me'em all sir. And you too presently. Even as you please sir. Bid my maid servant come: and bring my daughter I will have one shall please me. 'tis most fit sir. Bring me the money there: here M. Thomas, I pray sit down, ye are no more my son now, Good gentleman be covered At your pleasure.
