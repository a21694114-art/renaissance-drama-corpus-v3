Oh! oh! oh! A Moore! a devil! a mere devil! his very looks spoke him so, but for his clubfoot, his damnable clubfoot, (Ass that I was not to see it, I'm sure I feel it now,) 'tis an infallible sign: This damned Devil did I bring to Court, and preferred him; but I'm served well enough, he that does the Devil a good turn shall be sure to be thus rewarded. If I should chance to die a sinner (as 'tis ten to one but I shall,) he'll know me again, for I shall carry his marks to my grave: because my Lord Ursini was pleased to exchange some few words with him in private, he grew so insolent, that I going to strike him (in passion, in choler I confess) he falls a kicking me inth' open Court. And looks, as if he would, have spit fire; but had I not known him to be a devil indeed, I'd spit fire with him. How? so hot signior? Men in anger may do anything. Anything? with your leave Sir, And you're a fool, and an ass.— doye find it so by my ears?— And I'll try what you are— Draw i'th' Court? I must see the peace kept. Gentlemen, you know I'm passionate, choleric, somewhat choleric. And I intend to physic you for't; here's that will allay your heat. 'Tis down already. I won't be jeered. we intend no abuse, signior, we only come to gratulate your good fortune, you are turned favourite of late. It has pleased his Majesty to take some small notice of me. Yes, and the Ladies speak high, & gloriously of you. Of me? You could not but observe it as you walked the streets: you are the only object they gaze at. In troth I did not think— Come you are modest now. Who was't you blessed tother day with a favour? I give a favour? Why man? you meant it should be seen. I slipped a point indeed in a countess's chamber. Slipped a point? Dropped it, dropped it, but t' was not worth the taking up. And yet I've heard her boast it as a special gift from your own hands. Indeed I flung it somewhat scornfully, because she was very importunate; troth I was angry, she would have none but that. Were't not for this filthy fretting humour of yours, I could tell you— What good Grutti? Of a Lady. By my best hopes in love with me, is she not? I'm not so happy as to be acquainted with her intents, but I have heard her in my Lord Ursini's presence commend you highly. Prithee who is't? The Lady Charintha. The Lady Charintha; what should I do with her? she's honest, the only precise madam of the Court. They that drop the most beads, may commit the most sins, but were't not so, you have a kind of tempting presence, and besides— Oh! I understand you, she's his Lordship's reversion. St, not a word. I'll to her presently. This is my Lord Ursini's plot, to slander that lady's chastity.
