Besides, thought-feeding rumor forth will goe, And occupie vnquiet peoples spirits, While in this pile for Caine you may bestow Their blinded weaknesse, which with-hold your right. People doe power, not persons apprehend; Strength showes like truth; Mankinde loues policie: Defended Kings, but not reuenged be. Enuy will rise, and both wayes fall on vs; Either as hauing slaine an innocent, Or highly err'd by burying treason thus. In penall iustice silence best contents. Rumor must needs be borne of doing mindes: Enuy is but the smoke of low estate, Ascending still against the fortunate. I feare the cariage: it hath many parts. And Hazards courses may finde ouerthwarts. My shame is equally engag'd with yours. Intents ill carried are that men may know; When things are done, let rumor freely goe. Great works doe oft yeeld grieuous accidents, Which stirre vp peoples rage beyond intents. People are superstitious, caught with showes: To power why doe they else their freedome giue, But that in others pompe these shadowes liue? O blessed yoke! that vnder reason drawes The pleasant load of well-vnited loue: Thy counsell (as mine owne) I doe approue. Then send the Priest. To me bequeath the rest. For superstition hides ill meaning best. And is he gone? Rage then vnprisoned be! I like thee well! While Alaham was there, Thou then didst vse thy violence on me. Now prey abroad; swell aboue all respect; Feare nothing, if notorious thou wilt raigne: Thy glories shine, when euery one complaine. What now? A child? And do'st thou idly walke The beaten pathes of common cruelty? A iudge, and no reuenger then am I, If thou no more than his offences be. While Caine did liue thou thought'st of more than this: Shall death, desire, hope, fame, and fortunes lost Such fading Trophies haue? Can thankfulnesse abound? And shall offense Not feele, Reuenge hath her magnificence? Rage! now thou art aboue the Orbe of doubt, Where danger dangerlesse appeares to thee; Diuine (I pray thee) what shall fall to me? Must I be slaine? Monstrous I know, this womans nature is. The worst she still; her selfe she now exceeds, That dares scarce trust her selfe with that she breeds. Well! now I feele thee rise, when I admire: When hills haue clouds, let all the vallies feare. Scorn'st thou to make examples out of him? Hast thou found out his children? They are mine, Proud horrour! Do'st thou chuse the innocent? False conqueror of nature! do'st thou moue A womans spite to spoyle a mothers loue? Rage! shall we striue which shall giue other place? Hala! suppresse; you need not kindle Rage. Well! On. So that (like ruine) I may fall, And ruine him; take children, me, and all. Hala! distract? haue senses lost their vse? Is there a third that traffiketh abuse? I bring you pietie, dutie, reason, loue, Water, to quench these flames that passions moue. Throw on enough. No Sea can quench this flame; And then, what cannot quench doth but inflame.
