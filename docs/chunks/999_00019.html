Marriage a cloying meat, marry who thou wilt to make a show to shroud thee from the storms round headed opinion, that sways all the world, may let fall on thee. Me cousin? thou shalt call once in a month, or so: I'll read false Letters from a far distant Uncle, insert his commendations to thee, hug thy believing husband into a pair of handsome horns. Look upon him with one eye, and wink upon thee with other. Wouldst have any more? The return of Servants, or some friendly visit will intercept us now: reassume your habit, and be but Iaruis till tomorrow morning, I now by the potent truth of friendship, I will give you plenty of cause to confess I love you truly, and strongly. Y'are in earnest. On my life serious, let this kiss seal it. The softest wax ever sealed bawdy business. Now for old Bloodbound, I will meet you upon the Change Sir, with a blind bargain, and then help your son to a good pennyworth: this night shall be all mirth, a Mistress of delight. Nay, nay, nay, mark what follows, I must bring her home dark, turn her up to bed, and here she goes to Church: my Cloak sirrah. 'Tis a very dark night sir, you will not have a cloak for the rain. I am going to steal the Widow from I know not how many. Nay, than I let your cloak for the rain alone, and fetch you a cloak for your knavery. To bed, to bed— good son, what Sim I say. Sir. I charge ye let not one be up i'th' house, but yourself, after the clock strikes ten, nor a light be stirring, Moll, Trick up the green bedchamber very daintily. I shall Sir. And well remembered Moll, the keys of my Compting house, are in the left pocket of my hose above i'th' wicker chair, look to them and have a care of the black box there I have often told thee of, look to that as to thy Maidenhead. I shall sir. Pray for me all, pray for me all. Have you left out any thing for supper? Out Rogue shall not I be at infinite expense tomorrow, fast too night, and pray for me. An old devil in a greasy Satin doublet, keep you company. Ha, what's that? I say, the Satin doublet you will wear too morrow, will be the best in the company sir. That's true, that's true, I come Widow, I come Wench. Oh sweet Sim, what shall I do tomorrow, tomorrow must be the day, the doleful day, the dismal day, alas Sim, what dost thou think in thy conscience I shall do with an old man? Nay, y'are well enough served, you know how your brother not an hour ago, lay at you to have the Ancient, one that your teeth even water at, and yet you cry, I cannot love him, I wonot have him.
