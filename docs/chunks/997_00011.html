Your courtesies overwhelm me: I much grieve To part from such true friends, and yet find comfort; My attendance on my honourable Lord (Whose resolution holds to visit my Lady) Will speedily bring me back. Dar'st thou venture further? Yes, yes, and knock again. 'Tis he; disperse. Perform it bravely. I know my Cue, ne'er doubt me. Beast that I was to make you stay: most welcome, You were long since expected. Say so much To my friend I pray you. For your sake I will Sir. For his sake! Mum; this is nothing. More than ever, I would have believed though I had found it in my Primer. When I have given you reasons for my late harshness, You'll pardon, and excuse me: for, believe me Though now I part abruptly, in my service I will deserve it. Service! with a vengeance! I am satisfied: farewell Tom. All joy stay with you. You are happily encountered: I yet never Presented one so welcome, as I know You will be to my Lady. This is some vision; Or sure these men are mad, to worship a Dunghill; It cannot be a truth. Be still a Pagan, An unbelieving Infidel, be so Miscreant, And meditate on blankets, and on dogwhips. I am glad you are come, until I know your pleasure. I knew not how to serve up my Lady's dinner. His pleasure; is it possible? What's thy will? Marry Sir, I have some grouse, and Turkey chicken, Some Rails, and Quails, and my Lady willed me ask you What kind of sauces best affect your palate, That I may use my utmost skill to please it. The devil's entered this cook, sauce for his palate! That on my knowledge, for almost this twelvemonth, Durst wish but cheeseparings, and brown bread on Sundays. That way I like 'em best. It shall be done Sir. What think you of the hedge we shall dine under? Shall we feed gratis? I know not what to think; Pray you make me not mad. This place becomes you not; Pray you walk Sir, to the dining room. I am well here Till her Ladyship quits her chamber. Well here say you? 'Tis a rare change! but yesterday you thought yourself well in a barn, wrapped up in Peas-straw. O Sir, you are wished for. My Lady dreamt Sir of you. And the first command she gave, after she rose Was (her devotions done) to give her notice When you approached here. Which is done on my virtue. I shall be converted, I begin to grow Into a new belief, which Saints, nor Angels Could have won me to have faith in. Sir, my Lady. I come to meet you, and languished till I saw you. This first kiss is for form; I allow a second To such a friend. To such a friend! Heaven bless me! I am wholly yours, yet Madam, if you please To grace this Gentleman with a salute.
