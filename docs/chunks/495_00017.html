Having a wife so fair as Anabell, Beloved of me thy foe, and to entirely, That I do offer up my life for hers, Should mercy pardon now what law doth threaten, Thou must immagin if that we too live, I still should go about to murder thee, To enjoy thy wife, whom I so much do love, therefore beg of the king that only I may die, to save her honour, and thy infamy. Hark you housewife, do you hear all this: Doth not your heart melt at this amis. Melt Blunt yes, and doth weep brinish tears, to see what james them, and doth me confound, Here is a glass for such as lives by lust, See what 'tis to be honest, what 'tis to be just, Why this is well: now Sovereign hear me speak, I he that is upposed slaine doth liue, Then friendly may we reconsile these jars, Our law doth light on none, but guilty crimes: And that it punisheth as justice wills. Why then vouchsafe all in this princely presence, This gentleman and I brought all to pass, He in a Doctors shape, hath saved the life O my frend Sentloe, and o Anabell, I in like sort have aved Vallenger, And Sentloe which by me should have been slain, I saved him by an honest policy. And now alive present him to your sight, To make a pleasing end of these sad sights. This breathes new life into my hated heart. Sweet beauteous lets the causer of my smart, Forget in me, what I have done amiss, And seal my pardon with one balmy kiss My soul reputes her lewd impiety. My bloods dear solace, and my best content, My only dear esteemed Vallenger, Not all the world being turned into pleasure, Can give my soul such sweet contented treasure, Thou art more dear, more pleasing to my mind, then at the first: before thou provest unkind, 'tis incident for young men to offend, And wives must stay their leisures to amend. This kind contryssion of young vallenger, More joys my heart, than rest to travelers, Live long together, and may never fate, this new joined league of marriage separate. The like say I, to thee that now hath tried, A friends firm faith, that nothing can divide. the which I will endeavour to deserve, And not so much as once in thought to swerve. Since all things sorteth to this happiness, And pining care, is turned to joyful mirth, I will be partner in your merriment, Away with that same tradgike monument, For that same Florence there, because we see, she sorrows something for her follies past, Let her be had among the Conuertines. And as her faults shall vanish or else stay, Let her be used accordingly. Away with her, Glad parents, and glad friends, In Bristol here a while ourselves will stay, And spend some sportful hours to crown your joy After so many troubles, and tired annoy.
