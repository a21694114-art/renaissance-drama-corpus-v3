I am not enamoured of this painted Idol, This strumpet world; for her most beauteous looks Are poisoned baits, hung upon golden hooks, When fools do swim in wealth, her Cynthian beams Will wanton dance on the silver streams: But when this squint-eyed age sees virtue poor, And by a little spark sits shivering, Begging at all, relieved at no man's door, She miles on her (as the Sun shines on fire) To kill that little heat, and with her frown Is proud, that she can tread poor virtue down: Therefore her wrinkled brow makes not mine sour, Her gifts are toys, and I deride her power.

I, and next day too That will be rare, you slaue: For a leane dyet makes a fat wit. I had rather be a foole & weare a fat paire of chéekes.
