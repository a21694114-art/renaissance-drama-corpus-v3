Whom, Anthony our friend? Say man, how fares our Loves? How doth Mathea? Can she love Ned? how doth she like my suit? Will old Pisaro take me for his Son; For I thank God, he kindly takes our Lands, Swearing, Good Gentlemen you shall not want, Whilst old Pisaro, and his credit holds: He will be damned the rogue, before he do't? Prithee talk milder: let but thee alone, And thou in one bare hour will ask him more, Than he'll remember in a hundred years: Come from him Anthony, and say what news? The news for me is bad; and this it is: Pisaro hath discharged me of his service. Discharged thee of his service; for what cause? Nothing, but that his Daughters learn Philosophy. Maids should read, that it teacheth modesty. ay, but I left out mediocrity, And with effectual reasons, urged your loves. The fault was small, we three will to thy Master And beg thy pardon. Oh, that cannot be, He hates you far worser, than he hates me; For all the love he shows, is for your Lands, Which he hopes sure will fall into his hands: Yet Gentlemen, this comfort take of me, His Daughters to your loves affected be: Their father is abroad, they three at home, Go cheerly in, and cease that is your own: And for myself, but grace what I intend, I'll overreach the Churl, and help my Friend. Build on our helps, and but devise the means. Pisaro did command Frisco his man, (A simple sot, kept only but for mirth) To inquire about in London for a man, That were a Frenchman and Musitian, To be (as I suppose) his daughter's Tutor: Him if you meet, as like enough you shall, He will inquire of you of his affairs; Then make him answer, you three came from Paul's, And in the middle walk, one you espied, Fit for his purpose; then describe this Cloak, This Beard and Hat: for in this borrowed shape, Must I beguile and overreach the Fool: The Maids must be acquainted with this drift. The Door doth ope, I dare not stay reply, lest being descried: Gentlemen adieu, And help him now, that oft hath helped you. How now sirrah, whither are you going? Whither am I going, how shall I tell you, when I do not know myself, nor understand myself? What dost thou mean by that? Marry sir, I am seeking a Needle in a Bottle of Hay, a Monster in the likeness of a Man: one that in stead of good morrow, asketh what porridge you have to Dinner, Parley vous signior? one that never washes his fingers, but licks them clean with kisses; a clipper of the king's English: and to conclude, an eternal enemy to all good Language. What's this? what's this? Do not you smell me? Well, I perceive that wit doth not always dwell in a satin-doublet: why, 'tis a Frenchman, Bassimon cue, how do you? I thank you sir, but tell me what wouldest thou do with a Frenchman:
