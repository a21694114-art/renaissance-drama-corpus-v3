Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Him whom you talk withal he did not only seem, But 'tis the same, who though't be twenty years ago, Hath not a whit altered his ancient look. Stand then aside, Dametas stay with me, Tell me know'st thou this man? Me seemeth so, But yet I know not where. Him can I put in mind. Let me alone, stand you aside a while. I your commandment willingly obey. Now answer me Dametas, and take heed You do not lie, 'tis almost twenty years Since you returned from seeking out my child, Which the outrageous River bore away: Did you not tell me you had searched in vain All that same country, with Alpheus' waters? Why ask you this? Did not you tell me him You could not find? I grant I told you so. What child then was it (tell me) which you gave Unto this stranger which did know you here? Will you I should remember what I did So long ago? old men forgetful are. Is not he old? yet he remembers it. Tush he doth rather dote. That shall we see, Come hither stranger, come. I come. Oh that Thou wert as far beneath the ground. Tell me Is this the shepherd that gave thee the gift? This same is he. What gift is't thou speak'st of? Dost not remember in the temple of Olimpich Ioue, Having had answer of the Oracle, And being ready to depart, I met with thee, And asked thee of the Oracle, which thou declaredst, After I took thee home unto my house, Where didst thou not give me an Infant child, Which in a cradle thou hadst lately found? And what of that? This is that very child, Which ever since I like mine own have kept, And at these Altars must be sacrificed. Oh force of Destiny. Yet wilt thou feign? Is it not true which he hath told thee here? Oh were I dead as sure as it is true. And wherefore didst thou give another's goods? Oh master seek no more, let this suffice. Yet wilt thou hold me off and say no more? Villain thou diest if I but ask again. Because the Oracle foretold me that the child Should be in danger on his father's hands His death to have if he returned home. All this is true, for this he told me then. Ay me, it is too manifest, the case is clear. What resteth then, would you more proof than this? The proofe's too great, too much have you declared, Too much I understand, ●o Carino, Carino, How I change grief and fortunes now with thine, How they affections now are waxen mine, This is my son, oh most unhappy son, Of a more wretched father. More savage was The water in him saving, then in running quite away, Since at these sacred Altars by thy father's hands Thou must be slain, a woeful sacrifice, And thy poor blood must wash thy native soil.
