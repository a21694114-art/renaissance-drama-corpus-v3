Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

What? what durst thou venture now, and be plain wo'm There's nothing in the verge of my command That should not serve your Lordship. Speak, speak truth and flatter not, Upon what security? On that which you propounded sir, your honour, It is above all other obligation, And he that's truly noble will not stain it. Upon my honour will you lend me then But a night's lodging. How sir. She is angry I shall obtain, I know the trick on't, had She yielded at the first it had been fatal. It seems your Lordship speaks to one you know not. But I desire to know you better Lady. Better! I should desire my Lord. Better or worse, if you dare venture one, I'll hazard tother. 'tis your Lordship's mirth. Y'are in the right, 'tis the best mirth of all. I'll not believe my Lord you mean so wantonly As you profess. Refuse me if I do not Not mean? I hope you have more Charity Than to suspect, I'll not perform as much, And more than I ha' said, I know my fault, I am too modest when I undertake, But when I am to Act let me alone. You shall be alone no longer My good Lord. Frank Tryer. Which side holds your honour. I am o'thy side Frank. I think so! for All the Park's against me, but 6. to 4. Is odds enough. Is it so much against you. Lady I think 'tis two, to one. We were on even terms till you came hither, I find her yielding, and when they do run? They say presently. Will you venture any thing Lady? Perhaps she reserves herself for the horse race. There I may venture somewhat with his Lordship. That was a witty one. You will be doing. You are for the footmen. I run with the Company. I'll go your half. No thank you Jack, would I had ten pieces more On't. Which side. On the Irishman. Done! I'll maintain the English, As many more with you, I love to cherish Our own Countrymen. 'tis done my Lord. I'll rook for once, my Lord I'll hold you twenty more Done with you too. Your Lordship is very confident. I'll lay with you too. Lie with her he means. Come, you shall venture something, What gold against a kiss, but if you lose, You shall pay it formally down upon my lip. Though she should win, it would be held extortion To take your money. Rather want of modesty, A great sin if you observe the circumstance, I see his Lordship has a disposition To be merry, but proclaim not this free lay To every one, some women in the world Would hold you all day. But not all night sweet Lady. Will you not see 'em my Lord? Frank Tryar, you'll wait upon this gentlewoman, I must among the gamesters, I shall quickly Return to kiss your hand. How d'ee like this gallant. he's one it becomes not me to censure.
