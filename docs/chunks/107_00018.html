I understand, the army that he brings Against these walls, is such, that I me doubt Our city's force may scarce the same resist. Yet true it is, that right and reason both Are on our side, which bring the victory Oftentimes: for we our country to defend, They to subdue the same in arms are come. But what I would unto your highness show, Is of more weight, and more behoves to know. And what is that? o quickly tell it me. A Greek prisoner is come unto my hands. And what saith he that doth so much import? That even already by their ranks in ray, And straight will give assault to these our walls. Then must I straight prepare our Citizens In glittering arms to march into the field. O Prince (and pardon me) thy youthful years Nor see themself, ne let thee once discern, What best behoveth in this doubtful case. For Prudence, she that is the mighty queen Of all good works, grows by experience, Which is not found with few days seeking for. And were not this both sound and wise advise, Boldly to look our foemen in the face, Before they spread our fields with hugy host, And all the town beset by siege at once? We be but few, and they in number great. Our men have yet more courage far than they. That know I not, nor am I sure to say. Those eyes of thine in little space shall see How many I myself can bring to ground. That would I like, but hard it is to do. I nill pen up our men within the walls. In counsel yet the victory consists. And wilt thou then I use some other read? What else? be still a while, for haste makes waste. By night I will the Cammassado give. So may you do and take the overthrow. The vantage is to him that doth assault. Yet skirmish given by night is perilous. Let set upon them as they sit at meat. Sudden assaults affray the mind no doubt, But we had need to overcome. So shall we do. Not sure, unless some other counsel help. Amid their trenches shall we them invade? As who should say, were none to make defence. Should I then yield the City to my foes? Not, but advise you well if you be wise. That were thy part, that knowest more than I Then shall I say that best doth seem to me? Yea Creon yea, thy counsel hold I dear. Seven men of courage have they chosen out. A slender number for so great emprise. But they them chose for guides and captains. To such an host? why they may not suffice. Nay, to assault the seven gates of the city. What then behoveth so bestead to done? With equal number see you do them match. And then commit our men in charge to them? Choosing the best and boldest bloods in Thebes.
