Why, the heat's great, It burns in our Armour as we march. It burns the enemy as well as we. It warms our Soldiers spirits & makes them fire: I had rather die, then when my blood is hot, Be awed by counsel, till it freeze like Ice: He is no Soldier, that for fear of heat, Will suffer victory to fly the field. My Lord of Bourbon, ye are more hot than wise. Rodorick, methinks you are very peremptory. It is in zeal of the general good: Go to your Tent, refresh your unscorched limbs: There draw your battles model: and as soon As the cool winds have found the burning Sun, And made it tractable for travailers: Arm you, and mount upon your barbed Steed, Lead forth your Soldiers, and in good array, Charge bravely on the Army of our foe. The Duke of Orleans hath counselled well, I'll in and recreate me in my Tent. Farewell, my Lord, when you resolve to fight, Proclaim your meaning by a canon's mouth, And with a volley I will answer you. If you will needs retire, farewell my Lord. Ha Roderick, are not we fine politicians, That have so quaintly wrought the King of France Unto our faction, that he threatens war, Against the almost reconciled Navarre? But this is nothing to the acts we'll do: Come, come, my Lord, you trifle time with words: Sit down, sit down, and make your warlike plot: But wherefore stand these murderous Glaves so nigh? Touch them not, Rodorick, prithee let them stand. Some paper, pen, and ink. My Lord. Post to the Master Gunner, And bid him plant his demi culverings Against the king's pavilion. Presently. But first, bring pen and ink and paper straight. Roderick, thou shalt assist me in this plot. Do it yourself, my Lord, I have a charge Of Soldiers, that are very mutinous, And long I dare not stay, for fear my absence Be cause of their revolt unto Navar. Then to your Soldiers: I will to my plot. Away my Lord, leave me unto the Duke. Kill you the Duke, and after I'll kill thee. This pen is stabbed, and it will not write, The Ink that's in the Standage doth look black, This in my pen is turned as red as blood. The reason that the platform you would make, Must by this hand be written with thy blood. Zounds, what art thou that threatens Bourbon so? One that's as desperate-careless of his life, As thou art timorous, and fearest to die. Comest thou to kill me? If I should say no, This weapon would condemn me, which I seized Of purpose, Bourbon, to bereave thy life. Why, fond man, mad man, know'st thou what thou dost? I know it, Bourbon, and I know beside, What thou wouldst say, to daunt my resolution. What would I say?
