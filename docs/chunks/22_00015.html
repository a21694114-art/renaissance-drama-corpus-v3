Then lean not to much to gender gyglet For here desire contrary to mine is set I heard by her tale she would banish the sone And then were we poor launders all undone Except the son shine that our clothes may dry We can do right nought in our laundry Another manner loss if we should miss Then of such nyreby ceters as she is. I think it better that thou envy me Then I should stand at reward of thy pit It is the guise of such gross queynes as thou art With such as I am evermore to thwart By cause that no beauty ye can obtain Therefore ye have us that be fair in disdain. When I was as young as thou art now I was within little as fair as thou And so might have kept meyf I had would And as dearly my youth I might have sold As the tryckest and fairest of you all But I feared parels that after might fall Wherefore some belines I did me provide Lest vice might enter on every side Which hath tree entre where ydylnesse doth reign It is not thy beauty that I disdain But thine idle life that thou hast rehearsed Which any good woman's heart would have pierced For I perceive in dancing and singing In eating and drinking and thine appareling Is all the joy wherein thy heart is set But nought of all this doth thine own labour get For hadst thou nothing but of thine own travail Thou mightest go as naked as my nail Me think thou shouldest abhor such idleness And pass the time in some honest business Better to lose some part of thy beauty Then so oft to ieoberde all thine honest But I think rather than thou wouldest so do Thou hadst liefer have us live ydylly to And so no doubt we should if thou mightest have The clear son banished as thou dost crave Then were we launders marred and unto the Thine own request were small commodity For of these twain I think it far better Thy face were son burned and thy clothes the swetter Than that the son from shining should he smytter To keep thy face fair and thy smock be shytter Sir how lick ye my reason in her case. Such a railing whore by the holy mass I never hard in all my tyfe till now Iu dede I loue ryght wel the tone of you But or I keep you both by god's mother The devil shall have the tone to fet the other. Promise me to speak that the son may shine bright And I will be gone quyikly for all night. Get you both hens I pray you heartily Your suits I perceive and will report them truly un to Iupiter at the next leisure And in the same desire to know his pleasure Which knowledge had even as he doth show it Fere ye not time enough ye shall know it.
