But stay— O my Anacreon, my Anacreon, I have lost my Anacreon: Varlets, Villains, I'm deluded, my pockets are picked; I have lost my Anacreon: did I dream? or did I make Verses? or was I mad? now my dream's out, 'tis out indeed, all; for now I remember me, I left out the worst part unexpounded, and that was their vanishing from me: well, this 'tis to be a stargazer, and fall into a pit; I was thinking of Astronomia, when I was by promise to have met with Geographus: well, I'll pursue my first intendment, and to Geographus for the learning of the languages; and fear ne'er a corrival under Heaven, now Mithridates, and Scaliger are dead. ay, But Medicus, who brought you word that Poeta was sick? Why, Historia has sent one unto me, now to entreat me to minister unto him my best physic; and the messenger told me (as he heard, it seems) the occasion thereof, which was, that Historia who was in love with him, hearing that he was hurt in a fray with Logicus and Grammaticus, out of the jealous fear of her abundant love, sent to me thus carefully, upon the suspicion of his hurt. Why, Physiognomus, did Poeta seem to you, to be well? Yes, i'faith; or if he were sick 'twas more in mind then in body. Well, Medicus, where's the messenger? Why, at my house expecting my return. Back then, in all haste, and by her servant send him poison, that if he be sick he may die: and so one may be removed out of geometre's his way. And if the poison chance to be discovered, thou mayst pretend 'twas her treachery, because he does not love her, and that thy physic was good. Let me alone, I warrant you; but if I can but once come to the handling of him myself, I'll give him but a clyster, & blow him up with a Powder, I warrant him. But, Physiognomus, are you sure 'twas he? did not you mistake him? Faith, neither of us knew him very well; but Cheiromantes has brought some testimonies from him. What, I prithee? what? marry, Sir, a book, and that I think is a sign of a Scholar; but I have a purse too, and that, I think, is not a sign of a Scholar. What's in't? what's in't? Nay, I'll swear, we both ran since I Nimbed it, that we durst not be so bold yet, as to take leisure to look in't, but now I'll see. What's this? Anacreon? an old bawdy Poet? a fit companion for such a Gallant. A fire burn it; here's nothing but a scurvy paper. But a murrain, how couldst thou possibly get these things from him? Faith, Cheiromantes, by the slight of the Hand did it very neatly.
