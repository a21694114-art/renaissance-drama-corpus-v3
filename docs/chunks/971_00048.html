Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

This man amazes me! I pray you, like one of 'em, Sir. I do like him there, that has the best warrant, Mistress, to save your longing, and (multiply him) It may be this. But I am I still for justice Overdoo, that's my conscience. And quit you. Is't done, Lady? I, and strangely, as ever I saw! What fellow is this trow? No matter what, a Fortune-teller we ha' made him. Which is't, which is't. Nay, did you not promise', not to inquire? 'Slid, I forgot that, pray you pardon me. Look, here's our Mercury come: The Licence arrives i'the finest time, too! 'tis but scraping out Coke his name, and 'tis done. How now lime-twig? hast thou touched. Not yet, Sir, except you would go with me, and see't, it's not worth speaking on. The act is nothing, without a witness. Yonder he is, your man with the box fall'n into the finest company, and so transported with vapours, they ha' got in a Northern Clothier, and one Puppy, a Western man, that's come to wrestle before my Lord Mayor, anon, and Captain Whit, and one Val Cutting, that helps Captain Iordan to roar, a circling boy: with whom your Numps, is so taken, that you may strip him of his cloathes, if you william. I'll undertake to geld him for you; if you had but a Surgeon, ready, to sear him. And Mistress justice, there, is the goodest woman! she does so love 'em all over, in terms of justice, and the Style of authority, with her hood upright— that I beseech you come away Gentlemen, and see't. S light, I would not loose it for the Fair, what'll you do, Ned? Why, stay here about for you, Mistress Welborne must not be seen. Do so, and found out a Priest i'the mean time, I'll bring the Licence. Led, which way is't? Here, Sir, you are o'the backside o'the Booth already, you may hear the noise. WHit, bid Vall Cutting continued the vapours for a lift, Whit, for a lift. I'll ne mare, I'll ne mare, the eale's too meeghty. How now! my Galloway Nag, the staggers? ha'! Whit, gi'him a slit i'the forehead. Cheer up, man, a needle, and thread to stitch his ears. I'd cure him now an I had it, with a little butter, and garlic, long-pepper, and grains. Where's my horn? I'll gi'him a mash, presently, shall take away this dizziness. Why, where are you zurs? do you ulinch, and leave us i'the zuds, now? I'll ne mare, I'is even as vull as a Paipers' bag, by my troth, I Do my Northern cloth zhrinke i'the wetting? ha'? Why, well said, old Fleabitten, thou'lt never tire, I see. Not, Sir, but he may tyre, if it please him. Who told dee sho? that he vuld never teer, man? No matter who told him so, so long as he knows.
