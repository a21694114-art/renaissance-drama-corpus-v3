Now or we any further proceed Hold this vesture and put it on thee From henceforth thou shalt be called prosperity I thank god and you I am in felicity Now unto you I shall here show Of such things as ye shall eschew first your soul look that ye keep clean Beware of misrule in any wise Play not at caylles, cards nor dice Also from miswomen, for by them mischief may rise As it doth often, this daily is seen Haunt no taverns, nor sit not up late Let no hassardes nor riotours, with you be check mat. For then will envy come, and make debate The which shall cause great trouble Be plentiful of such as god hath sent Unto the poor people give with good intent For every penny that is so spent God will send the double. Take heed and do as I have said Sir therewith I hold me well apaid As ye have commanded me it shall be done Then let us depart for a season plyenede I will be your protection. joy and solace be in this hall Is there no man here that knoweth me at all I am beloved both with great and small abundance is my name I have all things as me list Meat drink, and cloth of the best Gold and silver, full is every chest In faith I will not lain I think ye know not my ways How I get goods now a days By a proper mean Think you that I would Lend either silver or gold That day shall not be seen But I will lend them ware That shall be both bad and dear Not worth the money he shall pay And if he can no surety get Of my ware he getteth right nought Without a good pledge he lay Then will I for mine avail He shall make a bill of sail To me full bought, and sold if the day be exspyred and passed Then will I hold it fast He shall not have it though he would this craft I have long used And some men do not yet refuse it this is openly known What is he in all this town that will lend without singular commodst Should I tend without a profit Nay than I hold nought worth my wit. All this ye say, is against conscience Conscience quoth a, nay then shall we never thrive For I know him not alive By conscience that cometh to substance I have all manner of marchaundy I sell for long days to them that are needy And for the payment I have good surety Bound in statute marchaunce Because I may forbear I sell my ware so dear I make forty of twenty in half a year Other men do so as well as I. Even sin, vary shame marry fie These goods are gotten untrewely Many a man is undone thereby To take this ware to dear
