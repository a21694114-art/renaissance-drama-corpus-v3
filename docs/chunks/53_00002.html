Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Grace and good governance of man For with good discretion they began That were the great winners of victory The victory is gotten by discretion I pray you sir show me this lesson How to come to richesse, for that is all my care For I am ever in great necessity Meat and drink with me is scarcity No man will trust me of a penny And also my clothes are but bare Good sir what say you therein. I hold it punishment for thy sin Show me what is thy name I am named Impatient poverty For sooth that may well be Thou art so full of wrath and envy In thee can grow no grace But if thou wilt forsake sensuality And be governed by reason, as I shall induce thee Thou shalt come to richesse, within short space Show me that now in this place And thereto I will agree Thou must love thy neighbour with charity Do unto him, no manner of disease Look how thou would he did to thee Do to him no worse in no degree And then thou shalt our lord please. Shall I love him that loveth not me? Those that trouble & rebuke me shamefully That will I never do while I live. Thou must charitably all faults forgive Wwat soever any man to thee say Let as thou hardest it not, turn thine care away Thou shalt please god, if thou so do. Nay by god there hoo What is he in all this place That will do as this man said Show me or I go if a man do you a great offence Will ye keep your patience Nay be god not so I put case I break your head Will ye suffer that in very deed. to suffer for Christ's sake I shall have meed That shall I know by god's bread. Hold thy hand and keep patience think what christe suffered for our offence He was beaten scourged, & spite on with violence And suffered death for our sake yet he took it patiently He forgave his death, & prayed for his enemies Pater dimite illis, his saying was truly An exam 〈◊〉 us to take to be meek in heart: beati pauperes spiritu Shall Christ say full even. Et venite benediciti: come my blessed children To the kingdom of heaven. Sir I thank you, for your ghostly instruction Unto your saying, I can make no delayaunce I put me under your governation And for my misdeeds, I take great repentance Then to my saying, take good remembrance Exercise yourself in virtue, from this time hence And unto peace evermore be obedient Sit before every sharp word, a shield of sufferance And when time is of your concupissaunce Then pacify it with benign resistance sir gramercy, that ye have brought me to this estate By your advertisement I am willing to live in christes law There as I have offended him both early & late I served him not for love nor for awe Therefore now right well I know That poverty and misery that I my life in lead it is but only punishment for my mysdeade
