Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

My master is here, what shall I do. O, all hail to you honest man. Now master, and master Chremes, all things be in a readiness within. O it is trimly done of you. Now send for him when you will. Very well surely: that is the cause forsooth he is now absent: Nay but answer me to this, what business hast thou there? * Who, I? Yea. What, I? Yea, you sirrah. I went in but even now. As though I asked how long ago it was. Your son and I went in together. Why then, and is Pamphilus within? I am still vexed unhappy man that I am. Why thou hangman thou, didst not thou tell me that they are fallen at debate. So they are. What makes he there than? What think you that he is a doing? he is sure chiding with her. Nay but master Chremes, you shall hear me tell you of a notable strange matter: I wot not what old man is come yonder even now, but to look to, he is a substantial and wary man: if you saw his face, you would take him for a right honest man. In his countenance is sad gravity, and his words do sound of truth. What tidings bringst thou? Nothing forsooth but what I heard him say. And what saith he, I pray you? marry that he knoweth Glycerie to be a free born woman of Athens. Hola howh, Dromo, Dromo. What is the matter? Dromo. Why, hear me sir. If thou speak one word more— Dromo. I beseech you hear me. What would you have sir? Hoist up this knave on thy back, and carry him in as fast as thou canst. Whom? Davus. Wherefore? Because I will have it so, take him away I say. What have I done sir? Away with him. If you do find that I told you any lie, kill me forth right. I will not here one word: I shall set thee in a beat by and by, I warrant thee. What, notwithstanding I say nothing but Troth? Yea nevertheless, sirrah see thou that he be kept fast fettered: and hearest thou me? bind his hands and feet together, * Now sir go to: By God if I live this day to an end, I will teach thee and him both, what danger it is for the one of you to beguile his master, and for the other to deceive his father. Tush man, be not in so great a rage. O Chremes, do you not pity me, to see what reverend regard my son hath towards me? and that I should take somuch travail for such a son? Well go to Pamphilus: Come out here Pamphilus, Is there no shame in thee? WHo calls me? O I am undone, it is my father. What sayest thou? thou errand—
