Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Now Lelio I have laid the burden of my war on thee: Thou art the champion of my weal or woe: Deceive not my firm hope, but in a manly fight? Attempt the winning of this happy day, By those moist tears which with a mournful heart, I often shed upon Sempronios herst: And by the love I bear Annetta fair: nought but my death, shall make me lose thy right, More than my life, I cannot hazard mighty Florentine I like thy courage gentleman: charge the combatants. Whom do my eyes behold, art thou not Brishio my father? Who forsaking weal and friends, Madest thy choice of baleful banishment, Rather than live and seem banished. Art not thou Lelio whom my zealous prayers, Have always wished, and wild thy greatest good, Cease trumpets cease, we two must never fight. What meaneth thy champion Medesa to faint. What means thy champion Forsa that he faints. To combat with my son were worse than death. To combat with my father were my death? Father and sons, both champions in our wars. Brother and brother, causer of the same What favour merit they, who love so well? What infamy deserve we that contend? See Forsa these champions are so kind they cannot fight. Shall we contend for titles wretchedly, While meaner men contend in perfectt love. A pardon Medesa all the world beside. Had not this man, this father of my wife: Encountered me, I would have died and perished in thy cause. A pardon Forsa, had not this virtuous husband of my child. Encountered me, I would have died and perished in thy cause. Rise combatants, you teach us what to do, Come Medesa, join you hands, And let these two which love so well, Be judges of our wars, and let it end. Brother content. Now champions end debate: What you conclude, shall make a peace with us: See see, my son, our love hath well nigh made their peace. Princes, are you agreed to determine war by us? I Brishio, now we are agreed. Then tell me mighty Duke, but dally not. Lovest thou Orelio as a husband should? I love and honour her in word and soul Then nothing is to dear for her my leech. I'll hazard life, and all to do her good. Perform her jointure then, and keep thy word. Then let the Florentine pay me her dowry. Fear not my Lord, the Florentines are men that honour right. Speak great Italian Duke, shall it be bruited in the ears of men? That Forsa grants all duty unto thee, And thou deny her right of marriage. 'tis pity that gold should part two noble minds, Here Forsa take my hand, this night one tent Shall lodge us both, & here a legare, shall my money lie, Until my treasurer hath brought thy dew. Then march in peace, here endeth all our hate. Thus poor men's love, doth great men's harms debate.
