That's the best: But shall we only build our hopes on strength; I think 'twere good to piece the lions skin Where it too short falls, with the fox's skin, A couple of Protean villains I have ready, For any dangerous attempt in peace, And they can poison, stab, and lie in wait Like Serpents, to entrap and cease their prey, Mendoza and Vandome, those are the men: Let them be called in. They are hear, great Caesar. Most mighty Emperor, what's your Highness will? That unto England presently you sail, And there consort you with the Earl of Artoyes, A Frenchman borne, but one that loves us well; Let him and you send us intelligence Of the proceedings of the English King, With the haughty Palsgrave, give the Earl this Letter, By which ye may win credit in his trust: And ere't be long I will devise a plot, Which you shall manage, for the general good. Be careful; as you do respect our love, And hope for gold in showers, mean time take this. Your will shall be obeyed. It shall be done. Great Ioue can say no more: That State thrives best that has such Slaves in store. See our Fleet ready: and ye swelling gales, That blow the good hours, fill our empty sails. My Lord of Poitiers, as you are informed, Where means the Emperor and his train to land? Here at this 〈◊〉 of Bulloyne, & the news Is certain that his Mightiness is near. How angry has the heavens as been with the sea, That it hath boiled so much, and cast the sands Into such mountains that they overlook The bounds that held them in. Well may our friends Escape the danger of this heavy storm: Small gusts at Land, sink a whole Fleet at sea; And whilst our Cities keep us from their rage, The Rocks bear them, as Tennis-Courts do balls. I'd have an Atheist travel through the deep, And he shall see such wonders, that his soul Would make him soon believe there is a God. But what fair Gentle woman have we here, Which is a shipwrecked creature, comes ashore? All wet and weary with a boisterous storm, At last I have set my foot upon the Land; I tremble as a Feauer shooke my oynes, But 'tis the Ocean that has frozen me: Drop there thou moisture of a swelling Flood. And let me see, no Fisherman at hand, To tell me in what Coast I am arrived: Alas I see none, I shall die with cold. Speak to her, give the woman comfort. How is it with pretty Gentlewoman? Sir, I am very cold, and wet, and ill, Would you could help me to a little fire To dry myself, and I would pray for you. 'tis now no time to reason of your state: Here take my Cloak. Alas Sir, 'tis but thin, And makes 〈◊〉 the more to 〈…〉 I would be shifted into warmer Roads, If I could meet with some kind Gentlewoman.
