And error deep have fostered up apace. The heaps of vice raked up in years long passed, Abounding flow in these our days at last. And this same troublous time, and cumbrous age, Oppresseth all men sore, both young and sage. Wherein those wicked ways that be do reign, And cruel, romping woodness boils again. Lust strong in filthy touch, doth bear a sway. And Princes, riot, now doth catch away With greedy paws, to bring it to decay. Th' whole worlds uncredible wealth, without delay. But lo, which staggering steps where Nero flings, And visage grim, I fear what news he brings. DIspatch with speed that we commanded have: Go, send forthwith some one or other slave, That Plautius cropped scalp and Silla's eke, May bring before our face: go some man seek. I nill protract your noble grace's hest: But to their camps to go am ready priest. 'gainst lineage nought should rashly pointed be. A light thing 'tis for to be just, I see For him, whose heart is void of shrinking fear. A sovereign salve for fear is for to bear yourself debonair to your subjects all. Our foes to slay, a chieftain's virtue call. A worthier virtue 'tis in country's sire, His people to defend with sword and fire. It well beseems such aged wights, to teach, Unbridled springolds young, and not to preach, Both to a man and prince of riper years. May, rather frolic youthful blood appears, To have more need of counsel wise and grave This age sufficient reason ought to have. That heavenly powers your doings may allow. A madness 'twere to Gods for me to bow, When I myself can make such Gods to be: As Claudius now ycounted is we see. So much the more because so much you may. Our power permits us all without denay. Give slender trust to Fortune's flattering face: She topsy-turvy turns her wheel apace. A patch he is that knoweth not what he may. A PRINCE's praise I counted have always, To do that same which with his honour stood, Not that which frantic fancy counteth good. If that I were a meacock or a slouch, Each stubborn, clubbish daw would make me couch. And whom they hate, with force they overquell. Then dint of sword the prince defendeth well. But faith more sure defence doth seem to me. Full meet it is that Caesar dreaded be. More meet of subjects for to be beloved From subjects minds, fear must not be removed What so by force of arms you do wring out, A grievous work it is to bring about. Well hardly then our will let them obey. Will nothing then, but that which well you may. We will decree what we shall best suppose. What people's voice doth jointly bind or lose. Let that confirmed stand. swords bloody dint, Shall cause them else at me to take their hint. God shield, and far that fact from you remove.
