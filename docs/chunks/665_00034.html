Shall I? by my troth I shall be beholding to you then. Now to ye all, be firmaments to stars, Be stars to Firmaments, and as you are Splendent, so be fixed, not wandering, nor Irregular, both keeping course together, Shine not in pride, and gorgeous attire, When clouds do fail, the pole where thou art fixed. Obey, cherish, honour, be kind enough, But let them wear no changeable stuff, Keep them, as shall become your state, Comely, and to creep ere they go. Let them partake your joys, and weep with you, Curl not the snarls that dwell upon these brows, In all things be you kind of all enough, But let them wear no changeable stuff. Fore God a mad spirit. Will ye believe, what such a biscuit brained fellow as this says? he has a mouth like a double cannon, the report will be heard all o'er the town. I warrant he ran mad for love, because no good face could endure the sight of him, and ever since he rails against women like a hot shot. Nay, nay, we must have all friends. jarring discords are no marriage music, Throw not Hymen in a cuckstoole, dimple Your furrowed brows, since all but mirth was meant, Let us not then conclude in discontent. Say, shall we all in friendly strain Measure our paces to bedward? Will Terentia follow? If Tully be her Leader. Good bloods, good spirits, let me answer for all, none speak but mine Host, he has his polls and his aedypols, his times and his tricks, his quirks and his quilits, and his demise and dimensions, God bless thee Noble Caesar, and all these brave spirits, I am Host of the Hobby, Cornutus is my neighbour: Graccus a mad spirit, Accutus is my friend, Sir Scillicet is my guest, all mad comrades of the true seed of troy, that love juice of Grapes: we are all true friends, merry hearts live long, let the Pipers strike up i'll dance my cinquepace, cut a loft my brave capers, whirl about my toe, do my tricks above ground, i'll kiss my sweet hostess, make a courtesy to thy grace, God bless thy Majesty, and the Mouse shall be dun. Come wife, will you dance? I'll not dance I, must you come to the Court to have horns set on your head? I could have done that at home. I, I, be ruled at this time, what, for one merry day we'll find a whole moon at midsummer. Gentles, we thank ye all, the night hath spent his youth, and drowsy Morpheus bids us battle, We will defy him still, we'll keep him out While we have power to do it, sound your loudest noise, Set forward to our chamber. Advance your light. Good rest to all. God give your grace God night.
