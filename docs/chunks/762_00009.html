Next week, sir. Came you sir from Spain lately? ay sir; Why ask you that? Marry sir, thou seems to have been in the hot countries, thy face looks so like a piece of rusty Bacon: had thy Host at Plymouth meat enough in the house, when thou wert there? What though he had not sir? but he had, how then? Marry thank God for it; for otherwise, he would doubtless have Cut thee out in Rashers to have eaten thee; thou look'st as thou wert through broiled already. You have said sir; but I am no mere for his moing, nor yours neither: If I had you in place where, you should find me tough enough in digestion, I warrant you. What will you swagger sirrah, will ye swagger? I beseech you Sir, hold your hand; Get home ye patch, cannot you suffer Gentlemen jest with you? I'd teach him a Gentle trick and I had him of the burse; but I'll watch him a good turn I warrant him. Assure ye master Towerson, I cannot blame him, I warrant you it is no easy loss; How think you master Stranger? by my faith sir, there's twenty Merchants will be sorry for it, That shall be partners with him in his loss. Why sir, what's the matter. The Spanish-galleys have beset our ships, That lately were bound out for Siria. What not? I promise you I am sorry for it. What an old Ass is this to keep us here: Master Pisaro, pray dispatch us hence. Master Vandal I confess I wrong you; But I'll but talk a word or two with him, and straight turn to you. Ah sir, and how then i'? Turn to us, turn to the Gallows if you will, 'tis midsummer-moon with him: let him alone, He calls Ned Walgrave, Master Vandal. Let it be shrovetide, I'll not stay an inch master Pisaro. What should you fear: end as I have vowed before, So now again; my Daughters shallbe yours: And therefore I beseech you and your friends, Defer your business till Dinner time; And what you'd say, keep it for table talk. Marry and shall; a right good motion: Sirs, old Pisaro is grown kind of late, And in pure love, hath bid us home to dinner. Good news in truth: But wherefore art thou sad? For fear the slave ere it be dinner time, Remembering what he did, recall his word: For by his idle speeches, you may swear, His heart was not confederate with his tongue. Tut never doubt, keep stomachs till anon, And then we shall have cates to feed upon. Well sir, since things do fall so crossly out, I must dispose myself to patience: But for your business, do you assure yourself, At my repairing home from the Exchange, I'll set a helping hand unto the same. Bon iurno signeour Padre, why be de melancholy so much, and grave in you a: what News make you look so nought?
