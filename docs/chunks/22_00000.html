Right far to long as now were to recite The ancient estate wherein ourself hath rained What honour what laud given us of very right What glory we have had duty unfaind Of each creature which duty hath constrained For above all goods sins our father's tale We Iupyter were ever princypale. If we so have been as truth it is in deed Beyond the compass of all comparison Who could presume to show for any meed So that it might appear to human reason The high renown we stand in at this season For sins that heaven and earth were first created Stood we never in such triumphant estate. As we now do where of we would report Such part as we see meet for time present Chiefly concerning your perpetual comfort As the thing self shall prove in experiment Which highly shall bind you on knees lowly beute Soolly to honour our highness day by day And now to the matter give ear and we shall say. Before our presence in our high parliament Both gods and goodesses of all degrees Hath late assembled by comen assent For the redress of certain enormities Bred among them thorough extremities Abusede in each to other of them all Namely to purpose in these most special. Our foresaid father's Saturny and Phoebus Aeolus and Phebe these four by name Whose natures not only so far contrarious But also of malice each other to defame Have longe time abused right far out of frame The dew course of all their constellations To the great damage of all earthly nations. Which was debated in place said before And first as became our feather most ancient With beard e white as snow, his locks both cold & hot Hath entered such matter as served is intent Lauding his frosty mansion in the firmament To air and earth as thing most precious purging all humours that are courageous. How be if he allegeth that of long time passed Little hath prevailed his great dilygens Full oft upon earth his fair frost he hath cast All things hurtful to banish out of presence But Phoebus' intending to keep him in silence When he hath laboured all night in his powers His glaring beams maryth all in two hours, Phoebus' to this made no manner answering Where upon they both than Phebe defied Each for his part laid in her reproving That by her showers superfluous they have tried In all that she may their powers be denied Where unto Phebe made answer no more Than Phoebus to Saturn had made before. Anon upon Aeolus all these did flee Complaining their causes each one a row And said to compare none was so even as he For when he is disposed his blasts to blow He suffereth neither son shine, rain nor snow They each against other, and he against all three Thus can these four, in no manner agree.
