Who did assist your passage to this walk? Mervolle Sir, with whom we used your name. Do you observe the Count, and his two lean janissaries? What in captivity? Certain stratagems (Sir) have been levelled 'Gainst the Prerogative of your life royal. Molard. Ere since the hope of day, they waited your Descent from the Cloister Bridge: but doubting Their own courage, and meeting us disguised, Thus in our original weeds; they would Have bribed us for two Pistolets t'assist The prosecution of your death. Our Grace And strength bound them to their good behaviour. They walk like Caterpillars on a Leaf.— Yield me the cause why you contrived my death. Good faith (Sir) for no harm. Give me a Knife!— Here is one belonged to Hans van Geulicke. Take yourself solemnly aside and cut Your Throat! do't straight, and neatly too, y''had best. The motion I dislike, 'tis dangerous. Have you three throats, can you revive as oft As you are killed, to take new punishment, That thus you mutiny 'gainst kind reason? These thousand years 't has been observed, th'eldest Brothers of her house (Sir) could ne'er endure To cut their Throats. 'tis fit (dear Count) that you Must die; I'm else unjust unto myself. With sapient Arts, we will project to save Th'reputation of your justice. As how? I will promulgate I am dead, and both, My Champions here shall swear't. We'll venture one Commandment to save another sir. This cannot satisfy. howe'er (Sir Count) If you will patiently accept your death. I'll furnish ye with Guides, for your last jaunt. You Punto, and Stoccata too! No more Of your philosophy. Fix Brow, to Brow, Knock out, each others Brains, and show your Lord The way, unto th'Elysian-field. Do it At first encounter too; For I'm in haste— 'slight Sir, I ne'er was in Elysium, I. Nor should I find the way thither, though Signior Argos, lent me ninety Nine of his Eyes. Then Punto shall be conduct to ye both. Good Signior, any courtesy but this You may command. You still do most presume Upon the calmest, and th'easy Nature. They all are pictures to th'Law. If you'll Bestow'em but as prisoners unto us, They shall suffer, or give us large ransom. How? a ransom? These Two are poorer than Carthusian-monks: lean as a Romish Lent. Slaves, who pretend sickness, that they may lie In Hospitals to steal the Sheets. Acorns They eat, such as westphalia-hogs do scorn. The Count (Sir) will untie his Strings for'em. Ransom, I'll give: for I do hate to die. The ransom must Be small. suffle'em together: and pack 'Em hence. I will not hear a syllable Of thanks. Rossa, see'em horsed for Milan. O dismal change! does your victorious Sword Hang now on aged Belt of Bandoleer? Is your high Plume moulted to a sprig, small, As if 'twere made o'th' wing of a Beetle.
