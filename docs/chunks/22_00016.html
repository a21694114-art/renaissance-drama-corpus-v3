Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Sir if ye medyll remember me first Then in this meddling my part shallbe the worst. Now I beseech our lord the devil the burst Who meddleth with many I hold him accursed Thou whore can I meddle with you both at ones. By the mass knave I would I had both thy stones In my purse if thou meddle not indifferently That both our matters in issue may be likely. Many words little matter and to no purpose such is the effect that thou disclose. The more ye byb, the more ye babble. The more ye babyl, the more ye fabyl The more ye fabyl, the more unstabyl. The more unstable, the more unabyll In any manner thing to do any good No hurt though ye were hanged by the holy rood. The less your silence, the less your credence The less your credence, the less your honesty The less your honesty the less your assistance The less your assistance the less ability In you to do aught wherefore so god me save No hurt in hanging such a railing knave. What monster is this I never hard none such For look how much more I have made her to much And so far at last she hath made me to little Where be ye Launder I think in some spittle Ye shall wash me no gear for fere of fretting I love no launders that shrink my gear in wetting I pray the go hens and let me be in rest I will do thine errand as I think best. Now would I take my leave if I wist how The longer I live the more knave you, The longer thou livest the pity the greater The sooner thou be rid the tidings the better Is not this a sweet office that I have When every drab shall call me knave Every man knoweth not what god's service is Nor I myself knew it not before this I think god's servants may live holily But the devil servants live more merely I know not what god giveth in standing fees But the devil servants have casweltees A hundredth times more than god's servants have For though ye be never so stark a knave It ye lack money the devil will do worse But bring you straight to another man's purse Then will the devil promote you here in this world As unto such ryche it doth most accord first Pater noster quies in celis And then ye shall since the strife with your heels The greatest friend ye have in field or town Sanding a typ to shall not reach your crown. This same is even he by allycklyhod Sir I pray you be not you master god. No in good faith son but I may say to the I am such a man that god may not miss me Wherefore with the god if thou wouldest have aught done Tell me thy mind and I shall show it soon.
