Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Fear not my gander, I protest, I should handle my matters very ill, if I had not a whole candle. Well than, look to't, and kiss me to boot. Now, here come the friends again, Pythias, aend Damon, and under their cloaks, they have of Bacon, a gammon. Drawer, fill some wine here. How, some wine there? there's company already, Sir, pray forbear! 'Tis Hero. Yes, but she will not be taken, after ●acke, and fresh herring, with your Dunmow- bacon. You lie, it's Westfabian. Westphalian you should say. If you hold not your peace, you are a Coxcomb, I would say. What's here? what's here? kiss, kiss, upon kiss. I, Wherhfore should they not? what harm is in this? 'tis Mistress Hero. Mistress Hero's a whore. Is she a whore? keep you quiet, or Sir Knave out of door. Knave out of door? Yes, Knave, out of door. Whore out of door. I say, Knave, out of door. I say, whore, out of door. Yea, so say I too. Kiss the whore o'the arse. Now you ha' something to do: you must kiss her o' the arse she says: So we will, so we william. O my haunches, O my haunches, hold, hold. Standest thou still? Leander, where art thou? standest thou still like a sot, and not offerest to break both their heads with a pot? See who's at thine elbow, there! Puppet Ionas and Cupid. upon'hem Leander, be not so stupid. You Goat-bearded slave! You whoremaster Knave. Thou art a whoremaster. Whoremasters all. See, Cupid with a word has ta'en up the brawl. These be fine vapours! By this good day they fight bravely! do they not, Numps? Yes, they lacked but you to be their second, all this while. This tragical encounter, falling out thus to busy us, It raises up the ghost of their friend Dionysius: Not like a Monarch, but the Master of a School, in a Scrivener's furred gown, which shows he is no fool. for therein he hath wit enough to keep himself warm. O Damon he cries, and Pythias; what harm, Hath poor Dionysius done you in his grave, That after his death, you should fall out thus, and rave, And call amorous Leander whoremaster Knave? I cannot, I will not, I promise' you endure it. Down with Dagon, down with Dagon; 'tis I, will no longer endure your profanations. What mean you, Sir? I will remove Dagon there, I say, that Idol, that heathenish Idol, that remains (as I may say) a beam, a very beam, not a beam of the Sun, nor a beam of the Moon, nor a beam of a balance, neither a house-beam, nor a Weaver's beam, but a beam in the eye, in the eye of the brethrens; a very great beam, an exceeding great beam; such as are your Stage-players, Rhymers, and Morris-dancers, who have walked hand in hand, in contempt of the Brethrens, and the Cause; and been borne out by instruments, of no mean countenance.
