How now more Visitants?— what Corporal Oath? Corporal? In prison honest Captain? this must not be? How do you Captain Kinsman? Good Coxcomb? what makes that pure— starched fool here? You see Kinsman I am somewhat bold to call in, and see how you do, I heard you were safe enough, and I was very glad on't that it was no worse; This is a double torture now,— this fool byth' book does vex me more than my imprisonment? What meant you Corporal to hook him hither? Who he? he shall relieve thee, and supply thee, I'll make him do't; Fie, what vain breath you spend! he supply? I'll sooner expect mercy from a Usurer when my bond's forfeited, sooner kindness from a Lawyer when my money's spent: nay sooner charity from the devil, than good from a Puritan? I'll look for relief from him, when Lucifer is restored to his blood, and in Heaven again! I warrant my Kinsman's talking of me, for my left ear burns most tyrannically? Captain Ydle? what's he there? he looks; like a Monkey upward; and a Crane downward. Pshaw; a foolish cousin of mine; I must thank God for him. Why the better subject to work a scape upon; thou shalt e'en change clothes with him, and leave him here, and so; Push, I published him e'en now to my Corporal, he will be damned, ere he do me so much good; why I know a more proper, a more handsome device then that, if the slave would be sociable— now goodman Fleer-face? Oh, my cousin begins to speak to me now, I shall be acquainted with him again I hope, Look what ridiculous Raptures take hold of his wrinkles, Then what say you to this device, a happy one Captain? Speak low George; Prison Rats have wider ears than those in Malt-lofts. cousin, if it lay in my power, as they say?— to— do; 'twould do me an exceeding pleasure indeed that, but near talk further on't, the fool will be hanged, ere he do't. Pax, I'll thump 'im to't. Why do but try the Fopster, and break it to him bluntly. And so my disgrace will dwell in his jaws, and the slave slaver out our purpose to his Master, for would I were but as sure on't as I am sure he will deny to do't. I would be heartily glad cousin, if any of my friendships, as they say, might— stand, ah, Why, you see he offers his friendship foolishly to you already? ay, that's the hell on't, I would he would offer it wisely? Verily, and indeed la, Cousin?— I have took note of thy fleers a good while, if thou art minded to do me good? as thou gap'st upon me comfortably, and giv'st me charitable faces? which indeed is but a fashion in you all that are Puritans? willt soon at night steal me thy masters chain?
