I pray Friar Iohn, what spirit do you deal with? Aestroth, did you not hear me name him? And what's he? One of the four great Spirits, that have Dominion o'er the four quarters of the Earth: good Dame, methinks you do not eat. I could eat thee. Could you not show your Devil for a need, To one that fain would see him? In what shape? Why in his own. Oh 'tis too terrible, it would fright us all. Yet would I see him. What, in his terrors? he would make you mad, Distracted and amazed, yet, good mine Host, To give you all content, I could be willing To show him but in some familiar shape, Such as should not affright you. With all my heart. Have you no Neighbour whom you best affect, whose Shape he might assume t'appear less terrible? Yes, twenty I could name. Soft, let me pause; It must be some that still wake at these hours, We have no power o'er sleepers; say I bring him In person of some Watchman? No shape better. Or in the habit of your Constable? Why he's my honest Gossip. Why then his. More scurvy tricks Friar Iohn, I may live to cry quittance with you. But mine Host, resolve me one thing; should great Asterothe appear to you in your gossip's shape, How would you deal with him? Why as my friend, my Neighbour, and my Gossip. No such thing; you must imagine him what he appears, An evil spirit, to kick him, and defy him, As you would do the Devil, otherwise When you are late abroad, and we gone hence, He'll haunt your house hereafter. Fear not that, If kicks and spurns will drive the Devil hence, I'll help to send him packing. I must tell you, 'tis merely for your own good, Appear Asteroth, Asteroth appear from underneath the bed In shape of Master Constable; do't when I say't, Not yet? Excruciabo te Asteroth, in jam jam jam apparebis. Now mine Host, either with zeal expel the Devil hence, Or have your house still haunted. Will kicks do't? Let me alone to conjure him, pox on the Devil, He hath put me in a sweat. I'll after him, and lest he should bear down part Of the house, I'll let him out of doors. Yes, do good Nan; I think Friar Iohn I have bombasted the Devil; thanks for our Supper, French wine, and Spanish Loaves, I never tasted meat That more contented me. But how came this? I'll tell you by the way; no questions now: Now good mine Host we see the night quite spent And the bright day star rising in the East; We'll take our leaves; make much of our good Dame, And think no worse of your good Officer, Your Gossip and your Neighbour, in whose form Asteroth so late appeared. Good night friar Iohn, and holy Father Bernard. Rather good morrow.
