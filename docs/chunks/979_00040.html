'tis well, I am glad of your happiness, And much joy to my brother Capritio, And his fair spouse. She is according to My heart's desire, sir. Well, a word with you, Master Trimalchio, and the rest. What say you? You were as good know it at first, as at last. You are not the first, that have been deceived. In what? my wife? I married her for a maid. And whether she be one, or no, I care not. Nay, should I hear a man that should abuse her In that, I would defend her with my sword. But she and you must call this man your father. I so she must, he gave her at the Church. Nay, her own natural father, flesh and bone, I hope she'll not deny it. No indeed, sir. I would not live to be so ungracious. I must acknowledge thee my child, or I Should do thy mother wrong. I do not think so, You'll not make me believe that I took her For a lord's daughter, and a great heir. Where are Agurtes and the Captain to justify it? Is he your father? He has ever bred me: And I have always called him so. I hope It is no shame: my parentage is honest. Well, if he be, 'tis no disparagement, To marry a justice's daughter. Come, you have Been carried hood winked through this business. Nor is the day yet clear before you. Mark me; I'll open but one leaf in all the book, And you shall see the whole discovery. Come sir, unease. Who have we here? Agurtes And the Captain? Was't you that played the justice? And you his clerk? And I the Constable. Then you are a knot of knaves for your labour. Now I perceive that I am plainly gulled. I am glad there's no man cheated but himself. Your arrow is one of the same quiver too. I'll none of her by this light. Why, you may choose. And yet I do not well see, how you can choose. She is your wife, and you have married her, And must allow her means to maintain her. You may declare yourself unto the world. And be laughed at: but keep your own counsel, And who needs know of it? Believe me sir, The Gentlewoman is not to be despised, Her wit and virtues are dowry sufficient. Nay, if you say so, then must I needs love her: But by this hand, I thought you would have jeered me. Hold on your course, march on as you came in, And rest content, since fate has thought it fit, To make your fortunes equal with your wit.
