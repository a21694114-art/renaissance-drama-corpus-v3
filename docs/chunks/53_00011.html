That shall make your thrift full bare What will it do I say we shall have good cheer When we come there. When phoebus draweth into the occydentall And observed with clouds misty and dark Then trees, herbs, and grass, by course natural want their chief comfort, thus saith many a clack And likewise that man in his work Is destitute of reason, following sensual operation The last time I was in this place Prosperite unto misrule put his hole confidence He regarded not my counsel, he lacked grace which in time coming shall turn him to inconvenience with hassarders, and ryotters, he keepeth residence At clash and cards with all unthrifty game which in continuance shall bring him to shame To him yet I will resort if he be brought in poverty I shall do him all the comfort And all the help that lieth in me I will never rest till I him see But seek about from place, to place And bring him to some better grace. Colhassarde art then there Whoreson knave wilt thou not appear By my troth I had went to have found him here I hold him gone some other way And where is envy I can not him espy I trow he is with prosperity Prosperyte, nay, I may call him foolish poverty As wise as a drake I have brought him to dice, cards, and clash And ever on his side ran the loss That he is not worth a hendfull of moss Neither hath not a hole brat to his back Passion of god is it come to that This tidings maketh my heart glad In faith he hath neither gold, silver, ne plate Colhassarde and I be both at one He promised me to have halfe the game that every thing shall be divided in twain He to have the one half and I the other Then let us be partoners as brother and brother I can not say, till colhassarde come then shall we know, both all and some Here is a bag of gold so round Here in is two thousand pound Of prosperity me it won what man with me is able to make comparison Now shall I take a merchants place to occupy I trust within short space To be in credence with Englyshmen And when I am so well betrust I may borrow so much as me lust A subtle craft then find I must to convey under colour like free men Hark this knave so proud and stout that had not to his arse a hole clout when he came to this land, & now hath brought about To compare with a state Now must I have half money and half plate Nay by god there thou spoke to late None thereof from me shall scape then had I lived to long Thou promised me, when thou began Half thy winning I shall have Hold thy peace lewd knave knowest thou to whom thou dost speak
