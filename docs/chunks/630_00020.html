Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

You have heard his usage Lady, seen his lodging, and if it please you, you both may and shall confer with him. Prithee call him. My Lord, your keeper hath brought a Lady or two to see you. To see me? why am I turned monster? doth he take money to show me? what doth a take a piece trow? Why how now gallant, not gone yet? Not, I thank you Lady, and yet I was near't. How do you man? Musty for want of airing. we'll have you hanged out i'the fresh air, one of these mornings. You'd be glad to take me in then. Yes, when you had hanged abroad a little: but my Lord Hortensio (for I think I must be your Lady when all's done) what sport? I would be merry a purpose to make him mad; the room's private and fit for any exercise. udsfoot to her, can a woman offer fairer for't? Why shall we go to span-counter madam? To span-counter, best ask her and she'll go to Coits. No, I love some stirring exercise, my body's conditioned like the sun; it would never be out of motion. I have't i'faith, when I was student in Padua, We used a most ingenious pastime. The name my Lord. I cannot give it a name equal to the merit. 'tis vulgarly called Blindman's buff. Blind man's buff? ha, ha, ha, Do you laugh at it? At the happiness of your wit my Lord, that you should hit upon that sport, which of all other I delight in. Will you hear an Apology I made in the commendation of it? we'll have the thing itself first; and as we like that, we'll hear your Apology after: who shall be hoodwinked first? Who but the Author? ay, ay, none shall be blind but I; help of with my gown boy. What shall we have to blind him? My scarf, take my scarf my Lord. There's a simple favour for you. And most fit, for indeed nothing blinds lovers sooner than ladies' favours, but who shall blind me. Mary that will I my Lord, let me alone to blind you. Good again; for who should blind men, but beauteous women? come sweet madam. But how if you take me? as I know that will be your aim, if I take you prisoner madam, you must either be hoodwinked yourself, or give your conqueror a kiss for your ransom. An easy ransom: i'll not be prisoner long, if a kiss will enlarge me. Lord what scambling shift has he made for a kiss and cannot get in neither; a little higher, so, so, so, are you blind my Lord? As a purblind Poet: have amongst you blind Harpers. methinks he looks for all the world like God Cupid. Take heed of his dart madam, he comes upon you, He cannot come to fast, O I am taken prisoner.
