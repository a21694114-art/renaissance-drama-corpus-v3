What should a man zay? who should he trust In these days? Hark you Iohn Clay, if you have Done any such thing, tell troth, and shame the Devil. Vaith do: my Gossip Turf zaies well to you Iohn. Speak man, but do not convesse, nor be auraid. A man is a man, and a beast's a beast, look to't. I' the name of men, or beasts! what do you do? Hare the poor fellow out on his five wits, And seven senses? Do not weep Iohn Clay. I swear the poor wretch is as guilty from it, As the Child was, was borne this very morning. Not, as I am a kyrsin soul, would I were hanged If ever I— alas I! would I were out Of my life, so I would I were, and in again— Nay, Mistress Audrey will say nay to that. Not, In-and-out? an you were out o' your life, How should she do for a husband? who should fall Aboard o' her than, Ball? He's a Puppy? Not; Hannibal has no breeding: well! I say little; But hitherto all goes well, pray it prove no better. Come Father; I would we were married: I am a cold. Well, Mr. Constable, this your fine Groom here, Bridegroom, or what Groom else, soe'er he be, I charge him with the felony; and charge you To carry him back forthwith to Paddington, Unto my Captain, who stays my return there: I am to go to the next Justice of peace, To get a warrant to raise Hue and Cry, And bring him, and his fellows all afore ' hun. Far you well Sir, and look to ' hun I charge you, As you'll answer it. Take heed; the business If you defer, may prejudicial you Moore than you thinke-for, zay I told you so. Here's a Bride-ale indeed! Ah son Iohn, son Clay! I little thought you would ha' proved a piece Of such false metal. Father, will you believe me? Would I might never stir i' my new shoes, If ever I would do so voule a fact. Well Neighbours, I do charge you to assist me With ' hun to Paddington. Be he a true man, so: The better for ' hun. I will do mine office, An he were my own begotten a thousand times. Why, do you hear man? Husband? Mr. Turf! What shall my Daughter do? Puppy, stay here. Mother, I'll go with you, and with my Father. Nay, stay sweet Mrs. Audrey: here are none But one friend (as they zay) desires to speak A word, or two, cold with you: How do you veele Yourself this frosty morning? What ha' you To do to ask, I pray you? I am a cold. It seems you are hot, good Mistress Audrey. You lie; I am as cold as Ice is: Feel else. Nay, you ha' cooled my courage: I am past it, I ha' done feeling with you.
