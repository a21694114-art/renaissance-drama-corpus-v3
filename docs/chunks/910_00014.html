Fear not, I will not speak Ought from thy lips what ere they open to me. But gentle Daphne, for the dear day's sake Of thy past youth, help me to help Aminte Poor wretch that dies. Ah what a propper style Of conjuration (fool) hast thou devised To move me with; bringing my youth to mind, The pleasure I have lost, and pain I find. But what wouldst have me do? thou'rt not to seek of wit, nor yet of power, Do but dispose thy will, I'll ask no more. Well then, I'll tell thee: we are going now Silvia and I together all alone Unto Diana's fount, to wash ourselves; There where the planetree with his safer shade Ore-spreds the cool stream, and is wont t'invite The weary huntresses to rest, and cool them: There she'll uncase her so-beloved limbs. And what of that? What of that? silly thou'rt Or else thou wouldst not ask me what of that. Suppose I hit thy meaning, who knows yet If he will dare to meet her there or no? No? Why then truly let him stay till she Come to woee him; and when will that be trow ye? Do she or not, he does deserve she did. But now let's leave this theme; and talk a word Or two of thee; say Thirsis, wilt not thou Resolve at last to be a lover too? thou'rt not yet old; few more than thirty years Have ouer-slipt thee, and I well remember Thy infancy; wilt thou live joyless still? For only he lovers is the happy life. The joys of Venus he enjoys as well, That shunning lovers painful miseries, Tastes of the sweet, and lets the sour alone. O but that sweet grows dull, and gluts betime, That is not seas'ned with a little sour. Better 'tis to be glutted (of the two) Then pine before one feeds, and after two. But if the food be pleasing, and possessed, 'Tis good before; and in the tasting best. No man can so possess what he desires, As just t'enjoy it then when's hunger craves it. Who hopes to find, that never means to seek? 'Tis dangerous to seek that which once found, Pleases a little, but not found, torments Much more; no, no, I'll go no more a wooing; Cupid shall triumph over me no more; I know a little what those sufferings be, Let others prove them if they list for me. Belike thoust not enjoyed loves pleasure yet: Nor do I wish to buy the plague so dear. You may perhaps be forced against your will; Who keeps himself far off, cannot be forced. Who can be far from love? Who fears & flies. But what avails to fly from him hath wings? Love but new borne, hath wings but short & small, And hardly strong enough to fly with all.
