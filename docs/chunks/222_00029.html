He is my lord, and hath discharged his troops Which came with him, but as your Grace did say, To heave the duke of Somerset from hence, And to subdue the rebels that were up. Then welcome cousin York, give me thy hand, And thanks for thy great service done to us, Against those traitorous Irish that rebelled. Long live Henry in triumphant peace, Lo here (my Lord) upon my bended knees, I here present the traitorous head of Cade, That hand to hand in single fight I slew. First, thanks to heaven, and next, to thee my friend, That hast subdued that wicked traitor thus, Oh let me see that head that in his life Did work me and my land such cruel spite: A visage stern, coal black his curled locks, Deep trenched furrows in his frowning brow, Presageth warlike humours in his life. Here take it hence, and thou for thy reward, Shalt be immediately created Knight: Kneel down my friend, and tell me what's thy name. Alexander Eyden, if it please your grace, A poor Esquire of Kent. Then rise up sir Alexander Eyden Knight, And for thy maintenance, I freely give A thousand marks a year for to maintain thee, Beside the firm reward that was proclaimed, For those that could perform this worthy act, And thou shalt wait upon the person of the King. I humbly thank your grace, and I no longer live. Then I prove just and loyal unto my King. O Buckingham, see where Somerset comes. Bid him go hide himself till York be gone. He shall not hide himself for fear of York, But beard and brave him proudly to his face. Who's that? proud Somerset at liberty? Base fearful Henry that thus dishonor'st me, By heaven, thou shalt not govern over me, I cannot brook that traitor's presence here, Nor will I subject be to such a King, That knows not how to govern, nor to rule, Resign thy crown proud Lancaster to me, That thou usurped haste so long by force, For now is York resolved to claim his own, And rise aloft into fair England's Throne. Proud traitor, I arrest thee on high treason, Against thy Sovereign Lord, yield thee false York. For here I swear thou shalt unto the Tower, For these proud words which thou hast given the King. Thou art deceived, my sons shall be my bail, And send thee there in spite of him. Ho, where are you boys? Call Clifford hither presently. Long live my noble Lord and sovereign King. We thank thee Clifford: Nay, do not affright us with thy looks, If thou didst mistake, we pardon thee, kneel again. Why, I did no way mistake, this is my King: What is he mad? to Bedlam with him. Yea, a bedlam frantic humour drives him thus, To levy Arms against his lawful King. Why do not your grace send him to the Tower? He is arrested, but will not obey, His sons he saith shall be his surety.
