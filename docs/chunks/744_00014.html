Make use of me, you shall not want for help. You seem to be of a Majestic state, What should a poor distressed Gentlewoman, Trouble a person of such eminence? Thy state wants present pity, women take it I have seen a King ere now, And by your Diadem you should be one: Pray, rather let me die then trouble you. 'tis perfect charity to help the poor: Yet by these jewels, you should bear a place, If not amongst the Royal, with the Noble. Indeed I am but a poor Gentlewoman, Punished for wearing jewels in a storm: But I have lost a husband whom I loved, For marrying whom, I have endured this cross; And now his friends, if they should find me out, Would finish what the storm has left undone. His name? 'twas Infortunio, as mine is, With the alteration of a letter only. Good Infortuna go along with me, I'll find some help for this thy misery. May the Sea never use you of this fashion; I take your courtesy, and will attend. Young, fair, and lovely, is she not Poitiers? She is a comely, and a sweet Gentlewoman. In my opinion she's the fairest creature Nature ere made. In love my gracious Liege? What and my Queen alive, that would seem strange. Love does regard no person, nor the time. Love is a power will overrule a King. Finding her honest, though of mean estate, You may do well to raise poor virtue up, And marry her to some great Nobleman. I'll think of that hereafter: now, the news? The Emperor and the Electors are arrived. Health to the Majesty and Seat of France. As welcome hither is your Mightiness, As if you were arrived in Germany. I thank King Iohn. Saxon, with Mentz, and Trier. We rest your loving friend for War. For Counsel. And Counsel is as great a friend as War. It hath prevailed as much. Thrice welcome all. The storm has kept us overlong at Sea: But Mighty King of France, worse storms than these Have and will shake us, if you help us not: All things go Backwards, that should bode us good; And he that is Conqueror already, The haughty palsgrave, is to England sailed, To join with Edward in our overthrow. We have felt the valour of the English King, And of his son, the Black Prince now deceased: Witness Poitiers and Cressey, where our blood Royal, although it be saved to make clay Moist with the showers, and temper the dry earth: When I and all my sons were prisoners ta'en, And had to England to be wondered at: Ransomed although I was, it grieves me much I cannot do the like unto my foe. Let your French Soldiers join themselves with ours, And we'll invade his Kingdom. And constrain him & the Palsgrave to the like disgrace. Fortune was never steadfast unto any. But like the Ocean that bounds in the Land, Both ebbs and flows according to the Moon.
