I assure you Iacob, the man is very weak. But hark once again, methink I hear them speak. I promise you I fear his life be already past. marry God forbid. Lo now they come at last. If ye believe not me, see yourself where he is. Fie brother Esau, what a folly is this? About vain pastime to wander abroad, and peak, Till with hunger you make yourself thus faint & weak. Brother Iacob, I pray you chide now no longer, But give me somewhat wherewith to slake mine hunger, Alack brother, I have in my little cottage, Nothing but a mess of gross and homely pottage. Refresh me therewithal, and boldly ask of me, The best thing that I have, whatsoever it be. I were a very beast, when thou my life dost save, If I should stick with thee for the best thing I have. Can ye be content to sell your birthright to me? Hold, here is my hand, I do sell it here to thee. With all the profits thereof henceforth to be thine, As free, as full, as large, as ever it was mine. Then swear thou hand in hand before the living Lord, This bargain to fulfil, and to stand by thy word. Before the Lord I swear, to whom each heart is known That my birthright that was, from henceforth is thine own. Thou shalt also with me by this promise indent, With this bargain and sale to hold thyself content. If each penny thereof might be worth twenty pound, I willingly to thee surrender it this stound. And if each cycle might be worth a whole talon, I promise with this sale to hold me content. Come, let us set him on foot that he may go sup. Nay, first I will know a thing, ere I help him up. Sirrah, will ye eat folk when ye are long fasting? No, I pray thee help me up, and leave thy jesting. No trow, eat your brother Iacob now if you lust. For you shall not eat me, I tell you, that is just. Come, that with my pottage thou mayst refreshed be. There is no meat on earth that so well liketh me. Yet I may tell you, it is pottage dearly bought. No not a whit, for my bargain take thou no thought. I defy that birthright that should be of more price, Than helping of one's self, I am not so unwise. And how then sir, shall poor Ragau have no meat? Yes, and if thou canst my brother Iacob entreat. God grant I have enough for Esau alone.
