Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

How? Will you have the truth out, he is married to a whore i'faith. I should laugh at that. Uncle, let me perish in your favour if you find it not so and that 'tis I that have married the honest woman. Ha? I'd walk ten mile afoot to see that i'faith. And see't't you shall, or I'll near see you again. A Quean i'faith? ha, ha, ha. Pup, pup, pup, pup, I like not this wine, is there never a better Teirs in the house. Yes sir, there are as good Teirs in the house, as any are in England. Desire your mistress you knave, to taste 'em all over, she has better Skill. Has she so, the better for her. and the worse for you. Arthur, is the cupboard of plate set out, all's in order sir. I am in love with my Liveries every time I think on 'em, they make a gallant show by my troth.— Niece. Do you call sir? Prithee show a little diligence, and overlook the knaves a little, they'll filch and steal today and send whole pasties home to their wives, and thou be'st a good Niece, do not see me purloined. Fear it not sir, I have cause, though the feast be prepared for you, yet it serves fit for my wedding dinner too. Master Lamprey, and Master Spichcocke two the most welcome gentlemen alive, your fathers and mine were all free ah Fishmongers. They were indeed sir, you see bold guests sir, soon entreated And that's best sir— how now sirrah? there's a coach come toth' door sir. My Lady Foxstone a my life. Mistress Iane Hoord, wife, mass 'tis her Ladyship indeed, madam you are welcome to an unfurnished house, dearth of cheer, scarcity of attendance. You are pleased to make the worst sir. Wife. Is this your Bride. Yes Madam salute my Lady Foxtone. Please you Madam a while to taste the air in the garden? 'twill please us well. Who would not wed the most delicious life, No joys are like the comforts of a wife. So we bachelors think that are not troubled with them Your worship's brother with another ancient Gentleman, are newly alighted Sir. Master Onesiphorus Hoord, why now our company begins to come in: my dear and kind brother welcome i'faith. You see we are men at an hour brother. ay, I'll say that for you brother you keep as good an hour to come to a feast, as any Gentleman in the sheer. what old Master Limber and Master Kicks, do we meet i'faith jolly Gentlemen? We hope you lack guess sir? Oh welcome, welcome, we lack still such guess as your worships. Ah sirrah brother, have you catched up widow Medler. From 'em all brother, and I may tell you, I had mighty enemies, those that stuck sore, old Lucre is a sore fox I can tell you brother. Where is she, i'll go seek her out, I long to have a smack at her lips.
