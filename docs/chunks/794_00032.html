With the most ingenuous freedom of a Poet, I accept it: Grammaticus, that our contention ending in love, may make a tragic-comedy, I drink to you. I protest to you, Sir, I do put all former wrongs in the preterpluperfect Tense, and am glad of this happy Conjunction, and that we are all of us in such a merry Mood: but by the way, my Masters, these noun-adjectives of the Feminine gender, sit all this while undrunk to: Astronomia.— Intruth, Grammaticus, I am not in Case to pledge you: I pledged Astrologia even now, and I am not since half well. Arithmetica— If you Count again, you shall find that I drunk last. Rhetorica— here's to moisten your eloquent tongue. An eloquent tongue is never dry, Astrologia will pledge you for me. Astrologia— In troth I have been drinking my Belly full of Nectar; but just now, my thoughts were upon the present Conjunction of Mars and Venus. Why how now, Grammaticus! who do you drink to? faith thou art now a Noun Substantive indeed, for thou standst alone by thyself, without being joined to any of these Adjectives. Nay, do not you jest. What? dost thou make a jester of me? Nay, I Conjure you both; by our present meeting, that you go not out of the Circle of harmless mirth. methinks I see a Direct line pass from the Eye of geometre's to Astronomia's. Nay, will you, Poeta? you make Astronomia blush. Some Aqua vitae, I say, for geometre's. Why, Poeta? Why, he's a dying I think, his eyes are fixed in's head already. It may be, Poeta, you measure geometre's his looks by your own. methinks I see a Direct line pass from the Eye of geometre's to Astronomia's. I'm e'en stifled, I do not use to be in such a close Room, I love the Open Air. Alas! Astronomia's extreme ill. Friends, you are all heartily welcome, rest you here I pray, and we'll in with her. Astrologia, follow her, and see you be never from her all the while she's sick. I saw this disastrous chance in the stars, for as Mars and Venus were sporting, they were beheld by the rest of the envious gods. I'll in too, to sit and Talk with her, whiles she's sick. I'll in too, that I may— Be made sit down again. Alas, my Mistress! she did not look well. Astronomia sick? than all the heaven's awry, and my music's quite out of tune. 'Twas, I fear me, a fit of an Ague. Astronomia in a fit of an Ague? I never understood the Motus trepidationis of the Heaven before. Musicians, depart the room. The Musicians go out. By Ioue I came to be merry, and I will be merry. Here's an health to Astronomia. Here's a health to Astronomia. Here's a health to Astronomia. Sir, you wrong us all, not to take off your full measure. Oh, Sir, they that drink with Measure, drink without Measure.
