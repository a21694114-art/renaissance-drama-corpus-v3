Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Your service? good! I think you'll writ to her shortly, Your very loving and obedient Mother. Come, sand your Maids of, I will have 'em sent Home again wife: I love no trains o' Kent, Or Christendom, as they say. We will not back, And leave our Dame. Why should her worship lack Her tail of Maids, more than you do of men? What, mutinin Madge? Zend back your C'lons again. And we will vollow. Else we'll guard our Dame. I ha' zet the nest of wasps all on a flame. Come, you are such another Mr. Turf: A Clod you should be called, of a high Constable: To let no music go afore your child, To Church, to cheer her heart up this cold morning. You are for Father Rosin, and his consort Of fiddling Boys, the great Feats, and the less: Because you have entertained 'em all from Highgate. To show your pomp, you'd ha' your Daughter, and Maids Dance over the fields like Fays, to Church this frost? I'll ha' no rondels, I, i' the Queen's paths; Let 'un scrape the Gut at home, where they ha' filled it At afternoon. I'll ha' 'em play at dinner. She is i' th' right, Sir; vor your wedding dinner Is starved without the Music. If the Pies Come not in piping hot, you ha' lost that Proverb. I yield to truth: wife are you sussified? A right good man! when he knows right, he loves it. And he will know't, and show't too by his place Of being high Constable, if no where else. Well over-taken, Gentlemen! I pray you, Which is the Queen's High Constable among you? The tallest man: who should be else, do you think? It is no matter what I think, young Clown: Your answer favours of the Cart. How? Cart? and Clown? Do you know whose team you speak to? Not: nor I care not: Whose Jade may you be? Jade? Cart? and Clown? O for a lash of whipcord! Three-knotted cord! Do you mutter? Sir, snorle this way; That I may hear, and answer what you say, With my schoole-dagger, 'bout your Costard Sir. Look to't, young grouse: I'll lay it on, and sure; Take't of who's will. Nay, pray you Gentleman—. Go too: I will not bate him an ace on't. What? Rowle-powle? Mapleface? All fellows? Do you hear friend, I would wish you, vor your good, Tie up your brinded Bitch there, your don rusty Pannyer-hilt poinard: and not vex the youth With showing the teeth of it. We now are going To Church, in way of matrimony, some on us: Tha ' rung all in a'ready. If it had not, All the horn beasts are grazing i' this close, Sold not ha' pull ' me hence, till this Ash-plant Had rung noon o' your pate, Mr. Broome-beard. That would I feign zee, quoth the blind George Of Holloway: Come Sir. O their naked weapons! For the passion of man, hold Gentleman, and Puppy. Murder, O Murder!
