Thanks noble Warwick, and York doth hope to see The Earl of Warwick live, to be the greatest man in England, but the King. Come let's go. Stand forth dame Elinor Cobham duchess of Gloster, & hear the sentence pronounced against thee for these treasons, that thou hast committed 'gainst us, our States, and Peers. First; for thy heinous crimes, thou shalt two days in London do penance barefoot in the streets, with a white sheet about thy body, & a wax taper burning in thy hand: that done, thou shaltbe banished for ever into the I'll of Man, there to end thy wretched days, and this is our sentence irrevocable. Away with her. Grieve not noble uncle, but be thou glad, In that these treasons thus are come to light, Lest God had poured his vengeance on thy head, For her offences that thou heldst so dear. Oh gracious Henry, give me leave awhile, To leave your grace, and to depart away. For sorrows tears hath gripped my aged heart, And makes the fountains of mine eyes to swell, And therefore good my Lord let me depart. With all my heart good uncle, when you please, Yet ere thou goest, Humphrey resign thy staff, For Henry will be no more protected, The Lord shall be my guide, both for my land and me. My staff, yea noble Henry, my life and all, As erst thy noble father made it mine, And even at willing at thy feet I leave it, As others would ambitiously receive it, And long hereafter when I am dead and gone, May honourable peace attend thy throne. Uncle Gloster, stand up and go in peace, No less beloved of us, then when Thou wert Protector over my land. Take up the staff, for here it ought to stand, Where should it be but in King Henry's hand? Please it your majesty, this is the day That was appointed for the combating, Between the Armourer and his man, my Lord, And they are ready when your grace doth please. Then call them forth, that they may try their rights. Here neighbour Horner, I drink to you in a cup of sack. And fear not neighbour, you shall do well enough. And here neighbour, here's a cup of Charneco. Here's a pot of good double beer, neighbour drink and be merry, and fear not your man. Let it come, i'faith I'll pledge you all, And a fig for Peter. Here Peter, I drink to thee, and be not afeard. Here Peter, here's a pint of Claret wine for thee. And here's a quart for me, and be merry Peter, And fear not thy master, fight for credit of the prentices. I thank you all, but I'll drink no more, Here Robin, and if I die, here I give thee my hammer, And Will, thou shalt have my apron, and here Tom, Take all my money that I have. O Lord bless me, I pray God, for I am never able to deal with my master, he hath learned so much fence already.
