But let a fine fool that's well feathered come, and withal good meat, I have a friend it may be, that may compassionate his wants, I'll tell ye an old Saw for't over my chimney yonder, a poor man seem to him that's poor, and prays thee for to lend, but tell the prodigal (not quite spent) thou wilt procure a friend. Trust me a thrifty saw. Many will have virtuous admonitions on their walls, but not a piece in their coffers, give me these witty politic saws, and indeed my house is furnished with no other. How happy shall I be to wed such wisdom. Shall bed it, shall bed it wench, shalt ha''t by infusion, look, look. Save ye Master Tim. Who's this, goodman file the blacksmith, I thought 'had been our old collier: did you go to bed with that dirty face goodman file? And rise with it too sir. What have you bumming out there goodman file? A vice sir, that I would fain be furnished with a little money upon't. Why how will you do to work then goodman file? This is my spare vice, not that I live by. Hum. ye did not buy this spare vice of a lean Courtier, did ye? No sir, of a fat Cook, that strained of a Smith for's rent. Oh hard hearted man of grease. Nay, nay, Sim, we must do't sometimes. Ha thrifty whoreson. And what wood serve your turn goodman file? A Noble Sir. What upon a spare vice to lend a Noble? Why sir, for ten groats you may make yourself drunk, and so buy a vice outright for half the money. That is no noble vice I assure ye. How long wood ye have it! But a fortnight, 'tis to buy stuff I protest sir. Look ye being a neighbour, and borne one for another. Ha villain, shalt have all. There is five shillings upon't, which at the fortnight's end goodman File you must make five shillings six pence. How sir? Nay and it were not to do you a courtesy— Ha Boy. And then I had forgot three pence for my Bill, so there is four pence, and nine pence, which you are to tender back, five shillings six pence, goodman File at the end of the fortnight. Well and it were not for earnest necessity, hay boys, I come, I come, you black rascals, let the Cans go round. Sim, because the man's an honest man, I pray lay up his Vice, as safe as it were your own. And if he miss his day, and forfeit, it shall be yours and your heirs for ever. What, disbursing money boy? here is thy mother in law Your nose drops, 'twill spoil her ruff. Pray forsooth— what's o'clock. Oh fie upon him Mistress, I thought he had begun to ask you blessing. Peace, weele have more oa't. I wonot kiss indeed. And he wonot, here are those that will forsooth. Get ye in you Rogue. I hope ye will Sir? I was bred in Ireland where the women begin the salutation. I wonot kiss truly. Indeed you must.
