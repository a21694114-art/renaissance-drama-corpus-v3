Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Shall I alone to do, forbidden be That every patch may do? that grieveth me No trifling toys the people looks to have Of him, that ought to rule with wisdom grave. It pleaseth us with daunted power to try, If people's rash conceived rage will fly. Seek rather for to please and calm their mood. Ill ruled is that reign where people wood, Their subject Prince doth wield, as they think good When nought that they require they can obtain, They justly then aggrieved are again. That gentle prayers cannot win with ease, By force to wring it out, it doth us please. An hard thing 'tis the people not to have That of their Prince, which they do justly crave. And horrible 'tis a Prince to be constrained. Let not your subjects then so sore be reigned. Why then the common brute abroad will be. How that the people have subdued me. That no man trusts that is of credit light. Be it so, yet many it marks with deadly spite. With country peers to meddle it is afraid, To quip and frump, 'tis nothing less dismayed. Your grace may easily couch that budding bruit Let Sainted sire's deserts with pliant suit, Your grace's mind remove: let spouses age, And courteous bashful shame disrump your rage. Leave off (I say) that we intend to grudge. For now your talk our patience moveth much: I pray you let it lawful be to do, That Senec giveth not advise unto. And we our people's wishes do defer, While Poppy feel in wombling womb to star, The pledge of faithful love to me and her. Why do we not appoint the morrow next, whenas our marriage pomp may be context? THrough paunch of rivened earth, from Pluto's reign With ghostly steps, I am returned again. In writhled wrists, that blood do most desire, For guiding wedlock vile with Stygian fire. Let Poppy, which these cressets coupled sure, Unto my son be joined in marriage pure: Whom mother's grief, and hand revenging wracks, Shall send with heave and hoe to funeral stacks I always do remember well beneath Where piteous, ghostly, crawling souls do breathe, Th'unkindly slaughterous deed, which to our sprite Yet unrevenged is grievous and of right: And for the good I did a cruel prize, That deadly framed ship in crafty wise: And due reward that he gave me again, For helping him to rule of Empyres reign: And eke that night, whenas I did bewail, Both loss of ship wherein we then did sail, And mates unhappy death, and while I thought, For this accursed deed to have beseeched The Gods to trickling tears he gave scant time But twice increased hath his devilish crime. Quite slain with sword, thrust through my bodies bounds And filthy laid through gory mattering wounds, Delivered safe from seas, devouring sup, In antic court my ghost I yielded up. Nor yet his cankered, and unsatiate hate. For all this blood doth Nero once abate.
