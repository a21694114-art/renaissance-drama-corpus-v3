Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Fair play is for Fencers. Yet thou seemst a good fellow. Thou shalt have it. Stand aside, partner. sayst thou me so, boy? than there's money, win't and wear't. Now could I get in and rob 'em both— Hercules! He lays about him like Orlando Furioso, or a coward turned desperate. Brave boy i'faith. we might ha'robbed two and twenty Taffeta-cloaked roarers, before this freese-jacket. Oh, your surly Boar is like a bloodied Mastiff: when your spruce pantaloon bawls like a whelp in a Tavern: yet at the sight of cold iron runs, as if he had seen a Sergeant. Hold, hold: Keep your cash. The money's good money Sir, if it be not too heavy for you Nay, for the weight I could make shift, but for the scurvy conditions go with it. have you any gall to't Sir? Not not so much as pigeon. Put up thy cash my brave quintessence of Hobbinolls. Give me thy hand. How many thousand cudgels hast thou broken i' thy days about a Maypole? I warrant, as many as would make all Bedford chimneys smoke a whole winter. jest on. Ha' you any more to say to me. Nothing my brave Clem o' th' Clough, but I would thou wouldst deal with us. Say, shall we put all our stocks together, and set out a ship of our own? Ha? first tell me truly what you are. Agreed. Let's sit down to counsel. I am the Abbot of Newnham. How? much? I'll not bate you an Ace on't, till the old Abbot returns from Pilgrimage. My chamber shall be our Rendezvous. The devil himself in the shape of a blurting Constable will not look for us there. I am a soldier, and in this vacation time am forced to do like Lawyers; when suits do not make them, they make suits: because the wars will not maintain me, I maintain the wars. I set up my Bills in Bedford here, for a Physician, and dealt with Gripe for the Gout. I have a project to swell our purses till they burst. Will you second me? As inseparably, as a condition does an obligation. I have often heard the gripulous Dotard talk of Fairies: and how rich the house proves that they haunt. I have ripened the blister of his imagination to the full. Shall we lance it? I have keys that shall secure our conveyance. Is't a match? The safest stratagem we could devise, By craft, more than by strength, all thieves do rise. Of many politic knaves you cannot spy one. The Fox will have his prey before the Lion. Two or three nights we'll scatter some small pieces of silver, till opportunity plump our project. I take it rightly. Oh 'tis quick and sharp. So with a Gudgeon lost, we'll catch a carp. A booty.
