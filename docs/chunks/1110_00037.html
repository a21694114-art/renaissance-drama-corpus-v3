Run for the Doctor Geoffrey. Let him stay, Death's Image cannot show so ghastly to me As would the shape of Doctor, I'll have none. Nay sweet Nan be persuaded. If he come Good faith I'll live no longer. Sooth her up, Or she will grow to frenzy. How doth my sweet heart? Sick, oh sick at heart, and you keep such a buzzing In my ears, that I can take no rest; Would you would leave me. See here's my Lord. From whom, if't Be his will, the Lord deliver me. The maid talks idly. What would my honey have? Your absence, Mother, and yours, and all of you; I am but dead Unless you give me rest; oh sick, sick, sick, Your tongues like untuned Bells, sound in my ears, Nothing but Grave and Church-yard. Whom wilt have To watch with thee all night? None but my Father, All tongues are noise and discords saving his; But his like charming Music, quietly Summons my eyes to rest; and could I sleep once I hope I should be better, oh. 'Tis best to humour her. I shall disclaim all friendship with that tongue That speaks another syllable; this prating It strikes quite through my head, and makes it beat As it would fall in pieces. Silence all. Mother, you talk of silence, and yet speak; how can that be? Mum. if you were sick at heart as I, you'd Say mum were a word, Mum sounds to me, being sick, Loud as a Cannon; why so? I thank you all, indeed you Do me now a wondrous kindness; I pray, pray for me, I shall be well at midnight, well at heart; And at head too I hope: and Mother, nay reply not, I'm like the man that could endure no noise Inth' silent woman, answer all in signs; Mother I hope ere morning I shall find Some more hearts ease; remember when you see't, I told you of't before hand: I would sleep: Hand in my bed, I'll turn to the wall, and try if I can Sleep, so good night all. So, softly as you can; some little rest Will bring her to her temper, for this night We will commit her to her Father's charge, since she so Much desires it. Any thing to give her least content. Thou know'st my mind, what thou hast undertook Manage with good discretion. 'Tis as safe Here in my breast as under bolts and locks, The cunningest picklock tongue that ever spoke Shall turn these springs to ope them; farewell sir. Farewell good Roger, but no word to' your Master Of what I have revealed thee kept from him. As I would do a kindness, lay't in me, From my old fellow Geoffrey. Now my Lord; I am still labouring for the general Good, to have my house at peace, and you content; But Wife, how is't with Nan?
