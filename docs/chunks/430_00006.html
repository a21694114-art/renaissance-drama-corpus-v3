Yes mass her's one, beside 'tis a goodly great long hand. So has the Duke a goodly huge hand, I have shook him by it, (God forgive me) ten thousand times: he must pass like Alexander of Saint Magnus; Well Sir, ('tis your duty to stand bare) the Duke has sent his fist to me, and I were a Iew if I should shrink for it; I obey, you must pass, but pray take heed with what dice you pass, I mean what company, for Sathan is most busy, where he finds one like himself. your name Sir? Lazarillo de Tormes in Castille, cozen Germane to the Adolantado of Spain. Are you so Sir? God's blessing on your heart: your name again Sir, if it be not too tedious for you? Lazarillo de Tormes in Castille, cousin-german to the Spanish Adolantado. I warrant he's a great man in his own Country. Has a good name, Slubber set it down: write, Lazarus in torment at the Castle, and a cozening German, at the sign of the Palantido diddle in Spain: So Sir you are engrossed, you must give my Officer a groat it's nothing to me signor. I will cancel when it comes to a sum. Well Sir, well he shall give you an Item for't, make a bill and he'll tear it he says. Most admirable Blurt, I am a man of war and profess fighting. I charge you in the Duke's name keep the peace. By your sweet favour most dear Blurt, you charge too fast, I am a hanger on upon Mars, and have a few Crowns. Two: his own and mine. And desire you to point out a fair lodging for me and my Train. 'tis my Office signor to take men up a nights, but if you will, my Maids shall take you up a mornings, since you profess fighting; I will commit you signor to mine own house, but will you pitch and pay, or will your Worship run— I scorn to run from the face of Thamer Cham. Then Sir, you mean not to run? signor no. Bear witness Slubber, that his answer is signor no: So now if he runs upon the score, I have him strait upon signor no; this is my house signor, enter. March excellent Blurt: attend Pilchard. Upon your trencher signor, most hungerly. Now sirrah where's thy Master? The Constable has priest him. What, for a Soldier? ay, for a Soldier; but ere he'll go, I think indeed, he & I together shall press the Constable. No matter, squeeze him, and leave no more liquour in him, than in a dried neats tongue: Sirrah thin-gut, what's thy name? My name? you chops, why I am of the blood of the Pilchers. Nay 'sfoot, if one should kill thee, he could not be hanged for't, for he would shed no blood, there's none in thee: Pilchard? thouart a most pitiful dried one. I wonder thy master does not slice thee, and swallow thee for an Anchoves.
