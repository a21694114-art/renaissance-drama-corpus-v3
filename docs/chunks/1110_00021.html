You hear that news of M. Slightall's frenzy, and his undoing? And yet your wisdom would have matched your Daughter unto that spendthrift Beggar. This his servant, since entertained by me hath told me all. And nothing more than truth. Use you your humours, And jest at his distress; but when I think What he hath been of late, what come to now, I cannot choose but sorrow; and the more When I Record the ground of his distress; But my soul's clear of all. You are a Noble Thief. Ha? You are a gentle fool. How? I am as cold as Ice, and you a scold. Minion. how? You are a Trencher friend. That meant by me? And thou a slave and Pander. Speak it not, I'll not believe it Mistress. This I'll prove. Why Daughter, daughter? Sure the girl's grown frantic. Faith mother a mad wench, I thank my stars. Star me no stars. Why mother, can you scold? Yes for a need. But Mistress Changeable, why did you call me these? Stand but in row, and as I am a woman I'll make all this good; you here, you there, And every one in order: First, in particular, And next in general I will go over you. I pray you do. A noble Thief, that was your Character, Some by the high way rob; some are Sea Thieves, We commonly call'em Pirates; some break houses, And others snap at stalls; some cunningly Dive into Pockets, whistlers, others lifts; Some are Poetical Thieves, and steal by wit, One from another plots, and projects, cheats, And decoys; but all these under Thieves, And steal but petty trash: but you more great, Under pretext of your Nobility, And countenance in Court, have from a Husband Stolen a contracted and a married Wife; For Contract upon Earth, in Heaven is marriage; And celebrate by Angels. But why fool? A gentle fool, such are your patient Husbands, That yield their wives the Breeches. Is he such? how now bold housewife, baggage, peevish Thing, rude, disobedient, apish, and perverse, Irregular, hare brained, harsh and obstinate? You see, she need not put me to my proof, Her tongue will do't itself. But Trencher friend? I pray your name? Take but the two first letters from your name, I take't, 'tis Eat-well. But all this Mistress, makes not me a Pander. No, but when thou first was't base bawd to the riots Of thy first Master, thou mad'st thyself such. But now in general let me see; The Prince is not without his flatterer, The Noble man his Secretary, The Lawyer his attorney, The Justice his clerk, The physician his Apothecary, The Usurer his Scrivener, The Extortioner his Broker, Nor the Lady cannot be without her Gentleman Usher; Your citizen's Wife her journeyman, Your Country Wench her Sweetheart, Your Tobacco woman her Pipe-maker, And every Whore her Pander. Farewell Geoffrey; God be with you Gentlefolks.
