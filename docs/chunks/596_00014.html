How is't Fleir? O my Lord, you are a welcome man. Save you O signeur. O my gracious knight, and whither are you two bound now? Faith e'en to your two Ladies signeur, Yea? and will you to the southward i'faith? will you to the confines of Italie my Gallants? take heed how ye go Northwards, 'tis a dangerous Coast, jest not with't in Winter, therefore go Southwards my Gallants, Southwards hoe: I have shipped two Gallants in a storm, I fear they have spent their main Masts by this time, and are coming home again: but if you will Southwards, my hearts of gold, I'll ship you in pomp, I'll send ye under the very line, where the sun's at hottest. But come, shall we go see thy Ladies, Fleir? ay, ay, ay, Come: but my good Lord you'll be a welcome man, for I have heard her often swear, that had she such a Husband, a man so richly decked in virtuous ornaments, she would forsake this life, herself, nay, her very being, to be yours; O my good Lord, she loves you dearly. Pheu, but I cannot requite it, Why my good Lord? she's a common thing. But say she may turn my Lord. She has been so much worn, she's not worth the turning now. O my Lord, penitence doth purge a spotted soul, and better leave sin late then not at all: and I do know my Lord, that for your love from her immodest life she'll turn. ay, ay, I doubt not but she'll turn: but 'twill be like a Buzzard Hawk that turns her tail to her game. Fleir, is the gentleman usher that I preferred to your Ladies in any favour with them?
