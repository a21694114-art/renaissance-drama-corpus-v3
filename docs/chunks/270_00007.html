Unbend thy musket soldier in the lock. Press me his thumbs, and make the slave confess, O I confess Lelio was shipped at our crane this night, My master Brishio took him in, I agreed with the shipmaster, made clcane his shoes, And so laid him in the rope of our crane, And let him down into the ship; And he is gone into the Florentine camp. So now let him loose, the truth apparent is. Marchetto, go prosecute the Senate's will, Attach Lord Brishio, seize upon his goods, Come break up the door. Soft gentle friend, a word or two with you, From whence proceed these troubles that arise? For young Sempronios death, my honest friend. Why gentle sir, is young Sempronio dead? Why doubts thou that, I tell thee I have sought and found it so. Where was he buried? Why Sepheards brought me tidings of his death Some ravenous beast did seize him for his pray. And what hast thou lost or gotten by his death? I have annual rents two thousand pounds, The worth in plate of twice so many more, A few such breakfasts friend, would make me rich, I'll tell thee, every day throughout the year, I'll lose a kinsman to possess so much. Than not for love thou bearest Sempronio, But for a colour of thy honest mind, Thou dost pursue Lord Lelio in this sort. A fool were I so to accuse myself, Think like a worldly man, that so it is, And so it is in deed, who longeth to be rich, Let him forget God but for a dozen years, He shall be rich, well landed, stout, and brave. Wipe out that water from thy eyes my friend. What aileth me? Why thou art blind and canst not see. Thou wilt not make me mad, I'll take my spectacles Tush they avail thee not, for thou art blind in deed, Look in thy heart and find an honest thought, Then will I say thy eyes are perfect clear, Look in thy conscience, find it not corrupt, Then thou shalt see without thy spectacles. Away, thou art a knave I say, tempt me not. Yes, but thou art an arrant covetous knave, for all mine uncle. 'tis true my Lord, I favoured Lelios' flight, My love hath crossed the rigour of your laws. Did you foreknow the penalty my Lord, That dooms you banishment and loss of goods. All this I knew, but none of this I fear, True friendship lighteneth all these burdenous harm If Lelio be escaped I fear no wants, My exile to me is liberty, Go fruits of nature, I will leave you here, Go toward children, thrive among my friends, Glut you with my excess of Vanities, Feed your unclean desires by spoiling me, I wreak them not, so Lelio live to me. Not irksome age, not limbs with sickness tired, Nor you my sons, nor all my other friends, Not fortune nor entreat shall keep me back.
