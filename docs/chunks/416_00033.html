Thou speakest this (Corisca) me to try, Not as thou think'st I am sure. But be assured Except thou showest some means how I may shun This marriage bonds, my thought's irrevocable, And I resolved am rather to die Then any way to spot my chastity. I have not seen so obstinate a fool, But since you are resolved I am agreed. But tell me do you think your Silvio is As true a friend to faith as you to chastity? Thou mak'st me smile. Silvio a friend to faith? How can that be? he's enemy to love. Silvio an enemy to love? O fool, These that are nice put thou no trust in them: loves theft is never so securely done As hidden under vail of honesty, Thy Silvio loves (good Sister) but not thee. What goddess is she? for she cannot be A mortal wight that lighted hath his love. Nor goddess, nor a Nimph. What do you tell? Know you Lisetta? She that your cattle keeps? e'en she. Can it be true? That same's his heart. Sure he's provided of a dainty love. Each day he feigns that he on hunting goes. I every morning hear his cursed horn. About noone-time when others busy are, He his companions shuns, and comes alone By a back way, unto my garden there, Where a shadow hedge doth close it in, There doth she hear his burning sighs his vows, And then she tells me all, and laughs at him. Now hear what I think good to do. Nay I Have done't for you already. You know the law That ties us to our faith, doth give us leave Finding our spouses in the act of perfidy, Spite of our friends the marriage to deny, And to provide us of an other if we list. That know I well, I have examples two, Leucipp to Ligurine, Armilla to Turingo, Their faith once broke, they took their own again. Now hear! Lisetta by my appointment hath Promised to meet th'unwary Lover here In this same Cave, and now he is the best Contented youth that lives, attending but the hour There would I have you take him. I'll be there To bear you witness ofted, for else we work In vain, so are you free from this same noisome knot Both with your honour, and your fathers too. Oh brave invention, good Corisca what's to do? Observe my words. In midst of this same cave Upon the right hand is a hollow stone, I know not if by Art or nature made, A little Cave all lined with ivy leaves, To which a little hole aloft gives light, A fit and thankful receptacle for loves theft. Prevent their coming and attend them there: I'll haste Lisetta forward, and as soon As I perceive your Silvio enter, so will I: Step you to her, and as the custom is, we'll carry both unto the Priest, and there dissolve This marriage knot. What to his father?
