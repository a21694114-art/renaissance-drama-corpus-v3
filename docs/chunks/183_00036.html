Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

I so they shall, M. Bradshaw sit you there, I pray you be content, I'll have my will. M. Mosbie sit you in my husband's seat. Susan shall thou and I wait on them, Or and thou saith the word let us sit down too. Peace we have other matters now in hand. I fear me Michael all willbe bewrayed. Tush so it be known that I shall marry thee in the Morning, I care not though I be hanged ere night. But to prevent the worst, I'll buy some ratsbane. Why Michael wilt thou poison thyself? No, but my mistress, for I fear she'll tell. Tush Michel fear not her, she's wise enough. Sirrah Michell gives a cup of bear. M. Arden, here's to your husband. My husband? What ails you woman, to cry so suddenly. Ah neighbours a sudden qualm came over my heart My husbands being forth torments my mind. I know some thing's amiss, he is not well. Or else I should have heard of him ere now. She will undo us, through her foolishness. Fear not M. Arden, he's well enough. Tell not me, I know he is not well, He was not wont for to stay thus late. Good M. Franklin go and seek him forth, And if you find him send him home to me. And tell him what a fear he hath put me in. I like not this, I pray God all be well I'll seek him out, and find him if I can. Michael how shall I do to rid the rest away? Leave that to my charge, let me alone, 'tis very late M. Bradshaw, And there are many false knaves abroad, And you have many narrow lanes to pass. Faith friend Michael and thou sayest true, Therefore I pray thee light's forth, and lends a link Michael bring them to the doors, but do not stay, You know I do not love to be alone. Go Susan and bid thy brother come, But wherefore should he come? Here is nought but fear. Stay Susan stay, and help to counsel me. Alas I counsel, fear frights away my wits, See Susan where thy quandam Master lies, Sweet Arden smeared in blood and filthy gore. My brother, you, and I, shall rue this deed. Come susan help to lift his body forth, And let our salt tears be his obsequies. Now now Alice whether will you bear him Sweet Mosbie art thou come? Then weep that will. I have my wish in that I joy thy sight. Well it hoves us to be circumspect. I for Franklin thinks that we have murdered him. ay but he can not prove it for his life, We'll spend this night in dalliance and in sport. O mistress the Maior and all the watch, Are coming towards our house with glaives & bills. Make the door fast, let them not come in, Tell me sweet Alice how shall I escape?
