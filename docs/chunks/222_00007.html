Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

What fate awaits the Duke of Suffolk? By water he shall die, and take his end. What shall betide the Duke of Somerset? Let him shun castles, safer shall he be upon the sandy plains, where castles mounted stand. Now question me no more, for I must hence again. Then down I say, unto the damned pool, Where Pluto in his fiery wagon sits, Riding amidst the singed and parched smokes, The Road of Dytas by the river Styx, There howl and burn for ever in those flames, Rise Iordane, rise, and stay thy charming spells: Sons, we are betrayed. Come sirs, lay hands on them, and bind them sure, This time was well watched: what Madam, are you there? This will be great credit for your husband, That you are plotting treasons thus with Conjurers, The King shall have a notice of this thing. See here my Lord what the devil hath writ. Give it me my Lord, i'll show it to the King: Go sirs, see them fast locked in prison. My Lord, I pray you let me go post unto the King, Unto Saint Alban's, to tell this news. Content away then, about it strait. Farewell my Lord. Who's within there? My Lord. Sirra, go will the Earl of Salisbury and Warwick to sup with me to night. I will my lord. My lord, how did your grace like this last flight? But as I cast her off the wind did rise, And 'twas ten to one old Ione had not gone out. How wonderful the Lords works are on earth. Even in these silly creatures of his hands! Uncle Gloster, how hie your hawk did soar, And on a sudden soused the partridge down. No marvel, if it please your majesty, My lord Protectors hawk do tower so well, He knows his master loves to be aloft. Faith my Lord it is but a base mind, That can soar no higher than a falcons pitch. I thought your grace would be above the clouds. Yea my lord Cardinal, were it not good Your grace could fly to heaven. Thy heaven is on earth, thy words and thoughts beat on a crown, proud Protector, dangerous Peer, to smooth it thus with King and commonwealth. How now my lord, why this is more than needs, churchmen so hot! good uncle can you dote? Why not? having so good a quarrel, and so bad a cause. As how, my lord? As you, my lord, and it like your Lordly lords Protectorship. Why Suffolk, England knows thy insolence. And thy ambition Gloster. Cease gentle Queen, and whet not on these furious Lords to wrath, for blessed are the peacemakers on earth. Let me be blessed for the peace I make, Against this proud Protector with my sword. Faith holy uncle, I would it were come to that. Even when thou darest. Dare! I tell thee Priest, Plantagenets could never brook the dare I am Plantaganet as well as thou, and son to Iohn of Gaunt.
