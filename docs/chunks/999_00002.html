Wood my girdle may break if I do. I have a mind. Niggers noggers I wonot. Nay nay, now his great oath's past, there's no talk on't, I like him ne'er the worse, there's an old saw sort, A kiss first, next the feeling sense, Crack say the purse strings, out fly the pence. But he can talk though, whose Boy are you Tim? Your boy forsooth Father. Can ye turn and wind a penny Tim. Better than yourself forsooth father. You have looked in the Church book late, how old are you Tim? Two and twenty years, three months, three days, and three quarters of an hour, forsooth Father. He has arithmetic. And Grammer too, what's Latin for your head Tim? Caput. But what for the head of a block? Caput blockhead. Do ye hear, your ear. Aura. Your eye. Occules. That's for one eye, what's Latin for two. Occulies, occulies. An admirable accidental grammarian I protest sir. This boy shall have all, I have an elder rogue that sucks and draws me, a Tavern academician: one that protests to Whores, and shires with Highway lawyers; an arrant unclarified Rogue, that drinks nothing but wicked Sack. Here's a Gentleman would speak with you, Look, look, now he's come for more money. A very hopeful house to match into wench, the Father a Knave, one son a drunkard, and other a fool. O monster Father, look if he be not drunk, the very sight of him makes me long for a cup of six. Pray Father, pray to God to bless me. Look, look, takes his brother for his Father. Alas Sir, when the drink's in, the wit's out, and none but wise children know their own Fathers. Why I am none of your Father, brother, I am Tim, do ye know Tim. Yes, umph— for a coxcomb. How wild he looks: good sir we'll take our leaves. Shalt not go faith Widow: you Cheater Rogue, must I ha' my friends frighted out my house by you, look he'll steal nothing to feast his Bawds, get ye out sirrah, there are Constables, Beadles, whips, and the College of extravaging, yclept Bridewell ye Rogue, ye Rogue there is, there is— mark that. Can you send me a Mark upon this Ring sir, and there set it down in your Book, and umph— mark that. I'll have no stolen Rings, picked out of pockets, or taken upon the way, not I. I'll give you an old saw for't. There's a Rogue mocks his Father, sirrah, get' ye gone, Sim, go let lose the Mastiff. Alas sir, he'll tear and pull out your son's throat. Better pull't out, than a halter stretched, away out of my doors rogue, I defy thee. Must you be my Mother in Law? So your father says sir. You see the worst of your eldest son, I abuse nobody. The Rogue will fall upon her. I will tell ye an old saw. Pray let's hear it. An old man is a bed full of bones, and who can it deny, By whom (umph) a young wench lies and grieves, for better company.
