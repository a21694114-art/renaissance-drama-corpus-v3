That causeth them to rest in their sin yet ever with thy strongest part runneth the ball yesterday thou canst not again call when thou art dead the gate of mercy is shut thou cannot come in Then let him stand without So of thy soul thou hast no doubt When thou seest my soul torn set on a clout if falsehood, usury, and extortion should not rout thousands in this realm should be put out The third part should not bide by saint Paul yet often falsehood hath a great fall An example by king Achab with this sooth Desired the vineyard of that poor man Naboth By counsel of Iezabell that kings wife Because he would not sell his possession Of two false witnesses he was peached of high treason And through the mouth of a false quest it rane which caused the poor man to lose both land & life After that of gods own bidding Came Helas the prophet to Achab the king Saying he should have evil ending And so he had, for by the way as he road He fell and broke his neck, where dogs lapped his blood this example to all usurers & oppnssours as thinketh me contrary Shul cause them of god sore a dread to be. Sir ye preach very holily, but your deeds be often contrary ye be so acquainted with covetous and simony That maketh us to take the same way So every evil disposed parson doth say The frailty of man doth often offend then call for grace, and shortly amend Therefore I counsel thee to pretend to repent and be sorry for thy misdeed yet thus I will my life lead For of your saying I take no heed ye will mucker up both gold and treasure ye have riches without measure And of the flesh ye have your pleasure ye can find no ways to amend yourself I you insure Therefore rebuke not me for my sin ne good God be with you, you shall not rule me O dole wit plunged by ignorance Regarding nothing by ghostly instruction Setting more his mind on worldly substance Then to the everlasting life that is to come God will strike when he list, ye know not how soon therefore to every man this counsel I give To be sorry of your sin, & do penance while ye live Here cometh envy running in laughing, & saith to conscience. Now in faith I would ye had be there Where should I have be. A better sport ye never see. Where at laugh ye so fast He to go and she after. And within a while he caught her He took of here an incroke And chopped her on the heel with his foot Anon be whypte her on the back A horsone quod she, playest thou me that And with her hele she gave him a seat That he was fain to go back again Good fellow thou art to blame Such words to have no good thou can.
