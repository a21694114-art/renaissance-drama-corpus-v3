'tis even so, and yet you are old Warwick still. O cheerful colours, see where Oxford comes. Oxford, Oxford, for Lancaster. Exit. The Gates are open, see they enter in, Let's follow them and bid them battle in the streets. No, so some other might set upon our backs, we'll stay till all be entered, and then follow them. Summerset, Summerset for Lancaster. Two of thy name both Dukes of Summerset, Have sold their lives unto the house of York, And thou shalt be the third and my sword hold. Montague, Montague, for Lancaster. Exit. Traitorous Montague, thou and thy brother Shall dearly abye this rebellious act. And lo where George of Clarence sweeps Along, of power enough to bid his brother battle. Clarence, Clarence, for Lancaster. Et tu Brute, wilt thou stab Caesar too? A parley sirrah to George of Clarence. C●m Clarence come, thou wilt if Warwick call. Father of Warwick, know you what this means▪ throw mine infamy at thee, I will not ruinated my father's house, Who gave his blood to lime the stones together, And set up Lancaster. Thinkest thou That Clarence is so harsh unnatural, To lift his sword against his brother's life, And so proud hearted Warwick I defy thee, And to my brothers turn my blushing cheeks? Pardon me Edward, for I have done amiss, And Richard do not frown upon me, For hence forth I will prove no more unconstant. Welcome Clarence, and ten times more welcome, Then if thou never hadst deserved out hate. Welcome good Clarence, this is bretherlie. Oh passing traitor, perjured and unjust. Now Warwick, wilt thou leave The town and fight? or shall we beat the Stones about thine ears? Why I am not cooped up here for defence, I will away to Barnet presently, And bid thee battle Edward it thou darest. Yes Warwick he dares, and leads the way, Lords to the field, saint George and victory. Alarms, and then enter Warwick wounded. Ah, who is ●? Come to my friend or foe, And tell me who is victor York or Warwick? Why ask I that? my ma glad body shows, That I must yield my body to the earth. And by my fall the conquest to my foes, Thus yields the Cedar to the axes edge. Whose arms give shelter to the princely eagle, Under whose 〈◊〉 add the ramping Lion slept, Whose top branch overpeerd Ioues sreading tree. The wrinkles in my brows now filled with blood, Were likened oft to kingly sepulchres. For who lived king, but I could dig his grave? And who durst smile, when Warwick bent his brow? Lo now my glory smeerd in dust and blood, My parks my walks, my manors that I had, Even now forsake me and of all my lands, Is nothing left me but my body's length.
