Thou chidst me well, proud Bullingbrooke, I come To change blows with thee for our day of doom: This ague fit of fear is overblown, An easy task it is to win our own. Say Scroop, where lies our uncle with his power? Speak sweetly man although thy looks be sour. Men judge by the complexion of the sky, The state and inclination of the day; So may you by my dull and heavy eye: My tongue hath but a heavier tale to say, I play the torturer by small and small To lengthen out the worst that must be spoken: Your uncle York is joined with Bullingbrooke, And all your Northern castles yielded up, And all your Southern Gentlemen in arms Upon his party.
