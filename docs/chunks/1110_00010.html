I am your Scrivener, and sir, I hope You'll not forget my pains? Forget thee? no, not whilst thy Parchment lasts: I do remember thee by thy shop, thy sign, Yes, thou hast Labels hanging at thy door; Thou writ'st a good fair hand, and hast in Horn, six several Seals with sundry strange inscripts All joined together; thee? not remember thee? I can call thee by thy name. But sir, my money. Money from me, thy writings are all paid for, It came from Slightall's purse. ay, but my Brokage? Brokage indeed hath some dependence still On Usury, and Usury on that, theyare Relatives; one is not called a Son That hath no Father, and no Father's he That hath no Son; yet money doth go hard. Yet let me have my due. Yes, give the Devil that, For he will have't at length; ha, let me see, Come, let us once be mad, we'll to the next Tavern, And there debate the business. At your own charge? Yes, for this once, not use't. A Gallon sir, betwixt us two? An usurer's Gallon, that's just half a pint, 'Tis none of Slightall's measure, 'tis too great, And come, good Scrivener, write it in Record, That I am now thus liberal. Sir, I shall. You have served me long, what have you got by me? Good Wine, good Victuals, Liveries; And the countenance of a good Master. And pray what's all this? That's as your worship shall be pleased to call it. Nay, name it you. So please you sir, I shall; That which I think y'are brought to now yourself, Or within little of't. And what's that? Nothing. Thy plains I commend, thee and thy wit, That canst give nothing name, such is my state; Yet out of this confused lump of nought, That which no man of sense can say it is, Or Title by the name of any thing, Something I have extracted, and reserved For you, for you my servants; take this Gold. All this sir? Why, all this alas is nothing. What call you something then? To me it is not, for now it is yours, And may it ever after something prove To you and your succession, as a Stock To thrive and prosper by; I only was, But am not now; however be you still, And may this give you Essence. Pray keep mine, Your service sir is all the heritage that I expect from you. Thou never wast one that did seek to husband my estate, Which I have vainly wasted; just, and honest, In all my loose designs didst counsel well, And still persuadest me to providence, That thrift of which I was uncapable, Employ it to thy own ends; had it been more, Greater had been thy stock. I'll keep it sir, as Steward to your use, but Always ready to furnish your least wants. And how for mine?
