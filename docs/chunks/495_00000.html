COme Vallen. lets to Sir God-fries house, I know there will be reveling to night This is his birth day: and he welcomes all, Fair Anabell his daughter is my love, There shalt thou see the Idol of my thought, Fair Bristows miror and my heart's delight. Friend Challener, I wonder at thy humour, To dote so much over this female kind, That charms thy senses makes thy eye sight blind. Thou art an enemy to women still, I prithee what doth best agree with thee. To see my hounds, to chase the fallow dear, to see my fathon strike the partridge dead. to hear my horse Caréere, to drink full healths, and not live puling for an nounce of Beauty, I love to see my hounda as well as thee, My horse, my falcon, and healths when time serves, But above all my mistress I prefer, She is the Fuel that doth heat my blood, And therefore Vallenger, for my sake go and see, the gallants will be here to night, Y faith you bind me to a mighty task I'll see your Lady, and your Lady's mask, then prithee peace here will we keep our stand. For by the Drum the Maskers are at hand. Now gentlemen, your welcome to my house, Good master Challener and your honest friend, So are you all young gallants every one, But we forget ourselves, body of me, Where be these Lads, what shall we have No dancing after dinner? Ho, up with the tables, If they have dined within, and come young Lads now to your dance again. False tongue that spoke such blasphemy before, That I dispraised, now doth my soul adore. How dost thou like my love now Vallenger. O she's divine and I become her thrall. Doth Bristol yield her fellow, prithee speak, A thousand, I must hence or else my heart will break. What means my friend in such a humour go, I'll know the cause before I leave him so, Gentlemen I thank you all, Let's in to supper the the cheer be small, Vallinger thou art a traitor to thy friend. Not to my friend but always to my foe. Why dost thou love the faint I do adore. To anger thee I swear to love her more. I loved her first, when thou didst love disdain. I love her now, therefore thy love is vain. For swear to name her else thou art my foe. For wear my Anabell, hence dotard go, Prepare thee Vallener it is decreed. For Anabell, or thou or I must bleed? On Sir 'tis welcome pair not but thrust home. Some Gracious Body help me I am slain. Whose that which calls for help, gods pity wife, The Gentleman, lies bleeding here that came with master Challen. I pray Sir peak who hath hurt ye thus. The villain Chalener hath almost slain me.
