THe puissant prince and innocent most pure which humbly descended from the seat sempiternal illumine his beams of grace to every creature To withstand the conflict of our enemies mortal The devil, the world, & the flesh, these three in special which setteth division between the soul & the body Inlike wise envy setteth debat between party & party I speak for this cause, daily ye may see How that by envy and malice, many be destroyed Which if they had lived in peace with patient humility riches and prosperity with them had been employed For there as is peace, no man is annoyed For by peace men grow to great richesse And by peace men live in great quietness I am named peace, which envy doth expel Envy with me shall never rest For envy is one of the pains of hell When that he sojourneth in a man's breast Like the burning Fenix in her own nest Though she can none other hurt ne grieve yet she doth not cease herself to mischief A sir here was a long predication Me thought ye said in your communication To every man peace was moste behoved. For sooth and so said I That shallbe proved contrary by and by For by peace much people are undone What people are tho. The armourer, the fletcher, and the bower Mariners, Gonners, and the poor sowdyer Yea and also many another artificer Which I do not rehearse by name I say the universal people doth best obtain where as peace is ever abiding Thou liest so god me help and halydome For then were surgeons clean undone Of them that will fight and break apate They get good living both early and late And what sayest thou by men of law Their living were not worth a straw And every man should live in peace. That is not for the commons increase For by peace they profit in many a thing Peace setteth amity between king & king In time of peace merchants have their course To pass and repass Thou liest knave by the mass For under colour of peace much suttelte hath been wrought And ships are taken the merchants deer have bought Was that for their promotion Nay in time of war Such a knave durst not steer By the mass were it not for shame that should bear me a blow Hold thy hands thou lewd fellow Thou art of evil disposition thus against peace to repugn The which from heaven descended down to bring man out of captivity A horton why dost thou lie When were thou in heaven tell me by and by How camest thou down with a ladder or a rope it were no sin to hang the by the throat Thy words be envious, not grounded on charity. Sir one thing I pray you tell me. What is that Have ye any wife or no Why ask ye so Because ye say peace is most expedient if your wife made you cockolde you being present What would ye do give her such punishment as longeth thereto
