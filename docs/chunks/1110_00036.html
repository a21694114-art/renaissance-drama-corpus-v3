By help of Heaven I shall: this deed thus runs; When all his Creditors are satisfied, His Bonds discharged and canceled, debts full paid, His Mortgages called in, his words redeemed, This Bond is then in virtue; not till then. All this I grant, and call to witness none Save his own Conscience; art thou not at freedom, And clear from all the world? Most true, I am, Nor can I name that party under Heaven To whom I owe one farthing; a brass token Will cancel all my scores. 'Tis a plain case. Yes, but against the Devil. Speak, how that? What were the sums for which he stood engaged? Some fifteen thousand pounds, which I have paid, And now the man owes nothing; therefore duly I Claim both soul and body. Both are quit, As thou thyself shalt to thy grief confess; For I will prove him more indebted now Than e'er he was before. To whom? To thee, Whom till he have discharged the utmost penny, The Bond's of no effect. And am I tripped? The Devil hath bragged he hath caught many a Friar, Now one hath caught the Devil. Am I clear then? Yes, till thou payest him all his money down, Which when thou dost thouart worthy to be damned. Insert but that condition 'mongst the rest, And I'll again subscribe; Gramercy Friar. Nay then I see vainly we Devils stand against the Church, they have still the upper hand, I yield me vanquished; in the mean time friar read And conceal. What, by a quid d it freed? by a quirk in the Law? by This I see the devil's no good Lawyer: give me my deed, That I may see it canceled and undone: I'll tear it small as atoms, that no memory Of the least Letter be reserved as witness Against my soul when I shall resurvive; Nor shall I be at patience till I see This Parchment ashes, and this horrid Writ Dissolved to smoke and air. Yet for my love, For all the good thou hast received by me, For all my cost and charges, large expense, As I acquit thee truly, so thou freely Lend me thine ear. Thy bounty hath deserved it; To save a soul I know no Scrivener here That would not only lend but give an ear. I am informed to th'full; if this be true, Devil, th' art honest, and shalt have thy due: Associate me Friar Iohn. With all my heart, If this be Asteroth I so late invoked, I never shall desire His company, but on an empty stomach. And shall I meet her there? Presume thou shalt. The self same spirit? And enjoy her to: Do thou but hazard as thou didst before And doubt not, she's thine own. Substantially? As a contracted Bride and Bedfellow. No difference in her shape and ornament? None. Thou canst do much with me, nor do I Know beyond what bounds thy credence to extend: thou May'st prove false, but thouhast been yet my friend.
