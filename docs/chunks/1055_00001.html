He acts his whole life on this earthy stage, In Childhood, Youth, Manhood, Decripit age. The very day that doth afford him light, Is Morning the Meridian, Evening, Night. Four seasons still successively appear, Which put together make a complete year. The earth, with all the Kingdoms therein guided, Is into four distinguished parts divided. The four Winds from the World's four quarters blow, Eorus, Favonius, Auster, Aquilo. All Moral virtues we in four include, As Prudence, justice, Temperance Fortitude. Court, City, Camp, and Country, the four CCCs; Which represent to us the four degrees, Required in every fair and flourishing Land, Subtract but one a Kingdom cannot stand. Four Colonels are in this City known, Of which you, honoured Sir, have long been one: And those four Crowns, (for so the high Powers please) Emblem the Kings four Sceptres, and four Seas. The * fift Imperial Arch above, proclaims That glorious Crown, at which his Highness' aims. Thus is our round Globe squared, figuring his power, And yours beneath Him, in the number four. THis Structure honoured Sir doth title bear Of an Imperial Fort, apt for that sphere In which you now move, borrowing all her grace, As well from your own person, as your place; For you have passed through all degrees that tended Unto that height which you have now ascended. You have been in this City ('tis known well) A Soldier, Captain, and a Colonel. And now in times fair progress, to crown all, Of this Metropolis chief General. You, of this Emblem, which this day we bring, To represent the Chamber of the King, Are the prime governor: a Royal Fort, And strongly setted, as not built for sport, But for example and defence: a Tower Supported by no less than Sovereign power: The Theologicke virtues, the three Graces, And Charites have here their several places. Here Piety, true Zeal, study of Peace, (By which small mites to Magozines increase) Have residence: now opposite there are To these, and with them at continual war, Pride, Arrogance, Sloth, Vanity, Prestigion, profaneness, the contempt of true Religion, With thousands more, who assiduatly wait This your Imperial Fort to insidiate. You may observe i'th' music of your Bells Like sound in Triumphs, and for funeral kneels; Marriage and death to them appear all one, Masking nor mourning cannot change their tone: With our Fort 'tis not so, whose fair pretence, is To comply with the nature of offences, Errors: she knows in low terms how to chide Great faults, with greater noise are terrified: But she can load her Cannons, and speak loud To encounter with the arrogant and proud: What's further in your Praetorship assigned, You, in your London's Mirror there may find.
