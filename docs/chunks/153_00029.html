Yea Chremes, there were then many other in Andros, which hard him say the same. Would to God it were no otherwise than I hope for. But tell me this Crito, what said he then as touching the girl? did he say that she was his daughter? No. Whose then? His brother's daughter. Without doubt she is mine. What say you? Nay what sayest thou? Listen to this gear Pamphilus. What think you of this? That same Phania was my brother. I knew the man, and I wot well he was your brother. He flying hence, for fear of the war, takes his way after me into Asia, and than he was afraid to leave her here behind him. Since which time, I never heard what became of him till now. I am well nigh beside myself, my mind is so whelmed, with fear, hope, Ioy, and with wondering at so great, and so sudden good hap. Now in good faith, I am glad that by many proofs she is found to be your daughter. Father I believe it well. But there remains yet one doubt, which sore troubles me. You are well worthy to be hated for your peevish preciseness: you make a doubt where all is as plain as a pack staff. What is that you doubt of? Her name falls not out right. Truly she had another name, when she was a child. What name? Cannot you remember it Crito? I am calling it to mind. Shall I suffer this man's memory to be a hindrance unto my wished joy, whenas I may help myself in this point? no, I will not suffer it. Lo you hear Cremes, the name you seek for, is Passibula. That same is she. Is it even she. she hath told it me her own self a thousand times. Chremes, I ween you believe that we all rejoice at this. Yea so God me help, do I believe it. Father, what remains now to be done? The matter itself hath reconciled me a good while since. O an excellent father. Chremes altereth nothing as touching my wife, but that I may still enjoy her, as I have done. marry and good cause why. Unless your father say otherwise. Only, as touching the dowry. Yea marry, that. Pamphilus her dowry is five hundred pound. I accept it. I will now high me to my daughter, and you Crito, go with me: for I believe she never knew me. And why do you not rather cause her to be brought hither to you? You put us well in mind, I will presently cause Davus to go about that matter. He cannot. How so? marry because he hath a great matter of his own, which toucheth him nearer. What is that? He lieth bound in prison. Father, then is he wrongfully bound. Not so, I commanded it. I beseech you, command that he be let loose.
