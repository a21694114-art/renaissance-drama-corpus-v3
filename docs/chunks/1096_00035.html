Text (EarlyPrint-regularized spelling)

Wonder in apprehension, I must Suspect your health indeed. No no, thou sha'not Be troubled, I but stirred thee with a moral, That's empty contains nothing, I am well, See I can walk poor man, thou hast not strength yet. What accident is ground of this distraction? Thou hast not heard yet what's become o'th' Chancellor? Not yet my Lord. Poor gentleman, when I think Upon the King, I've balm enough to cure A thousand wounds, have I not Allegre? Was ever bounteous mercy read in story, Like his upon my life, condemned for sacrifice By Law, and snatched out of the flame unlooked for, And unpetitioned? but his justice then That would not spare whom his own love made great, But give me up to the most cruel test Of judges, for some boldness in defence Of my own merits, and my honest faith to him Was rare, past example. Sir, the King Is coming hither. It will Become my duty sir to leave you now. Stay by all means Allegre,' 'tshall concernen you, I'm infinitely honoured in his presence. Madam be comforted, I'll be his Physician. Pray heaven you may. No ceremonial knees, Give me thy heart, my dear, my honest Chabot, And yet in vain I challenge that 'tis here Already in my own, and shall be cherished With care of my best life, violence Shall ravish it from my possession, Not those distempers that infirm my blood And spirits shall betray it to a fear, When time and nature join to dispossess My body of a cold and languishing breath, No stroke in all my arteries, but silence In every faculty, yet dissect me then, And in my heart, the world shall read thee living, And by the virtue of thy name write there, That part of me shall never putrefy, When I am lost in all my other dust. You too much honour your poor servant sir, My heart dispares so rich a monument; But when it dies— I wonot hear a sound Of any thing that trenched upon death, He speaks the funeral of my crown that prophecies So unkind a fate, we'll live and die together, And by that duty which hath taught you hitherto, All loyal and just services I charge thee, Preserve thy heart for me and thy reward, Which now shall crown thy merits. I have found A glorious harvest in your savour sir, And by this overflow of royal grace, All my deserts are shadows and sly from me, I have not in the wealth of my desires, Enough to pay you now, yet you encourage me To make one suit.
