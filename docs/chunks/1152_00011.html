My Lady, as I live Mistress, my lady's come, she's at the door, I peeped through, & I saw her, And a stately company of Ladies with her. This was a week too soon, but I must meet with her, And set a new wheel going, and a subtle one, Must blind this mighty Mars, or I am ruined. What are they at door? Such my Michael As you may bless the day they entered here, Such for our good. 'tis well. Nay, 'twill be better If you will let me but dispose the business, And be a stranger to it, and not disturb me, What have I now to do but to advance your fortune. Do, I dare trust thee, I am ashamed I am angry, I find thee a wise young wife. I'll wise your worship Before I leave ye, pray ye walk by and say nothing, Only salute him, and leave the rest to me Sir, I was borne to make ye a man. The rogue speaks heartily, Her good will colours in her cheeks, I am borne to love her. She is a mirror, But she is poor, she were for a PRINCE's side else, This house she has brought him too as to her own, And presuming upon me, and upon my courtesy, Conceive me short, he knows not but she is wealthy, Or if she did know otherwise, 'twere all one, He is so far gone. Forward, she has a rare face. This we must carry with discretion husband, And yield unto her for four days. Yield our house up, our goods and wealth. All this is but in seeming To milk the lover on, do you see this writing, 200 a year when they are married, Has she sealed too for our good; the time's unfit now, I'll show it you tomorrow. All the house. All, all, and we'll remove too, to confirm him. They'll into th' country suddenly again After they are matched, and then she'll open to him. The whole possession wife, look what you do, A part o'th' house.
