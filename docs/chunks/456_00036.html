Song. Beauty arise, show forth thy glorious shining, Thine eyes feed Love, for them he standeth pining, Honour and youth attend to do their duty, To thee (their only sovereign) Beauty. Beauty arise, whilst we thy servants sing, Ioue to Hymen wedlock jocund King. Ioto Hymen lo lo sing. of wedlock, love, and youth is Hymen King. Beauty arise, beauty arise, thy glorious lights display, Whilst we sing so, glad to see this day, Io Io Io Hymen Io Io sing, Of wedlock, love, and youth is Hymen King. Art thou as glad in soul as in thy song? Who can be glad when he endureth wrong? As Cod udge me Ian Niclas is honest man, he does not flatter and sembles, but tell his intentions: owe more melodies, owe here come her new pride. Salute my beauteous love. All joy betide to Gratiana our dear marquis Bride. Bring me a crown of gold to crown my love, A wreath of willow for despised Grissel. Grissel is not despised in your eye, Sithence you name her name so gently. Gwenthyan's there's wives, there's patient wives Fuh fuh is fools, Tawsone is arrant pobie fools. Grissel place you this crown upon her head, Put these embroidered slippers on her feet. 'tis well, deliver me your wedding ring, Circle her finger with it, now stand by, Art thou content with all? Content with all. My Bride is Crowned, now tell me all of you, Which of you ever saw my love before? What is her name, her birth, place, or estate, Till now I never beheld her beauty. Nor I. Trust me nor I. By my troth nor I. We hear that she was borne in Germany, And half heir to the Duke of Brandenburg. You all hear this, and all think this? We do. Then Fu. stand thou forth, Lords in his breast A loyal servants true soul doth rest, Furio shall be appareled in a rob. I shall not become it. Some that are great put robes on Parasites, Mario, Lepido come you two hither, Are not you richly clad? have I done so? What means your grace by this? Graceless, have done, Truth, seldom dwells in a still talking tongue, Furio bring Laureo from the porter's lodge, Take in Ianicola, and cloth them both In rich habiliments, they shall awhile Be flattered with false fortunes wanton smiles. Fortune can do no more than she hath done, They that are marked to woe, to woe must run. How do you like my Bride? I think her blessed. To have the love of such a noble Lord. You flatter me. Indeed I speak the truth, Only I prostrately beseech your grace, That you consider of her tender years, Which as a flower in spring may soon be nipped, With the least frost of cold adversity. Why are not you then nipped? you still seem fresh As if adversities cold icy hand, Had never laid his fingers on your heart.
