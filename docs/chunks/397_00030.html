It seems so fox, O what a world is this, There most sin raynes where least suspicion is, You'll come. Well by your leave sir Richard Faukenbridge, Hence free from fear, you'll melt you'll melt old man, Nay take her to you, she is a shrew I warrant, I'll to the holy Hermit, and inquire, About my chain your sword, the Pursuivant And other matters that I have to ask. Your welcome good sir Richard, Nay do not stand on terms, I am fire, all life, Nor never tell me that I have a wife. I do not mean to marry, ye think so, But to be merry, you the manner knowe. And you will have me, have me, point a meeting, I'll be your true love, you shall be my sweeting, If you deny to promise, this is plain I'll have my will ear you get home again. most gracious Lord. Tut tell not me of grace I like no goodness but a beauteous face. Be therefore brief, give me your hand & swear, Or I'll away with you into the heath, Neither shall Faukenbridge nor Hermit help, And what I do I'll answer well enough. Why, then my Lord. Nay do not stand on then, But tell me when my Lord shall have you Lady, It's presently, i'll venture for a baby. This night at stepney by my summer house, There is a tavern which I sometime use, When we from London come a gossoping, It is the Hind, Give me thy pretty hand. Thou'lt meet me at the Hind, I'll by thy Roe, One word's enough, Suffice then be it so, I'll fit my old adulterer and your grace, I'll send the Princess thither in my place. Prince Iohn, Prince Iohn the Hermit tells me wonders. He says it was Skinke that scaped us at the Tavern, Skink had my chain: nay sure that Skinke did all. I say go but to yonder corner, And ere the Sun be half an hour higher, There will the thief attempt a robbery, Who Skinke? Will Skinke? I Skinke upon my word. Shall we go seize upon him good Prince Iohn? Nay we will have him that's no question. Adeiu old Hermit: soon in th'evening Lass, I'll meet you both, and meet with both of you. Father what answer do you give to me? Lady start down I must into my cell, Where I am curing of a man late hurt, He dressed, I must unto my Orisons, In half an hour all will be dispatched, And then I will attend your Ladyship.
